# Research priorities

Refreshed 24 October 2026. Ranked by composite score among questions that pass all five gates and are not limited to a subspecialty journal. Feasibility is reported for every question but carries 5% of the weight.

## Top ten

### 1. Q-032: Prasugrel versus ticagrelor in acute coronary syndromes: resolving discordant trial results with a pooled analysis that includes the registry-based SWITCH-SWEDEHEART comparison

Composite 84.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D8, D6. Target journal: European Heart Journal or Circulation (a network meta-analysis of P2Y12 inhibitors appears in Circulation, PMID 32468837).

Design: Two-stage meta-analysis of randomised comparisons with reconstructed IPD from Kaplan-Meier curves (random effects, REML, Hartung-Knapp), exploring time-varying hazards, STEMI versus NSTE-ACS and age or weight effect modification, with the registry-based default-policy trial analysed as a sensitivity stratum

Why now: ISAR-REACT 5 (PMID 31475799) favoured prasugrel (HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.70 for ticagrelor), while the registry-based SWITCH-SWEDEHEART comparison (PMID 42670968, 17,095 patients, August 2026) reported an adjusted OR of 0.90 (95% CI 0.77 to 1.06). The only network meta-analysis in the store (PMID 32468837) predates SWITCH.

Feasibility for the owner: Reconstruction is within the owner's toolkit; full IPD needs collaboration.

Next three actions:

1. Register the protocol, naming PMIDs 31475799, 42670968, 29154813 and 27576777 as the eligible trials and prespecifying the update at NCT07507500.
2. Digitise and reconstruct the Kaplan-Meier curves and validate against reported hazard ratios.
3. Approach the SWEDEHEART and ISAR-REACT 5 investigators about patient-level data.

### 2. Q-031: Does omitting aspirin at the time of primary PCI for STEMI increase ischaemic events? An updated individual patient data meta-analysis of P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy trials with STEMI-specific effect estimates

Composite 81.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D8, D6. Target journal: Lancet or European Heart Journal (IPD analyses of P2Y12 monotherapy of this type appear in PMIDs 39226909 and 38750627).

Design: One-stage IPD meta-analysis (reference-case Cox models, stratified by trial, random effects on treatment with REML and Hartung-Knapp adjustment) with a prespecified STEMI-by-treatment interaction and landmark analysis at 1 month

Why now: Three randomised trials published between November 2025 and August 2026 (PMIDs 40888723, 40888726, 42670955) point in different directions in ACS or STEMI, and the prespecified STEMI stratum of 40888723 (PMID 41384891) reports an interaction (P for interaction = 0.030). The last IPD meta-analysis in the store (PMID 39226909, September 2024) predates all three.

Feasibility for the owner: Full IPD needs several trial groups to agree; the fallback is reconstructed IPD.

Next three actions:

1. Register a protocol on PROSPERO with the STEMI interaction and the update trigger for NCT05491200 prespecified.
2. Contact the steering committees of the trials behind PMIDs 40888723, 40888726 and 42670955 about IPD sharing.
3. Build the Kaplan-Meier reconstruction pipeline for the reconstructed-IPD fallback and test it against PMID 39226909.

### 3. Q-026: Does systematic coronary imaging with guideline-directed prevention improve outcomes after type 2 myocardial infarction?

Composite 80.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D7. Target journal: Circulation or European Heart Journal.

Design: Registry-based randomised trial embedded in a national MI or hospital-episode registry

Why now: Observational studies report high MACE after type 2 MI and a high prevalence of previously unrecognised coronary disease, yet no randomised evidence exists.

Feasibility for the owner: Requires a registry consortium and funding; emulation is feasible in the short term.

Next three actions:

1. Extract coronary imaging yield and outcome estimates from PMID 35341327 and PMID 37777947 for sample size inputs
2. Approach a registry partner about embedding randomisation
3. Draft a target trial specification as a fallback emulation

### 4. Q-028: Does routine postoperative troponin surveillance with a protocolised response reduce cardiovascular events after major noncardiac surgery?

Composite 78.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D7. Target journal: Circulation, JAMA Cardiology or European Heart Journal.

Design: Stepped-wedge cluster registry-based trial using hospital episode and audit data

Why now: Cohort data show high mortality associated with perioperative injury (PMID 28444280, PMID 29203498, PMID 36705050), and MANAGE (PMID 29900874) suggested a treatable target, but surveillance has not been tested.

Feasibility for the owner: Large multi-hospital collaboration required.

Next three actions:

1. Define the response protocol from MANAGE (PMID 29900874) and the AHA statement (PMID 34601955)
2. Identify national audit data with postoperative troponin
3. Approach perioperative investigators from PMID 28444280

### 5. Q-006: Transportability of the microaxial flow pump trial result to unselected AMI-CS: target trial emulation by DanGer Shock eligibility, age and sex

Composite 77.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D2. Target journal: Circulation or European Heart Journal.

Design: Target trial emulation in linked BCIS-ICNARC data with Harefield pump registry data, benchmarked first against the DanGer Shock result in eligible patients, then extended to non-eligible strata

Why now: The only randomised trial of the pump (38587239, 355 analysed) showed lower death in the whole population, while prespecified or exploratory analyses suggest less apparent benefit in older patients (39551167, P for interaction = 0.028) and women (41222528, P for interaction = 0.18), and a national registry reports that eligible patients were 35.6% of Impella-treated AMI-CS (41078250). Renal replacement therapy was more frequent with the pump (39462276).

Feasibility for the owner: Owner holds registry access and collaborators.

Next three actions:

1. Draft the emulation protocol with eligibility mapped to DanGer Shock criteria and register it.
2. Confirm variable availability and linkage permissions with BCIS and ICNARC collaborators.
3. Run the benchmarking emulation on the eligible stratum before any extension.

### 6. Q-010: First-line inotrope and vasopressor strategy in SCAI stage C to D cardiogenic shock: registry-embedded multi-arm randomised trial

Composite 77.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D2. Target journal: JAMA Cardiology or Circulation.

Design: Registry-embedded multi-arm adaptive randomised trial in the planned UK shock platform

Why now: The randomised data are small or neutral: epinephrine was stopped early for refractory shock (29976291, 57 patients), and milrinone did not differ from dobutamine (34347952, 192 participants). Trials of inotrope versus placebo and dopamine versus norepinephrine are registered but ask different questions.

Feasibility for the owner: Needs platform development and funding.

Next three actions:

1. Review the registered protocols of DOREMI-2 and ACTION-CS to define non-overlapping arms.
2. Specify the registry fields needed for embedded randomisation.
3. Run a target trial emulation as a feasibility check.

### 7. Q-035: Time-varying effects of left atrial appendage closure versus oral anticoagulation by reconstructed individual patient data from the randomised device trials

Composite 76.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D9, D8. Target journal: European Heart Journal or JACC.

Design: Reconstructed individual patient data meta-analysis from published Kaplan-Meier curves with flexible parametric and landmark models, one-stage random effects, pre-registered on PROSPERO, with a pre-specified update when NCT03642509 and NCT04226547 report

Why now: Recent randomised trials in the appraised subset report non-inferiority for efficacy in anticoagulation-eligible patients (PMID 41910347) and in post-ablation patients (PMID 39555822), while a trial in high-risk patients did not show non-inferiority to medical care (PMID 41849741). Three pooled analyses of randomised trials appeared in October 2026 in non-target journals (PMIDs 42860016, 42862780, 42898218) using aggregate risk ratios, with an ischaemic stroke signal in one (RR 1.41 [1.07-1.85], PMID 42860016) that aggregate data cannot place in time.

Feasibility for the owner: Matches the owner's reconstructed IPD workflow.

Next three actions:

1. Search PubMed and PROSPERO for any existing IPD or reconstructed-IPD analysis of closure trials
2. Extract Kaplan-Meier curves and numbers at risk from the trials in the store
3. Register the protocol with a pre-specified update trigger

### 8. Q-011: Antithrombotic strategy after TAVI by atrial fibrillation status and CT leaflet thrombosis: reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis with a pre-specified update at the next trial readouts

Composite 75.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D3, D8. Target journal: European Heart Journal or JACC.

Design: Reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis, random-effects with REML and Hartung-Knapp adjustment, landmark and restricted mean survival analyses, registered on PROSPERO with an update pre-specified for each listed readout

Why now: Trials in the appraised subset point in different directions (rivaroxaban inferior to antiplatelet therapy in 31733180, edoxaban and apixaban not superior to standard care or vitamin K antagonist in 34449183 and 35583186, bleeding excess with added clopidogrel in 32223116 and 32865376), while several adequately sized trials are registered to report from 2027. The latest meta-analysis in the subset (29516608) predates all of them. Guideline position unverified (guideline extracts not yet retrieved); equipoise judged on trial evidence only.

Feasibility for the owner: Entirely within the owner's methods with public data.

Next three actions:

1. Register the protocol on PROSPERO with the readout-triggered update rule
2. Extract Kaplan-Meier curves and event counts from the nine key trials and build the reconstruction pipeline
3. Check D8 candidates for duplication and agree a single owner of the question

### 9. Q-001: Time-varying effects of revascularisation versus medical therapy on spontaneous MI and cardiac death in stable coronary disease: reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis

Composite 74.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D1. Target journal: Circulation or JACC (European Heart Journal as alternative).

Design: Reconstructed individual patient data network meta-analysis from published Kaplan-Meier curves, with flexible parametric or RMST models and prespecified effect modification by ischaemia, anatomy and LV function

Why now: Two meta-analyses reached different conclusions on cardiac death (PMIDs 32794407, 34002203), and trials since, including extended ISCHEMIA follow-up (36335918), long-term FAME follow-up (41540107), REVIVED (36027563) and the ISCHEMIA CTO analysis (40139890), report non-proportional effects that trial-level relative risks cannot capture.

Feasibility for the owner: Matches the owner's reconstructed-IPD and network meta-analysis skills.

Next three actions:

1. Register on PROSPERO and define MI and cardiac death mapping across trials.
2. Extract Kaplan-Meier curves and numbers at risk from PMIDs 32227755, 36335918, 41540107, 36027563 and the trials in PMIDs 32794407 and 34002203.
3. Run a search outside the appraised subset for target-journal meta-analyses from 2024 to 2026 before committing.

### 10. Q-025: Continuation or withdrawal of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors after myocardial infarction with preserved ejection fraction: a registry-based randomised trial

Composite 74.0. Status: candidate. Domains: D6. Target journal: European Heart Journal, Circulation or JAMA Cardiology.

Design: Registry-based randomised non-inferiority trial embedded in a national MI registry, with outcomes from linked records

Why now: Open-label trials in MI with preserved ejection fraction reported neutral or modestly positive results for beta-blockers (38587241 hazard ratio 0.96; 40888702 hazard ratio 1.04; 40888716 hazard ratio 0.85; IPD 41211954 hazard ratio 0.97) and a spironolactone trial reported 0.91 (39555814). Together they have opened the question of which post-MI drugs remain justified in low-risk patients. The appraised subset contains no RAAS withdrawal trial; the original ACE inhibitor trials predate the store.

Feasibility for the owner: It suits the owner's registry-embedded methods but needs a partner registry and funding.

Next three actions:

1. Search the full store and PubMed for contemporary RAAS inhibitor trials and withdrawal studies after MI to confirm the gap
2. Run a UK Biobank target trial emulation as a feasibility and effect-size check
3. Approach registry-based trial groups about a non-inferiority platform and a margin
