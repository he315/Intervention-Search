# D3 Aortic valve intervention: evidence landscape

Written 24 October 2026.

## Scope and data basis

This landscape is drawn from the appraised subset only: 178 Tier A and B records for D3 (about 1,000 appraised records across all domains, out of roughly 51,000 stored; the appraisal backlog is still being worked through). It also uses 185 ClinicalTrials.gov and ISRCTN records for D3 and the monthly trend counts. Statements about what is settled or contested therefore reflect the subset and may change as the backlog is appraised. Guideline extracts have not yet been retrieved, so no guideline class or level is stated anywhere in this file and equipoise is judged on trial evidence alone. The trials file does not record primary outcomes (the field is empty for every entry), so primary endpoints are listed as not recorded.

## Settled questions

- TAVI versus SAVR in high-risk patients: no difference in mortality at 5 years, 55.3% versus 55.4% (30249462); 3-year death or stroke lower with TAVR (27050187).
- TAVI versus SAVR in intermediate-risk patients to 5 years: no significant difference in death or disabling stroke (27040324, 28304219, 31995682; 5-year 47.9% versus 43.4%, hazard ratio 1.09, 95% CI as given in the abstract).
- TAVI versus SAVR in low-risk patients to mid-term: 1-year composite 8.5% versus 15.1% with balloon-expandable valve (30883058) and 24-month 5.3% versus 6.7% with self-expanding valve (30883053); pooled randomised data in 31537261 and, with reconstructed time-to-event data, 40044297 (all-cause death hazard ratio 0.80; 95% CI 0.66-0.97).
- Routine cerebral embolic protection does not reduce stroke: 2.3% versus 2.9% (36121045, N=3000) and 2.1% versus 2.2% (40162661, N=7635); no cognitive benefit in 40884786.
- Antithrombotic therapy after TAVI without an anticoagulant indication: aspirin alone caused less bleeding than aspirin plus clopidogrel (32865376), and rivaroxaban led to more death or thromboembolism than antiplatelet therapy (31733180).
- Adding clopidogrel to oral anticoagulation after TAVI increased bleeding (32223116).
- Early intervention in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis lowers the primary composite: 31733181 and 41880613 (10-year follow-up), 34779220 and 39217448, 39466903 (N=901), pooled in 39641732 (four trials, 1,427 patients). EVOLVED (39466640) was neutral and fell short of its planned sample.
- Balloon-expandable versus self-expanding valves: no difference in hard outcomes in the randomised comparisons 32049283, 33926657 and 38587261; ACURATE neo failed non-inferiority in 31570258.
- Denosumab and alendronic acid do not slow calcification progression (33913339, N=150).
- Anaesthesia and sedation: non-inferiority of a monitored-anaesthesia strategy shown in 40878766 (N=752); no difference in 33926657.
- TAVI in women: non-inferiority with a lower event rate than surgery in RHEIA (40171878); consistent with sex-specific results of DEDICATE (40900118).

## Contested questions

- Long-term mortality and durability after TAVI versus SAVR. At 10 years, mortality was 86.1% after TAVR versus 82.8% after surgery (42300821, hazard ratio 1.13, 95% CI 1.02-1.25), whereas NOTION showed 65.5% in each arm (38321820). Low-risk trials show no significant difference to 6 and 7 years (41697183, 41144631), and younger low-risk patients in NOTION-2 had numerically higher events with TAVI at 1 and 3 years (38747246, 40884768). Structural deterioration differs by trial (30732707, 33059828).
- PCI in patients undergoing TAVI. NOTION-3 favoured PCI (39216095, hazard ratio 0.71; 95% CI 0.51-0.99) and FFR-guided strategies lowered events (39644913, 41417623), but deferral and TAVI-first timing were non-inferior (41921523, 42670987), and an observational pooled analysis shows lower early but higher late mortality with TAVR plus PCI (42882018). A meta-analysis of randomised trials appeared in October 2026 (42910974).
- Anticoagulant choice and leaflet thrombosis. Edoxaban was non-inferior to vitamin K antagonist but with more major bleeding (34449183); apixaban was not superior to standard care (35583186); edoxaban versus dual antiplatelet therapy did not significantly lower leaflet thrombosis (35373583); rivaroxaban reduced leaflet motion abnormality (31733182) but worsened clinical outcomes (31733180).
- Bicuspid aortic stenosis. Registry analyses show similar mortality (31184741, 34546301, 32098500) with lower device success in earlier work (28330793); randomised data are limited to subsets (38747246).
- Conduction disturbance and pacing. New left bundle branch block is associated with death in pooled observational data (31899484), while pacemaker implantation is not clearly associated with mortality (28698290); conduction-system pacing showed a lower event rate than right ventricular pacing in a matched registry (42863877). Guideline discordance between ESC/EACTS and ACC/AHA is reviewed in 36632841; its content has not been extracted.

## Open questions

- Moderate aortic stenosis: observational excess mortality (31491546, 37877909, 34082909) against one small trial, TAVR UNLOAD, with win ratio 1.31 (95% CI 0.91-1.88) (39480381). Larger trials are registered (NCT04889872).
- Drugs after TAVI beyond dapagliflozin: GLP-1 receptor agonists (42886864), renin-angiotensin inhibitors (30512100, 31370954), and SGLT2 inhibitors by frailty (42885795) rest on observational or post hoc data. Candidate D3-C2.
- Aortic stenosis presenting in cardiogenic shock (37350747). Candidate D3-C4.
- Redo-TAVR and failed transcatheter valves (37660719, 32327098, 33413929, 40044295), with registered trials NCT06400342 and NCT07462624.
- Native aortic regurgitation: single-arm studies only (38552656, 41260228); randomised trials NCT04864145, NCT06608823 and NCT07453407 are registered.
- TAVI without on-site cardiac surgery: similar outcomes in a national registry (27190093); trials NCT05751577, NCT07604402, NCT06818006 and NCT07515768 are registered.
- Pre-TAVI coronary assessment strategies: NCT06898086, NCT06559332 and NCT07107568.
- Frailty and very old patients: nonagenarians had higher mortality (27012397); frailty modifies the dapagliflozin effect in a post hoc analysis (42885795).
- Lifetime strategy in younger patients, including redo options. Candidate D3-C3.

## Evidence trend

The count file holds D3 rows only up to November 2019 (the trend retrieval was incomplete when this file was written), so the 2016 to 2026 trend cannot be summarised. Annual totals for all D3 records were 1,741 in 2016, 1,871 in 2017 and 1,999 in 2018, and 1,912 for January to November 2019. Randomised trial counts were 50, 39 and 25 for 2016 to 2018 and 31 for January to November 2019; meta-analyses were 61, 79 and 88, then 85; guideline items were 4, 14, 15 and 8. Among the appraised records, the publication dates show a steady flow of randomised trials from 2022 onwards, led by the NEJM and the European Heart Journal. The trend section should be regenerated once counts for 2019 to 2026 are in the file.

## Candidate questions

- D3-C1: Antithrombotic strategy after TAVI, reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis with pre-specified updates (gates G1 to G5 pass; guideline position unverified).
- D3-C2: Cardiometabolic and neurohormonal drugs after TAVI, target trial emulation benchmarked to DapaTAVI (gates G1 to G5 pass; G3 margin narrow).
- D3-C3: Lifetime management in younger and low-risk patients, decision model with value-of-information analysis (G3 fails because two trials are registered to complete within 24 months).
- D3-C4: Aortic stenosis presenting in cardiogenic shock (G4 fails; no identified comparator dataset).

Considered and not proposed: conventional meta-analysis of early intervention in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis (repeat of the same four trials, fails G3 and G5); PCI in TAVI candidates (COMPLETE TAVR, NCT04634240, N=4000, is registered to complete in April 2026 and a meta-analysis has just appeared); TAVI without on-site cardiac surgery (TRACS, NCT05751577, reports 2027-03).
