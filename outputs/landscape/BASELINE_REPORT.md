Every retrieval chunk of the evidence corpus is complete (no chunks pending), and the monthly trend corpus, the ClinicalTrials.gov snapshot, the nine domain landscapes and the citation benchmark have been written. Model appraisal of baseline records is still under way: 5,133 records remain in the appraisal backlog, so the landscapes rest on the appraised subset and some relevant Tier A and B records will not yet have been read. The baseline is not yet complete, because the appraisal backlog, guideline extraction and two of the top-ten briefs (Q-018 and Q-024, which have adversarial reports but no full brief) are outstanding.

## Where the evidence stands

### D1 Coronary intervention

Several device and technique questions are settled. Drug-eluting stents reduced the primary outcome against bare-metal stents in an IPD meta-analysis of 20 trials and 26,616 patients without a difference in death (PMID 31056295); radial access was associated with lower mortality and major bleeding in pooled trial data (PMID 36036610); iFR met non-inferiority against FFR (PMIDs 28317438, 28317458); and left main PCI did not differ from CABG for 5-year death in low to intermediate anatomical complexity (HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.91 to 1.32), at the cost of more spontaneous MI and repeat revascularisation (PMID 34793745). In multivessel disease, pooled IPD reported higher 5-year mortality after PCI than CABG (HR 1.20, 95% CI 1.06 to 1.37; PMID 29478841). The field is contested on four fronts: whether revascularisation reduces cardiac death in stable disease, where two meta-analyses disagree (PMIDs 32794407, 34002203) and extended ISCHEMIA follow-up gave an adjusted HR of 1.00 (PMID 36335918); whether intravascular imaging benefit is general, since positive trials in complex lesions sit beside two neutral 2026 IVUS trials (PMIDs 41911016, 41911017) and a pooled analysis reports benefit concentrated in Asian trial populations (PMID 42879937); whether drug-coated balloons can replace stents in de novo disease (PMID 41194754); and whether angiography-derived physiology can replace the pressure wire (PMIDs 34742368, 39488224). Open questions include preventive PCI of vulnerable plaque (PMID 38604213), hard outcomes after CTO PCI, calcium modification, revascularisation in advanced CKD, and choices in women and older patients.

### D2 Cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest and MCS

Randomised trials have settled culprit-only PCI as the initial strategy in multivessel infarct-related shock (PMID 29083953), the absence of benefit from routine balloon pump support (PMID 30586721) and from routine early VA-ECMO in infarct-related shock (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.80 to 1.19; PMID 37634145; four-trial IPD OR 0.93, 95% CI 0.66 to 1.29, PMID 37643628), and the absence of benefit from immediate angiography after arrest without ST elevation (PMIDs 30883057, 41636663). The microaxial flow pump reduced death in STEMI-related shock in a single trial of 360 patients (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.99; PMID 38587239), but its generality is contested: an exploratory age analysis suggests no benefit at 77 years or older (PMID 39551167), renal replacement therapy was more frequent with the pump (PMID 39462276), and in a national registry only 35.6% of pump-treated AMI-CS patients met the trial criteria (PMID 41078250). Left ventricular unloading during VA-ECMO is contested between favourable observational associations and a neutral 116-patient trial (PMID 37850383), as is extracorporeal CPR (PMID 36720132). Open questions are support in heart failure-related shock (PMID 40162941), the first-line vasoactive agent (PMID 29976291), escalation and weaning, stage-directed therapy, and the organisation of shock care, where shock teams were associated with lower CICU mortality (PMID 34556316).

### D3 Aortic valve intervention

TAVI is at least as good as surgery to mid-term across surgical risk strata, with pooled low-risk data reporting an all-cause death HR of 0.80 (95% CI 0.66 to 0.97) for TAVI (PMID 40044297). Routine cerebral embolic protection did not reduce stroke in two large trials (PMIDs 36121045, 40162661). After TAVI without an anticoagulant indication, aspirin alone caused less bleeding than dual antiplatelet therapy (PMID 32865376) and rivaroxaban led to more death or thromboembolism (PMID 31733180). Early intervention in asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis lowered the primary composite in pooled data from four trials and 1,427 patients (PMID 39641732). Long-term mortality and durability are contested: 10-year mortality was higher after TAVR in one trial (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.25; PMID 42300821) but equal in NOTION (PMID 38321820). PCI in TAVI candidates is contested between NOTION-3 (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.99; PMID 39216095) and two 2026 trials that found deferral or TAVI-first timing non-inferior (PMIDs 41921523, 42670987), now pooled in an October 2026 meta-analysis (PMID 42910974). Anticoagulant choice and leaflet thrombosis, bicuspid valves and conduction disturbance remain contested. Open questions are moderate aortic stenosis (PMID 39480381), drug therapy after TAVI beyond dapagliflozin (PMID 40162639), redo-TAVR, native aortic regurgitation and TAVI without on-site surgery (NCT05751577).

### D4 Mitral intervention

TEER added to medical therapy in selected secondary MR reduced heart failure hospitalisation (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.70; PMID 30280640) and 5-year all-cause mortality (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.89; PMID 36876756), and a second trial reduced recurrent hospitalisation or cardiovascular death (rate ratio 0.64, 95% CI 0.48 to 0.85; PMID 39216092). The discordance with the neutral trial (PMID 30145927) is still explained by population differences rather than tested, and no randomised evidence covers patients outside the COAPT criteria, in whom registries report worse outcomes (PMID 38267110). TEER against surgery is unresolved in secondary MR (PMID 39216093) and observational in primary MR (PMID 38243821). Open questions are early intervention in asymptomatic severe degenerative MR, where a single-centre cohort associated early surgery with lower cardiovascular mortality (PMID 40799133) and the randomised trial reports in 2033 (NCT03389542), TEER in atrial functional MR (PMID 40629531), and transcatheter replacement, which has single-arm evidence only (PMID 41167201).

### D5 Tricuspid intervention

Tricuspid TEER (win ratio 1.84; PMID 39471883) and transcatheter replacement (win ratio 2.02, 95% CI 1.56 to 2.62; PMID 39475399) improved hierarchical composites against medical therapy, and a third trial reported a win ratio of 2.42 (95% CI 1.76 to 3.33; PMID 42670975). Replacement carried more severe bleeding (15.4% versus 5.3%) and new pacemakers. Whether benefit extends to death and heart failure hospitalisation is contested: one trial reported annualised hospitalisation of 0.17 versus 0.20 (P=0.40; PMID 39471883), another reported a HR of 0.40 (95% CI 0.29 to 0.55) for death or hospitalisation (PMID 42670975). Surgery for isolated functional TR has not shown a survival difference overall (PMIDs 31071413, 39212387). Open questions are heterogeneity of effect by baseline risk and right ventricular function, transcatheter against surgical repair, combined mitral and tricuspid TEER, and durability and antithrombotic therapy after replacement.

### D6 Acute coronary syndromes

Complete revascularisation reduced cardiovascular death or MI in STEMI with multivessel disease (PMID 31475795; six-trial IPD meta-analysis, 8,836 patients, HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.87, PMID 41223860) and in older patients (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.93; PMID 37634150). Routine beta-blockers after MI with ejection fraction of 50% or more did not reduce events (IPD of five trials, 17,801 patients, HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.87 to 1.07; PMID 41211954), and empagliflozin (PMID 38587237) and spironolactone (PMID 39555814) did not differ from comparators on their primary outcomes. Colchicine after MI reduced events (HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.61 to 0.96; PMID 31733140). The timing of complete revascularisation is contested, with a four-trial meta-analysis reporting higher one-year death with immediate revascularisation (HR 1.50, 95% CI 1.02 to 2.21; PMID 42863871); this overlaps the owner's active network meta-analysis. Invasive management in older patients (PMIDs 39225274, 37968019, 38596853), aspirin omission at PCI (PMIDs 42670955, 40888726), bivalirudin in STEMI and beta-blockers at mildly reduced ejection fraction (PMID 40897190) are also contested. Open questions are incretin-based therapy initiated after MI, continuation of RAAS inhibitors in low-risk patients, frailty as an effect modifier, and SCAD and MINOCA management.

### D7 Type 2 MI and myocardial injury

The evidence is prognostic. Type 2 MI was associated with more MACE than no myocardial injury in an IPD meta-analysis of nine cohorts (subdistribution HR 3.36, 95% CI 2.92 to 3.86; PMID 41553312), and perioperative troponin elevation was associated with 30-day mortality (HR 4.69, 95% CI 3.52 to 6.25; PMID 28444280). Introducing high-sensitivity troponin raised the number of type 2 MI and injury diagnoses without a measured change in treatment or outcome (PMID 31587565). Remote ischaemic preconditioning, ivabradine and colchicine did not reduce myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery (PMIDs 40511609, 40884771, 37640035). Contested are the 12-month safety of the 0/1-hour rule-out (PMIDs 31478763, 33998255), the treatment of established injury after surgery, where one trial of dabigatran lowered the composite outcome (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.93; PMID 29900874), and whether type 2 MI should be investigated like type 1, given a high prevalence of coronary disease on imaging (PMIDs 35341327, 37777947). No randomised trial of any treatment strategy for type 2 MI appears in the store, and routine postoperative troponin surveillance has not been tested.

### D8 Antithrombotic therapy around interventions

Dual therapy with an anticoagulant and a P2Y12 inhibitor caused less bleeding than triple therapy after PCI in atrial fibrillation (PMIDs 28844193, 30883055), with no difference in efficacy across strategies in an IPD network meta-analysis (PMID 42669062). Short DAPT followed by P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy reduced BARC 3 or 5 bleeding (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.34 to 0.54) without a measurable ischaemic penalty (PMID 39226909), abbreviated DAPT was non-inferior in high bleeding risk (PMID 34449185), and chronic P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy reduced events against aspirin in pooled open-label, mainly East Asian trials (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.77 to 0.96; PMID 40902613). Contested are aspirin omission at primary PCI, where one trial reported a HR of 1.34 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.75) for monotherapy (PMID 42670955) and another an interaction by presentation (PMID 41384891); prasugrel against ticagrelor (PMIDs 31475799, 42670968); bivalirudin (PMID 39384262); guided therapy; extended DAPT; and anticoagulation after TAVI (PMIDs 34449183, 35583186, 39216096). Open questions are antithrombotic therapy after TEER (NCT06901466) and LAAO, in SCAD and advanced CKD (NCT04708587), after drug-coated balloon PCI, and the place of factor XI inhibitors (NCT05754957).

### D9 Adjacent interventions

Surgical left atrial appendage occlusion at cardiac surgery in atrial fibrillation lowered stroke or systemic embolism (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.85; PMID 33999547). PFO closure reduced recurrent stroke in pooled data from six trials (PMID 34905030). Renal denervation lowered 24-hour systolic pressure by 4.4 mm Hg against sham across ten trials (PMID 39355923), with no event data. Interatrial shunts did not improve outcomes overall (PMID 39308371). Percutaneous LAAO against anticoagulation is contested: non-inferiority was shown in anticoagulation-eligible patients (PMID 41910347) but not in high-risk patients (PMID 41849741), and an aggregate meta-analysis reported more ischaemic stroke with LAAO (RR 1.41, 95% CI 1.07 to 1.85; PMID 42860016). Open questions are hard outcomes after renal denervation, whether advanced therapy for intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism lowers death or decompensation (PMID 39470698), the consequence of peri-device leak, and hard outcomes for the coronary sinus reducer.

## The most important open questions

Section 8.7 asks for the 20 most important open questions. Only 15 register questions are active (status candidate) and pass all five gates; the other 24 are parked or merged. The list below therefore gives 15 questions ranked by composite score, with ties ordered as in PRIORITIES.md, and does not pad the list. Five high-scoring parked questions follow separately so that the reason for their exclusion is visible. All gate judgements that rely on a guideline position are provisional until guideline extraction is done.

1. Q-026: Systematic coronary imaging with guideline-directed prevention after type 2 MI. Composite 76.0; all gates pass. No randomised trial of any strategy in type 2 MI exists, and the only recent IPD meta-analysis (PMID 41553312) is prognostic.
2. Q-006: Transportability of the microaxial flow pump result to unselected AMI-CS by trial eligibility, age and sex. Composite 73.0; all gates pass. One trial (PMID 38587239) underpins practice, the only competing trial (NCT07762560) completes in 2030, and most treated patients fall outside its criteria (PMID 41078250).
3. Q-010: First-line inotrope and vasopressor strategy in SCAI stage C to D shock, as a registry-embedded randomised trial. Composite 73.0; all gates pass. Existing trials are small or stopped early (PMIDs 29976291, 34347952), and registered trials test different comparisons.
4. Q-028: Routine postoperative troponin surveillance with a protocolised response after major noncardiac surgery. Composite 72.0; all gates pass. Injury is common and associated with mortality (PMID 28444280), but no adequately powered surveillance trial is registered.
5. Q-001: Time-varying effects of revascularisation against medical therapy on spontaneous MI and cardiac death in stable coronary disease, by reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis. Composite 70.0; all gates pass. Pooled analyses disagree (PMIDs 32794407, 34002203), and later trials report non-proportional effects (PMIDs 36335918, 41540107).
6. Q-016: TEER against medical therapy in secondary MR outside the COAPT criteria, by target trial emulation. Composite 69.0; all gates pass. Ineligible patients do worse in registries (PMID 38267110) and no trial addresses them before 2030.
7. Q-011: Antithrombotic strategy after TAVI by atrial fibrillation status and leaflet thrombosis, by reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis. Composite 68.0; all gates pass. Trials conflict (PMIDs 31733180, 34449183, 35583186), and the latest meta-analysis in the store (PMID 29516608) predates about 5,548 randomised patients (derived: 1,644 + 665 + 313 + 1,426 + 1,500 from the register record).
8. Q-024: Incretin-based therapy initiated after MI in people with obesity or diabetes, by target trial emulation. Composite 68.0; all gates pass. No outcome trial of initiation after MI exists; whether the prior-MI subgroup of NCT05556512 would answer it is unconfirmed.
9. Q-025: Continuation or withdrawal of RAAS inhibitors after MI with preserved ejection fraction, as a registry-based randomised trial. Composite 67.0; all gates pass. Neutral beta-blocker trials in this population (PMID 41211954) raise the same question for RAAS inhibitors, and no withdrawal trial is registered.
10. Q-018: Value of information for early intervention in asymptomatic severe degenerative MR. Composite 65.0; all gates pass. Observational data favour early surgery (PMID 40799133) and the main randomised trial (NCT03389542) reports in 2033.
11. Q-004: PCI against CABG at 75 years or older with multivessel or left main disease, by target trial emulation. Composite 64.0; all gates pass. Observational and randomised-subgroup results disagree (PMIDs 27009629, 34082905).
12. Q-012: Cardiometabolic and neurohormonal drugs after TAVI, by target trial emulation benchmarked against the dapagliflozin trial (PMID 40162639). Composite 64.0; all gates pass, with a narrow G3 margin (NCT05672836 completes in December 2028). Other drug classes rest on observational signals.
13. Q-023: Frailty and follow-up time as modifiers of invasive strategy in older patients with NSTE-ACS or MI, by IPD meta-analysis. Composite 64.0; all gates pass. Trials conflict (PMIDs 39225274, 37968019), and the latest IPD meta-analysis (PMID 38596853) pooled 1,479 participants.
14. Q-003: Operator and hospital volume thresholds for complex PCI, by natural-experiment analysis. Composite 62.0; all gates pass. Large British and US registries disagree (PMIDs 29579219, 30586696), and no trial is feasible.
15. Q-020: Heterogeneity of treatment effect of transcatheter tricuspid intervention by baseline risk. Composite 61.0; all gates pass. Composite win ratios are large but component effects are weaker (PMIDs 39471883, 36876753), and NCT04097145 reports in December 2027.

Parked questions with the highest composite scores, which remain clinically open but cannot enter the top ten:

- Q-013, lifetime management of aortic stenosis in younger patients (composite 76.0): fails G3, because two registered trials (NCT06861361, NCT03112980) complete within 24 months.
- Q-032, prasugrel against ticagrelor (76.0): fails G3 and G5, because a target-journal meta-analysis (PMID 42201709) and the registry-based trial (PMID 42670968) cover the evidence base.
- Q-005, preventive PCI of vulnerable plaque (73.0): fails G3, because INTERCLIMA (NCT05027984), NCT06855537 and a third trial report within 24 months.
- Q-031, aspirin omission at primary PCI for STEMI (73.0): fails G3 and G5, because two trials (PMIDs 42670955, 41384891) answer the question for the average STEMI patient and a further pooling would repeat them.
- Q-029, rapid rule-out pathways and 12-month ischaemic events (72.0): fails G3, because DanRUSH (NCT07727135, 28,000 patients) is due in September 2028.

Three themes run through the active list. Seven of the 15 questions concern populations excluded from the defining trials or treated outside their criteria (Q-006, Q-016, Q-004, Q-012, Q-023, Q-024 and, for type 2 MI, Q-026). Four are strategy questions that industry is unlikely to fund and that suit registry-embedded randomisation (Q-010, Q-025, Q-026, Q-028). The synthesis questions (Q-001, Q-011, Q-020, Q-023) all depend on effect modification or time-varying effects that single trials could not test.

The structural heart and shock domains grew fastest. Annual output in tricuspid intervention rose from 184 records in 2016 to 717 in 2025, in shock and mechanical support from 925 to 2,407, and in adjacent interventions from 560 to 1,230, whereas the two largest domains, coronary intervention and acute coronary syndromes, grew by 14% and 17%, and antithrombotic therapy by 5%. Growth continued into 2026 in every domain, most in D9 (34%), D7 (33%) and D8 (24%) on the January to October comparison.

Randomised evidence has not kept pace. The number of indexed RCTs fell between 2016 and 2025 in coronary intervention (408 to 270), acute coronary syndromes (456 to 252) and antithrombotic therapy (177 to 85), and the RCT share fell in eight of nine domains. Meta-analyses and systematic reviews, by contrast, rose in number in every domain, and their share rose in every domain except D2, where the count went from 28 to 70 but the share held at about 3%. In D3 there were 153 meta-analyses and 53 RCTs in 2025. Observational output (registry, cohort or observational study) rose in every domain, from 194 to 610 in D2 and from 60 to 193 in D4. The pattern is one of fewer, larger trials surrounded by a growing volume of secondary synthesis and registry analysis, which is the setting in which a repeat meta-analysis is least likely to add and in which emulation and registry-embedded trials have the most room.

The citation benchmark (outputs/landscape/citation_benchmark.md), built from 1,129 in-scope research articles published from 2019 to 2023 in the seven target and general journals, gives a context for these designs: IPD meta-analyses had a median of 125 citations against 80 for RCTs and 62 for registry or cohort studies, and 15% of conventional meta-analyses and 12% of registry or cohort studies reached their journal's top citation decile, against 9% of RCTs. Counts came from Europe PMC because OpenAlex was unavailable without a key.
