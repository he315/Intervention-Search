# D6 Acute coronary syndromes: evidence landscape

## Scope and data basis

This landscape covers STEMI, NSTEMI and unstable angina, complete revascularisation and its timing, MINOCA, SCAD, and post-MI pharmacotherapy. It is drawn from the appraised subset only: 519 Tier A and B records for D6 in state/synth/D6_records.jsonl (of the much larger stored corpus, with appraisal of the backlog still under way), and 1,461 ClinicalTrials.gov or ISRCTN entries in the D6 trials file, which includes many entries unrelated to ACS and was filtered by title for this document. Guideline extracts have not yet been retrieved, so no guideline class or level is stated and equipoise is judged on trial evidence alone. Every PMID and NCT identifier below appears in the input files. Of the 519 records, 42 are Tier A (32 RCTs, 6 IPD meta-analyses, 3 guidelines and 1 consensus statement) and the remainder Tier B.

## Settled questions

- Complete revascularisation in STEMI with multivessel disease reduces cardiovascular death or MI against culprit-only PCI: 31475795 (COMPLETE) and the IPD meta-analysis of six trials, 8836 patients, hazard ratio 0.76 (95% CI 0.67-0.87) in 41223860. The timing and physiology-guidance sub-questions remain contested (below).
- Complete revascularisation in older patients with MI: 37634150 reported a hazard ratio of 0.73 (95% CI 0.57-0.93) in 1445 patients with a median age of 80 years.
- Routine beta-blockers after MI with LVEF of 50% or more: 38587241 (hazard ratio 0.96, 95% CI 0.79-1.16) and 40888702 (hazard ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.89-1.22), with an IPD meta-analysis of five trials, 17,801 patients, hazard ratio 0.97 (95% CI 0.87-1.07) in 41211954. One further trial (40888716) reported 0.85 (95% CI 0.75-0.98).
- Beta-blocker interruption in stable post-MI patients with preserved ejection fraction: the pooled analysis of 6238 patients (42669300) gave a hazard ratio of 1.09 (95% CI 0.97-1.24), with non-inferiority P of 0.016; the single trial 39213187 did not show non-inferiority (P = 0.44).
- Empagliflozin after MI did not clearly reduce heart failure hospitalisation or death (38587237, hazard ratio 0.90).
- Routine spironolactone in AMI (39555814, 7062 patients) and sacubitril-valsartan against ramipril (34758252, hazard ratio 0.90, 95% CI 0.78-1.04) did not differ from comparators on their primary outcomes.
- Prasugrel against ticagrelor policy (42670968, 17,095 patients): adjusted odds ratio 0.90 (95% CI 0.77-1.06) for the primary endpoint.
- Lipid lowering and anti-inflammatory therapy after ACS or MI: alirocumab (30403574, hazard ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.78-0.93) and low-dose colchicine (31733140, hazard ratio 0.77, 95% CI 0.61-0.96), with a fixed-effect meta-analysis of nine trials, 30,659 patients, relative risk 0.88 (95% CI 0.81-0.95) in 40314334.
- Thrombus aspiration in STEMI: IPD of three trials, 18,306 analysed, cardiovascular death hazard ratio 0.84 (95% CI 0.70-1.01) in 27941066.
- Bivalirudin in NSTEMI: serious bleeding lower, mortality not different (37746717 IPD meta-analysis); bivalirudin in STEMI remains mixed (see contested).

## Contested questions

- Immediate against staged complete revascularisation in STEMI with multivessel disease: 37634190 (risk ratio 0.52, 95% CI 0.38-0.72) against 40902612 (hazard ratio 1.24, 95% CI 0.86-1.79; non-inferiority P 0.24) and 36889333 (hazard ratio 0.78, 95% CI 0.55-1.11). A meta-analysis of four trials, 2651 patients, reported higher one-year death with immediate (42863871, hazard ratio 1.50, 95% CI 1.02-2.21), and a heart failure subgroup of 40902612 gave 1.79 (95% CI 1.05-3.04) in 41493330. This overlaps the owner's active reconstructed-IPD NMA and is not proposed as a candidate.
- Physiology-guided complete revascularisation: 42670979 (AIR-STEMI, hazard ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.47-0.83) against 41159879 (iFR against MRI timing, hazard ratio 0.95, 95% CI 0.65-1.40); comparisons differ, so they are not directly conflicting.
- Invasive strategy in older patients with NSTE-ACS or MI: 39225274 (hazard ratio 0.94, 95% CI 0.77-1.14, 1518 patients, 32% frail) against long-term follow-up of 37968019 (incidence rate ratio 0.76, 95% CI 0.63-0.93) and the IPD meta-analysis 38596853 (hazard ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.63-1.22).
- Omitting aspirin from the time of PCI in AMI: 42670955 (PREMIUM, hazard ratio 1.34, 95% CI 1.02-1.75; P = 0.40 for non-inferiority) against 40888726 (difference -0.09 percentage points, 95% CI -1.39 to 1.20; non-inferiority P = 0.02, low-risk AMI). A 2026 meta-analysis of eleven trials, 37,443 patients (42247196), already covers aspirin discontinuation at 1 and 3 months, and several trials report within 24 months (below), so no candidate is proposed.
- Bivalirudin against heparin in STEMI: 36351459 favoured bivalirudin (hazard ratio 0.69) while the pooled IPD (39384262) found lower mortality (adjusted odds ratio 0.78, 95% CI 0.62-0.99) with more reinfarction and stent thrombosis.
- Beta-blockers with mildly reduced ejection fraction: IPD of four trials, 1885 patients, hazard ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.58-0.97) in 40897190, against neutral results at higher ejection fractions.
- MINOCA stratified treatment: 41150941 favoured stratified treatment on a patient-reported endpoint (101 randomised) but MACE did not differ (P = .18).

## Open questions

- Incretin-based therapy after MI: no outcome trial of initiation after MI in the appraised subset (candidate D6-C2).
- RAAS inhibitor continuation after MI with preserved ejection fraction (candidate D6-C3).
- Frailty as an effect modifier of invasive strategy in older patients (candidate D6-C1).
- SCAD antiplatelet and beta-blocker therapy: NCT04850417 and NCT07683923 are the only randomised entries, both small or late; the observational evidence includes 34338759 (DISCO registry) and 40049585. Likely subspecialty-level.
- MINOCA: StratMedMINOCA (NCT05198791, 400, primary completion 2026-07) is the principal randomised entry besides 41150941.
- Type 2 MI: transfusion effect differed by MI type in a subgroup of a randomised trial (39206549, hypothesis-generating); the IPD analysis 41553312 links type 2 MI with MACE (subdistribution hazard ratio 3.36, 95% CI 2.92-3.86). Management trials are absent; this belongs mainly to D7.
- Early discharge and systems of care after STEMI: policy statement 34641735 and race-based differences in process metrics (37459415); trials of fast discharge are ongoing (below).

## Candidate questions

- D6-C1: older and frail patients, invasive strategy, patient-level synthesis with frailty interaction (gates G1 to G5 pass).
- D6-C2: incretin-based therapy after MI, target trial emulation (gates G1 to G5 pass; G3 needs a check of the prior-MI enrolment in NCT05556512).
- D6-C3: RAAS inhibitor continuation against withdrawal after MI with preserved ejection fraction, registry-based randomised trial (gates G1 to G5 pass; G2 rests on the absence of contemporary evidence in a subset that omits pre-2016 trials).

Questions considered and not proposed: aspirin omission from index PCI (fails G3 and G5 on the existing meta-analysis and the number of trials due), immediate against staged complete revascularisation (overlaps the owner's active NMA), beta-blockers with mildly reduced ejection fraction (IPD exists), colchicine and early PCSK9 inhibition (several adequately powered trials report within 24 months), complete revascularisation in NSTEMI (NCT05786131 reports within the window).
