## Tier A

### Long-Term Survival After Initial Invasive or Conservative Management of Stable Coronary Disease in ISCHEMIA-EXTEND.

Hochman et al., JAMA, 7 November 2026; PMID 42998244; DOI 10.1001/jama.2026.20375

Design and size: Long-term follow-up of the randomised ISCHEMIA trial; 5179 participants, 1071 deaths, randomised at 320 sites in 37 countries.

Primary result as reported: All-cause 11-year mortality was not significantly different between groups (25.8% invasive vs 26.9% conservative; estimated difference, -1.0 percentage point [95% CI, -4.2 to 2.1 percentage points]; adjusted hazard ratio [AHR], 0.97 [95% CI, 0.86-1.09]). Cardiovascular mortality at 11 years was 15.8% in the invasive group vs 17.1% in the conservative group (AHR, 0.88 [95% CI, 0.75-1.02]).

Why it matters: It settles the question of long-term survival for routine invasive management of stable high-risk chronic coronary disease and bears directly on guideline recommendations for revascularisation in chronic coronary syndromes.

Main limitation: Excludes left main disease, unacceptable angina and recent acute coronary syndrome, and the invasive strategy was not limited to symptomatic benefit; follow-up beyond the active trial phase relied on vital status ascertainment.

### Cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor in acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI): a randomised, double-blind trial.

Motovska et al., Lancet (London, England), 7 November 2026; PMID 42998670; DOI 10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00976-1

Design and size: Double-blind randomised trial at 29 hospitals in five European countries; 605 patients randomly assigned to cangrelor (n=298) or ticagrelor (n=307).

Primary result as reported: The primary laboratory endpoint was achieved in 56 (100%) of 56 patients receiving cangrelor and 15 (22%) of 68 receiving ticagrelor (p<0·0001). The 30-day primary clinical endpoint occurred in 112 (38%) of 298 patients receiving cangrelor and 126 (41%) of 307 receiving ticagrelor; non-inferiority was not demonstrated (difference -4 percentage points [90% CI -10 to 3]; Pnon-inferiority=0·13). Major bleeding at 30 days occurred in 19 (6%) of 298 patients receiving cangrelor and 16 (5%) of 307 receiving ticagrelor.

Why it matters: It provides the first randomised evidence on antiplatelet strategy in cardiogenic shock after MI, a setting where oral absorption is impaired, and defines the effect size for future trials.

Main limitation: The laboratory endpoint was analysed in only 56 (19%) and 68 (22%) patients, and the non-inferiority margin of 1 percentage point was narrow relative to the observed event rates.
