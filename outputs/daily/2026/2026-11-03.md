## Tier A

### Antithrombotic Therapy After Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion: The SIMPLAAFY Randomized Clinical Trial.

Reddy et al., JAMA, 2 November 2026; PMID 42970643; DOI 10.1001/jama.2026.21361

Design and size: Open-label, 3-group, multicenter clinical trial randomized 1857 patients at 87 US investigational sites: DAPT for 6 months followed by aspirin (n = 624), a reduced-dose NOAC for 3 months followed by aspirin (n = 620), or aspirin alone for 12 months (n = 613).

Primary result as reported: The primary outcome occurred in 41 patients (6.9%) with DAPT vs 31 (5.1%) with a reduced-dose NOAC (risk ratio, 0.75 [95% CI, 0.48-1.18]; risk difference, -1.7 percentage points; 97.5% upper bound, 1.2) and 20 (3.3%) with aspirin alone (risk ratio, 0.49 [95% CI, 0.29-0.82]; risk difference, -3.5 percentage points; 97.5% upper bound, -0.8). Both monotherapy strategies met noninferiority criteria (P < .001 for noninferiority for both).

Why it matters: Addresses a regimen question with no settled answer after left atrial appendage closure and supports simpler, less bleeding-prone antithrombotic therapy, with direct relevance to guideline wording and to evidence synthesis on post-implant regimens.

Main limitation: Open-label, with a primary outcome limited to 6 months; bleeding and device-related thrombus rates were exploratory without multiplicity adjustment, and all sites were in the US.

### TAVR in Patients with Moderate Aortic Stenosis with At-Risk Features.

Généreux et al., The New England journal of medicine, 2 November 2026; PMID 42970708; DOI 10.1056/nejmoa2607326

Design and size: International, open-label, randomized trial; 744 patients underwent randomization: 373 patients were assigned to the TAVR group and 371 to the clinical-surveillance group.

Primary result as reported: At 2 years, the Kaplan-Meier estimate of the percentage of patients with a primary effectiveness end-point event was 16.7% in the TAVR group and 23.9% in the clinical-surveillance group (risk difference, -7.2 percentage points; 95% confidence interval, -13.1 to -1.3; P = 0.02 for superiority).

Why it matters: First randomised evidence for intervention before severe stenosis, in a population for which guidelines currently recommend surveillance, and so a direct challenge to current recommendations and timing of aortic valve intervention.

Main limitation: Open-label; the composite includes heart-failure events with or without hospitalisation, which are open to ascertainment bias, and all-cause death did not differ (8.4% vs 7.3%); industry funded, with a 2-year horizon and no data on valve durability.
