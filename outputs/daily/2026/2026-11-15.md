## Tier A

### Angiography-Derived vs Wire-Based Fractional Flow Reserve to Guide Revascularization: The Randomized LIPSIA-STRATEGY Trial.

Majunke et al., Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 1 November 2026; PMID 43033171; DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.10.064

Design and size: Multicentre randomised noninferiority trial at 8 German centres; 1,053 patients with acute or chronic coronary syndromes (vFFR, n = 528; FFR, n = 525).

Primary result as reported: Major adverse cardiac events at 1 year occurred in 15 of 493 patients (3.0%) with vFFR and 20 of 493 (4.1%) with FFR (risk difference: -1.01 percentage points; P for noninferiority <0.001; upper boundary of the 1-sided 97.5% CI 1.29 percentage points; relative risk: 0.75; 95% CI: 0.39-1.45).

Why it matters: Adds a randomised comparison to a field where earlier trials of angiography-derived physiology against wire-based FFR conflicted, and bears directly on whether guidelines can endorse wire-free physiology.

Main limitation: Event rates were lower than anticipated, so the absolute margin was lenient on a relative scale, and noninferiority was not met in a post hoc analysis using the relative margin implied by the original assumptions; vFFR also led to more revascularisation.
