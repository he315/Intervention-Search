## Tier A papers and what they change

- Antithrombotic Therapy After Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion: The SIMPLAAFY Randomized Clinical Trial.. Reddy et al., JAMA, 2 November 2026; PMID 42970643; DOI 10.1001/jama.2026.21361. Addresses a regimen question with no settled answer after left atrial appendage closure and supports simpler, less bleeding-prone antithrombotic therapy, with direct relevance to guideline wording and to evidence synthesis on post-implant regimens.
- TAVR in Patients with Moderate Aortic Stenosis with At-Risk Features.. Généreux et al., The New England journal of medicine, 2 November 2026; PMID 42970708; DOI 10.1056/nejmoa2607326. First randomised evidence for intervention before severe stenosis, in a population for which guidelines currently recommend surveillance, and so a direct challenge to current recommendations and timing of aortic valve intervention.
- Long-term outcomes after percutaneous coronary intervention with drug-eluting stents versus coronary artery bypass grafting for left main coronary artery disease: a prospective analysis of the multicentre IRIS-MAIN registry.. Wee et al., Lancet (London, England), 3 November 2026; PMID 42976899; DOI 10.1016/s0140-6736(26)02104-5. Large long-term emulated-trial comparison for left main disease in a general-tier journal; informs the PCI versus CABG debate but covers all ages and Asian registries, not the older-patient population of Q-004.
- Long-Term Survival After Initial Invasive or Conservative Management of Stable Coronary Disease in ISCHEMIA-EXTEND.. Hochman et al., JAMA, 7 November 2026; PMID 42998244; DOI 10.1001/jama.2026.20375. It settles the question of long-term survival for routine invasive management of stable high-risk chronic coronary disease and bears directly on guideline recommendations for revascularisation in chronic coronary syndromes.
- Cangrelor versus crushed ticagrelor in acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock (DAPT-SHOCK-AMI): a randomised, double-blind trial.. Motovska et al., Lancet (London, England), 7 November 2026; PMID 42998670; DOI 10.1016/s0140-6736(26)00976-1. It provides the first randomised evidence on antiplatelet strategy in cardiogenic shock after MI, a setting where oral absorption is impaired, and defines the effect size for future trials.
