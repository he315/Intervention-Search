# Adversarial review: Q-028

Question reviewed: routine postoperative hs-troponin surveillance with a protocolised cardiology response, compared with troponin on clinical suspicion only, in adults aged 45 years or older after major noncardiac surgery; outcomes MACE and all-cause death at 1 year; proposed design a stepped-wedge cluster registry-based trial.

Searches were run on 24 October 2026 in the local store (title and abstract scan of data/records, and data/trials/current.jsonl.gz), PubMed E-utilities and ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Every identifier below was retrieved in this session. I did not retrieve the wording of the 2022 ESC or 2024 AHA/ACC perioperative guideline recommendations, so guideline positions are not stated here beyond what the retrieved abstracts say.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review, meta-analysis (IPD or network), trial emulation, biobank analysis or randomised trial that compares routine postoperative troponin surveillance with a protocolised response against testing on suspicion was found in a target or general journal in 2024 to 2026. PubMed searches combining troponin, surveillance, screening or monitoring with noncardiac surgery returned observational, prognostic and implementation papers only, and a search for stepped-wedge designs with troponin in surgery returned nothing. The closest items are listed below.

- PMID 38213306, Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica, April 2024. Retrospective cohort of 558 patients having acute high-risk abdominal surgery, comparing a troponin screening period with a historical control period. The abstract reports 30-day mortality of 13.8% (95% CI 8.7% to 18.9%) in controls and 11.1% (95% CI 8.0% to 14.3%) with screening, absolute risk difference -2.7% (95% CI -8.7% to 3.3%). Of 81 patients with myocardial injury, no intervention or change in management was recorded in 67 (82.7%). This is the same question in a small, single-centre, before-and-after form; it is inconclusive and does not answer the question. It is registered as NCT05992961 (observational, 558, completed).
- PMID 37973786, Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia, March 2024. Single-centre retrospective study of 36,386 patients before and after the 2017 Canadian Cardiovascular Society guideline. Troponin surveillance was done in 13% and then 14% of patients (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.15). Adjusted ORs were 0.83 (95% CI 0.54 to 1.27) for MI and 0.88 (95% CI 0.64 to 1.19) for mortality, with in-hospital outcomes only. Low uptake limits its inferential value, but it shows that guideline publication alone produced little change in testing.
- PMID 34031808, Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia, August 2021. Interrupted time series of implementation of the Canadian guideline (title only retrieved).
- PMID 38233301, British Journal of Anaesthesia, April 2024. Modelling study in 2350 patients (METS and TEAMS cohorts) comparing which patients American, Canadian and European guidance would select for surveillance. The Canadian and European approaches selected 78% and 79% of patients and had sensitivity 0.85 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.92) for postoperative MI, with low specificity. This bears on the eligibility and cost of the intervention, not on its effect.
- PMID 42022610, Health Science Reports, March 2026. Swiss single-hospital retrospective study of 6413 inpatient surgeries; 1089 patients (17% of all surgeries) would have needed testing under the 2022 ESC guidance. It documents the resource burden.
- PMID 41882681, Perioperative Medicine, March 2026. Editorial by Devereaux and colleagues stating that routine monitoring is needed to avoid missing most MINS events, and that management strategies and outcomes require further research. This is an opinion piece but it signals that the leading group regards the effect of surveillance as unproven.
- an out-of-store publication, European Heart Journal, September 2023. Editorial, "Routine post-operative troponin surveillance after non-cardiac surgery: are we ready?" (no abstract). Its existence in a target journal shows editorial interest and also that the question was framed as unresolved in 2023.
- PMID 41326931, Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia, December 2025. Historical cohort of guideline adherence at one quaternary centre; no outcome comparison relevant to the question.
- PMID 37319136, PLoS One, 2023. Meta-analysis of the incidence and risk factors of postoperative myocardial injury; no overlap with the intervention.
- PMID 41043850, BMJ Open, October 2025. Protocol for a systematic review and meta-analysis of risk prediction models for postoperative cardiac injury; prognostic, no overlap.
- Cost-effectiveness: the Eur J Anaesthesiol items PMID 37909155 and PMID 36722187 are an editorial and a related record on a cost-effectiveness analysis of hs-troponin T systematic screening against usual care; PMID 37530718 is an erratum. The abstracts retrieved contain no results, so overlap with a decision-model angle cannot be judged from them.

Evidence on the response arm, which is what makes surveillance potentially beneficial or not:

- PMID 29900874, Lancet, June 2018 (MANAGE). Randomised trial of dabigatran 110 mg twice daily against placebo in 1754 patients within 35 days of MINS. Primary efficacy composite occurred in 97 of 877 (11%) against 133 of 877 (15%), HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.93); primary safety composite HR 0.92 (95% CI 0.55 to 1.53). This is the only randomised support for a treatment in MINS, and it is the basis for a protocolised response. It is one trial with 46% and 43% permanent discontinuation, and no confirmatory trial was found.
- PMID 40884771, Circulation, October 2025. Ivabradine against placebo in 2101 patients; MINS in 17.0% against 15.1%, RR 1.12 (95% CI 0.92 to 1.37); stopped for futility. NCT05279651.
- PMID 40511609, Circulation, October 2025 (PRINCE). Remote ischaemic preconditioning in 1213 patients; myocardial injury 38.0% against 37.4%, RR 1.02 (95% CI 0.88 to 1.18).
- PMID 37935833, European Heart Journal, April 2024 (SPACE). Continuing against stopping RAS inhibitors in 262 patients; myocardial injury OR 0.77 (95% CI 0.45 to 1.31).

These three show that recent target-journal trials have focused on preventing MINS, not on whether detecting it improves outcomes.

## Ongoing trials

No registered randomised or cluster-randomised trial of routine postoperative troponin surveillance with a protocolised response against suspicion-only testing was found in ClinicalTrials.gov using several term combinations (surveillance, screening, monitoring, stepped wedge, cluster, MINS), nor in the local trial snapshot. Relevant registered studies, all of which I read at registry-field level:

- NCT02573532, University Hospital Basel, observational, 19,645 estimated, primary completion 2027-12. Screens major noncardiac surgery patients for perioperative myocardial injury and describes 1-year outcomes. It will inform incidence and prognosis, not the effect of surveillance.
- NCT05812248, observational cohort, 20,000 estimated, primary completion 2027-11, recruiting (title only: prediction of vascular events after major noncardiac surgery).
- NCT06324461, GLP-1 receptor agonist for reduction of MINS, 372, primary completion 2028-12; a possible treatment arm, not a surveillance trial.
- NCT05279651 (PREVENT-MINS, ivabradine), results published as PMID 40884771.
- NCT03408522 (UMC Utrecht, observational, 1000, disability after perioperative myocardial injury), NCT03436238 (Linkoeping, observational, 1269), NCT07850492 (TROP-MAS, observational, 650, primary completion 2027-05-31): prognostic cohorts.
- NCT04187664 (EXCARE, extended care pathway in high-risk surgical patients, 1720, status unknown, primary completion 2021-07-31): seen in the local store by title and enrolment only and not read in detail; it may be relevant as a pathway intervention.

G3 therefore holds on the registry evidence retrieved: no adequately powered randomised comparison of the same strategy is expected within 24 months. The caveat is that the registry search is keyword-based, and a surveillance trial registered under pathway or enhanced-recovery terms could be missed.

## Editor objections

1. The effect of surveillance depends entirely on an effective response, and the evidence for a response is one trial (MANAGE, PMID 29900874) with high discontinuation, plus negative prevention trials. An editor may judge the trial premature: if the response is weak, surveillance cannot improve outcomes, and a null result would be uninterpretable.
2. The "protocolised cardiology response" is undefined. It could include aspirin, statin, dabigatran, echocardiography, angiography or referral, and the contrast with usual care is a bundle. PMID 38213306 found that 82.7% of patients with injury had no intervention or change, so adherence to the protocol would determine the effect size.
3. Detection of injury is the mechanism, so the contrast between arms depends on how often suspicion-only testing already finds injury. The dilution from background testing (13% to 14% surveillance rates in PMID 37973786, and increasing uptake described in PMID 41882681) is not stated.
4. Statistical power and the effect size are not stated. With 1-year MACE and death as outcomes and a plausible modest effect, the required cluster sample is very large; the design needs a calculation with an intracluster correlation before a journal would take it seriously.
5. Regulators and guideline groups already recommend surveillance in some form (the editorial PMID 41882681 and the ESC and Canadian approaches described in PMID 38233301), so some editors will see a trial as ethically awkward in the intervention direction, or as a health-services question for a perioperative or anaesthesia journal rather than a cardiology parent journal. The topic sits in D7 and is not interventional in the strict sense, so fit with Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, the European Heart Journal or JACC would rest on a strong trial design and a hard outcome result.
6. Cost and workload (PMID 42022610, PMID 38233301) are the practical barrier, and a trial that ignores harms of surveillance (overinvestigation, anticoagulant bleeding, contrast exposure) will be criticised.

## Design objections

1. Registry-based ascertainment of troponin testing is unreliable. Hospital episode and audit data rarely hold postoperative troponin values with timing, and the exposure (protocol delivered) and the intermediate outcome (injury detected) would need laboratory data linkage. MINS is also largely asymptomatic, so event capture for the control arm depends on tests that the control arm does not order.
2. Stepped-wedge designs carry secular-trend and contamination risks here. Surveillance guidance is spreading nationally (PMID 41882681, PMID 42022610), so calendar-time trends in testing and in perioperative care could confound a before-and-after step comparison, and clinicians in control clusters could start testing, diluting the contrast. Concurrent changes in anaesthetic practice, such as haemodynamic management, are a further threat.
3. Outcome ascertainment for 1-year MACE through administrative data is feasible in countries with linked national registers (death, MI, stroke), but the definition of MACE would need validation; all-cause death is robust.
4. Cluster randomisation of the response requires consent decisions, ethics for the intervention step, and a fixed definition of eligible surgery (major, age 45 or over), which is more complex than the pre-post comparison in PMID 38213306. A parallel-cluster or individual-patient design within a registry-based platform, or a pre-specified target trial emulation using staggered guideline adoption across hospitals as a natural experiment, may be more credible and cheaper as a first step, with a trial reserved for after the response is better defined.
5. A weaker design would not suffice for a guideline committee, which supports G4, but only if the exposure definition and event capture can be made robust. As proposed the design is plausible rather than demonstrably credible.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The question is unanswered: no synthesis or randomised trial of surveillance with a response was found, the only direct empirical attempts are small single-centre before-and-after studies (PMID 38213306, PMID 37973786) that are inconclusive, and the leading research group describes management and outcomes as requiring further research (PMID 41882681). G3 appears to hold on the retrieved registry evidence, with the keyword-search caveat above. The weakness is the response arm and the design: the evidence for a treatment response rests on MANAGE (PMID 29900874), and a stepped-wedge registry trial would struggle with exposure and event capture and with temporal confounding as surveillance becomes standard. The narrower angle is a staged programme: first a decision model with value-of-information analysis and a target trial emulation using staggered adoption of surveillance across hospitals or regions with linked laboratory data, to size the effect and define the response bundle, then a pragmatic randomised trial of a defined response (for example anticoagulant, statin and cardiology review) within injury-positive patients.

Suggested changes: G3 stays pass, with a note that the keyword registry search covers trial registrations only and that the observational cohorts (NCT02573532, NCT05812248) will report in 2027 but do not answer the comparison. G5 should be recorded as a conditional pass: not a repeat meta-analysis, but weak if reduced to another before-and-after single-region analysis, as PMID 38213306 already shows. Reduce Equipoise from its current level by about one point, to reflect that guideline groups already recommend some surveillance and that the real equipoise sits in the response and in the effect of detection, not in testing as such. Keep Novelty unchanged or reduce by at most half a point, since the question has been framed in target-journal editorials (an out-of-store publication) and tested in small cohorts, although no definitive evidence exists. Design credibility should fall by one point for the stepped-wedge registry-based form as proposed, and the proposed primary design should be changed as above.
