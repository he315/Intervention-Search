# Adversarial review: Q-032 (prasugrel versus ticagrelor in ACS)

All identifiers below were retrieved from the local store in this session, with title, journal and date copied from the stored record. Web search and live PubMed were not used to add any further identifiers. The store holds about 51,000 records and most are unappraised, so the absence of an item below is not proof that none exists.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 42201709, JAMA Cardiology, 1 July 2026 (entrez 27 May 2026): "Efficacy and Safety of Prasugrel, Ticagrelor, or Clopidogrel After Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis". Fifteen randomised trials, 48,904 patients, searched to 15 November 2025, mixed treatment comparison. Reported prasugrel versus ticagrelor MACE OR 0.83 (95% CI 0.70-0.98), myocardial infarction OR 0.78 (0.65-0.94) and stent thrombosis OR 0.66 (0.49-0.88). Overlap is high for the aggregate-data question in a target-tier journal within 24 months (this alone engages G3). Its search predates the SWITCH-SWEDEHEART report, so it excludes the registry-based default-policy trial, and it is study-level only, with no time-varying or effect-modification analysis described in the abstract.
- PMID 42670968, New England Journal of Medicine, 29 August 2026 (NCT05183178): "Prasugrel versus Ticagrelor in Acute Coronary Syndromes" (SWITCH-SWEDEHEART). Registry-based stepped-wedge cluster-randomised trial, 17,095 patients, primary composite at 1 year 11.8% versus 11.1% (adjusted OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.77 to 1.06), major bleeding 4.4% versus 4.2% (adjusted OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.64 to 0.99). It is itself an adequately powered randomised answer to the same comparison, published, and it is a policy-level estimate in an unselected population. It is larger than ISAR-REACT 5 and PRAGUE-18 combined.
- PMID 31475799, NEJM, 17 October 2019 (ISAR-REACT 5): 4018 patients, primary composite 9.3% ticagrelor versus 6.9% prasugrel (HR 1.36, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.70); major bleeding HR 1.12 (0.83 to 1.51).
- PMID 27576777, Circulation, 22 November 2016 (PRAGUE-18): about 1230 patients, terminated for futility; 7-day primary 4.0% versus 4.1% (OR 0.98, 95% CI 0.55-1.73).
- PMID 41671005, JAMA Cardiology, 1 April 2026 (TUXEDO-2): 1800 patients with diabetes and multivessel disease, ticagrelor not noninferior to prasugrel (risk difference 2.33 percentage points, 95% CI -2.07 to 6.74); registered with CTRI, not an NCT.
- PMID 42892998, American Heart Journal Plus, 1 November 2026: three trials, 7048 patients, MACE HR 0.83 (0.67-1.03). Non-target journal, aggregate data, pre-SWITCH.
- PMID 42332387, Cardiology in Review, 23 June 2026: three RCTs plus one post hoc study, 10,480 patients; mortality RR 0.95 (0.75-1.20). Non-target journal.
- PMID 42832923, JACC: Advances, 5 October 2026: network meta-analysis of ticagrelor monotherapy versus aspirin plus prasugrel (27,225 patients); adjacent, not the same comparison.
- PMID 39621344, JAMA Network Open, 2 December 2024: German claims trial emulation of ISAR-REACT 5, 17,642 matched patients, primary HR 1.24 (1.12-1.37) favouring prasugrel.
- PMID 41489712, EuroIntervention, 3 November 2025: Danish nationwide cohort, 10,984 patients, prasugrel MACE adjusted HR 0.67 (0.47-0.95).
- PMID 42341834, Thrombosis and Haemostasis, 8 July 2026: editorial-type argument ("Prasugrel vs. Ticagrelor: Can TUXEDO-2 Settle the Debate?") that evidence favours prasugrel.
- Older aggregate syntheses exist in non-target journals (for example PMID 37751668, American Journal of Cardiology, 15 November 2023; PMID 32816142, Cardiovascular Drugs and Therapy, 1 June 2021). No IPD or reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis of prasugrel versus ticagrelor was found in the store.

Beyond 24 months, so it does not trigger G3, but it is a future update point.

## Editor objections

- The question's premise has weakened: with SWITCH-SWEDEHEART showing no significant difference in the primary composite and a JAMA Cardiology meta-analysis already pooling 15 randomised trials, an editor will ask what a further pooled analysis adds beyond a reconciliation of ISAR-REACT 5 (prasugrel favoured), PRAGUE-18 and TUXEDO-2 (numerically favouring prasugrel) and SWITCH (null for the primary).
- Only about four randomised head-to-head sources with long-term outcomes exist, so the pooled sample is dominated by ISAR-REACT 5 and SWITCH. Effect-modification findings from so few trials would be hypothesis-generating and ecologically confounded.
- SWITCH is a policy-level, intention-to-treat cluster trial with an odds ratio and crossover between agents; it estimates a different estimand from the patient-level assignment in ISAR-REACT 5. Pooling them risks a mixed estimand, and an editor would regard this as the main novelty and also the main weakness.
- The central trial question is likely to be seen as answered by the NEJM report and the target-journal meta-analysis; a subspecialty-journal fate is plausible.

## Design objections

- Reconstructed IPD from Kaplan-Meier curves adds little where the trial primary reports are already available and where SWITCH reports an odds ratio from a mixed model with no standard Kaplan-Meier structure; time-varying hazards could not be reconstructed credibly for SWITCH.
- Reconstruction cannot support credible effect modification (STEMI versus NSTE-ACS, age, weight) because subgroup curves are rarely published; true IPD from ISAR-REACT 5, PRAGUE-18, TUXEDO-2 and SWITCH-SWEDEHEART would be needed, which needs collaboration with each trial group.
- Hartung-Knapp with three or four studies has very wide intervals and little power; the sensitivity stratum design is acceptable but heterogeneity cannot be estimated reliably.
- A credible narrower design exists: a prospective IPD collaboration, with SWITCH registry data and ISAR-REACT 5, predefined for time-varying effects, STEMI versus NSTE-ACS and age or weight, plus a Bayesian reanalysis that expresses the probability of a clinically meaningful difference. This would meet G4 where reconstructed IPD from summary curves may not.

## Verdict

partly scooped

The aggregate-data question of whether prasugrel reduces MACE relative to ticagrelor has been addressed by PMID 42201709 (JAMA Cardiology, 15 trials) and the randomised question by PMID 42670968 (NEJM, 17,095 patients), so the title's framing of discordant results awaiting a pooled analysis is largely out of date. The remaining angle is narrower: a true-IPD or well-reconstructed pooled analysis of the head-to-head trials that includes SWITCH-SWEDEHEART, aimed at heterogeneity of treatment effect (STEMI versus NSTE-ACS, age, weight, diabetes) and time-varying hazards, which the aggregate meta-analysis does not address and which a guideline committee could use to individualise the choice; it needs participation by the trial groups. Suggested changes: G3 should be recorded as failing or borderline (a target-tier meta-analysis in the past 24 months covers the same evidence base, and trials since add SWITCH with 17,095 patients to a pool of about 5,200 patients from ISAR-REACT 5 and PRAGUE-18, plus TUXEDO-2 at 1800, so the pooled sample would increase materially only for the heterogeneity-of-effect angle); G5 should be recorded as at risk (a repeat meta-analysis of the same trials) unless the design is restricted to true IPD with effect-modification as the prespecified aim; lower the novelty score by about two points; lower the equipoise score by one point because the primary SWITCH result is null and ISAR-REACT 5 had favoured prasugrel, leaving residual rather than strong conflict. If the owner cannot obtain IPD, the question should be parked and revisited when STREAMLINE (NCT07507500, primary completion 2029-07) approaches reporting.
