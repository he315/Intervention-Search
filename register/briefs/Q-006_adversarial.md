# Adversarial review of Q-006

Reviewer access: local store, PubMed E-utilities and ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, all queried on 24 October 2026. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session; titles, journals and dates are copied from the source records. Searches for UK-specific work (BCIS, ICNARC, Harefield) returned no study of microaxial flow pump effectiveness in a UK registry, and this absence is a search result, not proof.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

The randomised evidence has been pooled several times, and the pump-versus-no-pump contrast is already stratified by DanGer Shock-like eligibility in the pooled data.

- PMID 39236726, Lancet, 14 September 2024. Individual patient data meta-analysis of randomised trials with 6-month follow-up (nine reports, n=1114). Early unselected MCS did not reduce 6-month mortality (HR 0.87, 95% CI 0.74-1.03); left ventricular unloading devices versus control gave 0.80 (0.62-1.02). The abstract states that patients with ST-elevation cardiogenic shock without risk of hypoxic brain injury had a reduction in mortality with MCS (HR 0.77, text truncated in the stored abstract). This covers the eligible stratum in randomised data and the device-type question. Overlap: high for the eligible stratum, none for the non-eligible strata.
- PMID 40737493, European Heart Journal Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes, 4 November 2025. Network meta-analysis of 18 RCTs (n=1907). Impella reduced 6-12 month mortality versus standard therapy (RR 0.81, P < 0.05) with more renal replacement therapy, limb complications and major bleeding. Overlap: bleeding and RRT outcomes in the randomised setting.
- PMID 39217624, European Heart Journal Acute Cardiovascular Care, 25 September 2024. IPD meta-analysis of four VA-ECMO trials restricted to DanGer-like patients (n=202); no mortality benefit (HR 0.84, 0.56-1.26). Relevant to the ECMO comparator, not to the pump.
- PMID 39551167, JACC, 18 February 2025. DanGer Shock secondary analysis by age (n=355). Benefit in patients under 77 years (OR 0.45, 0.28-0.73) but not at 77 or over (OR 1.52, 0.57-4.08; P for interaction 0.028). This already addresses the age angle within the eligible stratum, as a randomised subgroup.
- PMID 41222528, JACC, 17 February 2026. Prespecified sex-specific DanGer Shock secondary analysis (74 women, 281 men), with an accompanying registry of excluded patients (n=495). This pre-empts the sex angle in the eligible stratum.
- PMID 41078250, European Heart Journal, 24 January 2026. J-PVAD registry, 3975 AMI-CS patients treated with Impella, stratified into eligible STEMI-CS, non-eligible STEMI-CS, OHCA, mechanical complications and NSTEMI-CS, with 30-day mortality by stratum. This is the stratification proposed in Q-006, published in a target journal, but it has no non-Impella comparator and so describes outcomes without estimating effect.
- PMID 39387763, JACC, 17 December 2024. "Using Selection Criteria From the DanGer Shock Trial in a Contemporary Cohort With Cardiogenic Shock." The store holds no abstract; only the title was read, so content is not characterised here.
- Other eligibility-overlap cohorts, all descriptive and mostly single-centre or without a comparator: PMID 41946200 (JACC Advances, May 2026, CSWG registry; 19.3% met DanGer Shock criteria), PMID 41077835 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, December 2025; 16.2% of registry), PMID 40108949 (ESC Heart Failure, August 2025), PMID 42663366 (JACC Asia, 28 August 2026; pragmatic eligibility, Japanese administrative data, Impella/MCS-treated patients only), PMID 39890500 (Cardiovascular Revascularization Medicine, November 2025; comparison of DanGer Shock with RCTs and registries).
- Observational comparative effectiveness: PMID 37342056, JAMA Cardiology, 1 August 2023. Medicare claims, 23,478 patients, with IPTW, instrumental variable, difference-in-differences and grace-period analyses; 30-day mortality was higher with the pump (risk difference 14.9%, 12.9%-17.0%), with the authors flagging probable residual confounding. This is the nearest precedent for the proposed design and shows how weakly confounding-prone pump-versus-no-pump registry comparisons have been received.
- Non-AMI shock: PMID 41966345 (Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation, 1 August 2026; 976 pump-treated patients, AMI-CS and non-AMI-CS with similar 180-day mortality after matching, no non-pump comparator) and PMID 42403523 (Annals of Intensive Care, 2026; age quartiles among 1043 pump-supported patients, Fine-Gray model).
- Emulated-trial precedent in shock: PMID 42661221 (Critical Care, 27 August 2026; LV unloading during VA-ECMO), PMID 40340843 (Critical Care, 8 May 2025; ELSO registry, ECPR), PMID 39505059 (Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation, February 2025; commentary on emulated trials in cardiogenic shock). None addresses pump versus no pump in unselected AMI-CS, but they show the method is already in use in the field, at specialty-journal level.

No target- or general-journal analysis found that emulates a trial of pump versus no pump across DanGer-eligible and non-eligible strata with a standard-care comparator. That specific gap appears open, narrowly.

## Ongoing trials

Searched ClinicalTrials.gov for cardiogenic shock with Impella, microaxial flow pump and mechanical circulatory support terms.

- NCT07762560 (DOUBLE-SHOCK), not yet recruiting, n=780, primary completion 2030-10. Microaxial pump versus medical therapy, factorial with pulmonary artery catheter monitoring; STEMI or NSTEMI shock; primary outcome all-cause death; excludes age over 80 and unwitnessed or prolonged OHCA. It will test the pump in NSTEMI-CS, one of the non-eligible strata, but not in older patients, OHCA or mixed shock. Expected after 24 months, so it does not trigger G3 on its own, but it would answer the NSTEMI stratum within the 2030 horizon.
- NCT05366452 (ULYSS), recruiting, n=204, primary completion 2026-06 (date now passed; status still recruiting on the registry). Early Impella CP versus conventional therapy in ACS-related cardiogenic shock, age 18 to 80, primary outcome death from all causes. It is underpowered for stratum-level effects but includes ACS shock beyond strict DanGer criteria and may report within 24 months. Its publication (PMID 37657594 is the design paper) would add to pooled evidence.
- NCT06886113 (CorVad AMICS), not yet recruiting, n=269, primary completion 2026-08-31 (date passed), STEMI shock with DanGer-like criteria; a different device, so relevant to the eligible stratum only.
- NCT07817472 (PRIME-SHOCK), not yet recruiting, n=126, 2029-08-31. Timing of the pump (routine pre-PCI versus selective post-PCI); not a pump-versus-no-pump comparison.
- NCT05064202 (UNLOAD HF-CS), not yet recruiting, n=456, 2027-10-01. Heart-failure shock, outside the AMI-CS population.
- NCT05506449 (RECOVER IV), terminated after 4 participants, primary completion 2024-05-10. The planned US pump-versus-standard-care trial in STEMI shock will not report.
- NCT01633502 (DanShock), completed; published as PMID 38587239 (New England Journal of Medicine, 18 April 2024).

## Editor objections

1. The headline result is a pump-versus-no-pump contrast in observational data, in a field where randomised data now exist for the eligible stratum. An editor at the target journals will ask what the emulation adds beyond a randomised benchmark, and the Medicare analysis (PMID 37342056) showed that registry estimates of the pump can point the opposite way from the trial. If the benchmarking step fails (emulation does not reproduce the DanGer Shock effect in eligible patients), the extension to non-eligible strata is uninterpretable; if it succeeds, the result still rests on unmeasured severity.
2. Eligibility stratification of real-world cohorts is already published in EHJ (PMID 41078250), JACC (PMID 39387763), JACC Advances, Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions and elsewhere. Novelty is limited to adding a non-pump comparator.
3. Age and sex are already covered as randomised secondary analyses in JACC (PMID 39551167 and PMID 41222528), so the age and sex strata no longer read as unexplored.
4. UK pump use is low and concentrated in a few centres, so a UK-only emulation is likely to be underpowered for stratum-level hazard ratios; editors will want an international or multi-registry design for a parent-journal paper. I could not verify the number of UK pump-treated AMI-CS patients from retrieved sources.
5. DOUBLE-SHOCK (NCT07762560) and ULYSS (NCT05366452) will supply randomised data for some non-eligible strata, which weakens the argument that only observational data can inform them.

## Design objections

- Time zero and exposure definition. Pump insertion in AMI-CS is typically decided around the angiography, so clone-censor-weight with a grace period is appropriate, but the eligibility moment (shock diagnosis with revascularisation decision) is rarely recorded in BCIS (a PCI registry) or ICNARC (an ICU audit) in a way that aligns with the trial's lactate, blood pressure and left ventricular function criteria. Lactate and LVEF thresholds were the most restrictive DanGer criteria in the CSWG registry analysis (PMID 41946200), so these must be captured to define eligibility at all.
- Data linkage. A linked BCIS-ICNARC dataset with Harefield pump registry data is a feasibility assumption that nothing retrieved confirms. Harefield data would supply only exposed patients, so it cannot supply the unexposed comparator on its own and risks selection if used alongside national registry controls.
- Confounding by indication. Severity at pump decision (SCAI stage, vasopressor dose, pH, neurological status after arrest) is the main unmeasured confounder, and the Medicare analysis flagged exactly this. Negative-control outcomes and a randomised-trial benchmark help but do not remove it.
- Power for strata. With the eligible-trial hazard ratio near 0.74 for 180-day mortality and strata such as women, older patients and OHCA being small in UK data, interaction tests are unlikely to be informative.
- Bleeding and RRT outcomes are better captured prospectively; in registries they are poorly ascertained and subject to detection differences.
- The design could credibly answer a narrower question: whether the registry-based emulation reproduces the randomised result (a transportability calibration exercise), and descriptive-plus-adjusted outcomes in non-eligible strata presented as hypothesis-generating, not as practice-changing estimates.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The pump-versus-standard-care effect in the eligible stratum is established by the DanShock trial (PMID 38587239) and the Lancet IPD meta-analysis (PMID 39236726), and the age and sex questions within that stratum are already published as randomised secondary analyses (PMID 39551167, PMID 41222528). Eligibility-stratified registry descriptions exist in a target journal (PMID 41078250). What remains unanswered is an effect estimate against a non-pump comparator in the non-eligible strata (OHCA, older patients, mixed and non-STEMI shock) with the benchmarking step as a validity check, but DOUBLE-SHOCK (NCT07762560) will randomise NSTEMI shock by 2030 and ULYSS (NCT05366452) may report earlier, and UK data may be too small. The question should be narrowed to strata not covered by those trials (older patients over 80, comatose or unwitnessed OHCA, mixed shock) and the design should add international registries or a UK linkage whose pump-treated numbers are confirmed before further investment. Suggested changes: G3 stays a pass but should record DOUBLE-SHOCK (780, 2030-10) and ULYSS (204, 2026-06 on registry) as partial competitors; G5 should be marked at risk, since the benchmarking-only version would be incremental to PMID 41078250 and PMID 39387763; reduce novelty by about 1 point (to 3 if currently 4) given the stratified registry literature; reduce equipoise by 0 to 1 point for the eligible, NSTEMI and age and sex strata, keeping it for the over-80, OHCA and mixed-shock strata.
