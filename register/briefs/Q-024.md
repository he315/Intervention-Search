# Q-024: Early versus deferred initiation of incretin-based therapy after myocardial infarction in people with obesity or type 2 diabetes, in the window the outcome trials excluded: a target trial emulation of timing

This brief is built on the narrowed question. The register title asked whether incretin-based therapy started within 12 months of myocardial infarction (MI) improves outcomes against no initiation. The independent check (register/briefs/Q-024_adversarial.md, 24 October 2026) found that this broad question already has concordant observational answers in lower-tier journals, randomised evidence in people with prior MI, and further trials reporting in 2027, so a plain exposed-against-unexposed emulation would be incremental. The surviving angle is timing: whether starting early after discharge, inside the window the trials excluded, does better than deferring, in populations the trials excluded or under-represented. The brief therefore uses one primary composite, demotes major bleeding to safety, and drops the SGLT2 inhibitor comparator, which has already been reported (PMID 42219874). Guideline positions have not been extracted for this register, so no class or level is stated.

## 1. Question in PICO form

- Population: adults discharged alive after type 1 MI with type 2 diabetes, or with body mass index of 27 kg/m2 or above without diabetes, no incretin use in the preceding 90 days and no contraindication. The two strata are analysed separately.
- Intervention: initiation of a GLP-1 receptor agonist or dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist within 30 days of discharge.
- Comparator: deferred initiation, between day 91 and month 12 (primary); no initiation within 12 months (secondary); in the diabetes stratum, early DPP-4 inhibitor initiation as an active comparator.
- Outcomes: primary, cardiovascular death, recurrent MI, ischaemic stroke or heart failure hospitalisation; safety outcomes including major bleeding; negative control outcomes.
- Timing: time zero at discharge; primary horizon 12 months, extended to 3 years.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision is whether an incretin agent belongs in the discharge bundle after MI or should wait until the patient is stable and reviewed months later. Cardiologists make the first decision, diabetes physicians and general practitioners the deferred one, and commissioners decide access.

The window is a documented gap. Registry eligibility fields read from ClinicalTrials.gov on 12 November 2026 show that SELECT (NCT03574597), SOUL (NCT03914326), REDEFINE 3 (NCT05669755) and SURPASS-CVOT (NCT04255433) excluded MI within 60 days of screening, SURMOUNT-MMO (NCT05556512) and the retatrutide outcome trial (NCT06383390) excluded MI or acute coronary syndrome within 90 days, and ATTAIN-Outcomes (NCT07241390) excludes a major heart condition within 60 days. Prior MI is common in these trials (in SURPASS-CVOT, 65.0% had coronary disease, of whom 47.3% reported prior MI; PMID 37758044), so the trials speak to chronic post-MI patients but not to the first two to three months, when recurrent events and heart failure cluster.

Population size. In the Western Denmark Heart Registry, 7398 patients with first-time MI and body mass index of 27 or above without diabetes met SELECT-type criteria (PMID 41580697). Taiwan's national data held 36,103 people with diabetes and acute MI (PMID 41392024).

Practice variation. In a Croatian registry of 870 people with diabetes discharged after acute coronary syndrome, 35 received a GLP-1 receptor agonist alone and 74 with an SGLT2 inhibitor (109 of 870, 12.5%, derived; PMID 41979990). In a US network, 7201 patients with AMI and PCI started a GLP-1 receptor agonist within 14 days (PMID 42219874). Danish authors describe implementation after MI as "challenging due to costs and limited supply" (PMID 41580697). Discharge prescribing happens in some systems and not others, with no evidence on whether timing matters.

## 3. What is known

Randomised evidence:

- PMID 37952131 (NEJM, SELECT): 17,604 patients with cardiovascular disease and obesity without diabetes; primary composite 569 of 8803 (6.5%) on semaglutide against 701 of 8801 (8.0%) on placebo, hazard ratio 0.80 (95% CI 0.72 to 0.90). Recent MI excluded.
- PMID 42591121 (Frontiers in Endocrinology, network meta-analysis of randomised trials in diabetes with prior MI): GLP-1 receptor agonists reduced MACE against placebo, hazard ratio 0.83 (95% CI 0.77 to 0.89); certainty for cardiovascular death was graded low, citing "predominantly remote MI populations".
- PMID 40704485: pooled SUSTAIN 6 (NCT01720446) and PIONEER 6 (NCT02692716); the semaglutide effect on MACE was consistent by prior cardiovascular disease (P for interaction above 0.05).
- PMID 42099143: five small randomised trials of GLP-1 receptor agonists in acute MI (300 and 333 patients per arm) showed no significant effect on clinical endpoints.
- PMID 38587237 (NEJM, EMPACT-MI, NCT04509674): empagliflozin within 14 days of MI admission did not reduce heart failure hospitalisation or death (hazard ratio 0.90, 95% CI 0.76 to 1.06) but reduced first heart failure hospitalisation (0.77, 0.60 to 0.98); PMID 38581389 reported total heart failure events. Early post-MI initiation of a cardiometabolic drug is therefore testable, and early heart failure is where an effect appeared.

Observational evidence after MI (associations only, none in a target- or general-tier journal):

- PMID 41297711: diabetes, semaglutide or tirzepatide within 14 days of AMI, 5338 per matched group; recurrent coronary events hazard ratio 0.913 (95% CI 0.750 to 1.111), all-cause mortality 0.484 (0.413 to 0.567).
- PMID 42219874: GLP-1 receptor agonist against SGLT2 inhibitor within 14 days of AMI with PCI, 1752 per group; MACE 0.788 (0.706 to 0.881).
- PMID 41862113: tirzepatide within 14 days of AMI or stroke without diabetes, 833 per group; MACE 0.91 (0.814 to 1.021).
- Smaller cohorts: PMIDs 42210124, 42958020, 41979990 and 41703411 (the last reporting persistence of semaglutide started at discharge in 60 patients).
- PMID 42387250 (September 2026): seven mainly observational studies, 37,393 patients with diabetes; all-cause mortality hazard ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.49 to 0.90), I2 87.3%, with significance lost for every endpoint under Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman adjustment. This is the register's latest review.
- Two TCT 2026 abstracts address the same exposure (DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.09.060 and DOI 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.09.061); the store holds titles only.

Emulations in broader atherosclerotic populations:

- PMID 42556854 (BMJ): tirzepatide against sitagliptin as placebo proxy, 52,971 people with diabetes and atherosclerotic disease, with negative control outcomes; MACE hazard ratio 0.68 (0.58 to 0.80), all-cause mortality 0.55 (0.42 to 0.72).
- PMID 42103200: obesity and atherosclerotic disease without diabetes; mortality 0.68 (0.53 to 0.88).
- PMID 43010688 (10 November 2026, added to the store today): semaglutide against tirzepatide in obesity and atherosclerotic disease without diabetes; revised 5-point MACE 1.21 (0.99 to 1.51), with results sensitive to "decisions surrounding early events, off-label use, analytic approach and outcome definition". Not post-MI and not about timing, but direct evidence that early-event and time-zero choices drive the answer.
- Preprint, not peer reviewed: doi 10.64898/2026.11.05.26367396 (medRxiv), 119,911 matched pairs; mortality hazard ratio 0.479 (0.463 to 0.496).
- PMID 41392024: emulation of SGLT2 inhibitor against DPP-4 inhibitor started within 14 days of MI in diabetes; composite 0.89 (0.82 to 0.96). This is the template for the active-comparator arm.

Timing precedent: PMID 40240093 (JACC, SWEDEHEART) used clone-censor-weight methods to compare ezetimibe added within 12 weeks of discharge, from 13 weeks to 16 months, or not at all, in 35,826 patients; 3-year hazard ratio for late against early 1.14 (95% CI 0.95 to 1.41).

No randomised trial and no target- or general-tier analysis of early against deferred incretin initiation after MI was found.

- NCT05669755 (REDEFINE 3): cagrilintide with semaglutide, 7101, primary completion 2027-09-01, 3-point MACE.

- Completed trials whose prior-MI subgroups may appear: NCT03574597 (SELECT), NCT03914326 (SOUL, 9651), NCT04255433 (SURPASS-CVOT, 13,299).
- Post-MI incretin trials are small and mechanistic: NCT07295223 (GALACTUS, 44, completed 2026-10-30), NCT07297589 (EPIC-CT, 136), NCT06557811 (SemaFatCard, 88, status unknown).

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: population excluded from the defining trials, combined with a timing strategy that industry will not fund (the register's original label was "no evidence").

- G1 Consequence: pass. Recurrent MI, heart failure and death are common in the months after MI, and the decision is made at every discharge.
- G2 Equipoise: pass, weaker in the diabetes stratum. Long-term benefit is supported by trials; whether early initiation adds benefit, given early intolerance and polypharmacy, and whether benefit extends to older, CKD and non-diabetic patients early after MI, is not known.
- G3 Not about to be answered: pass. No trial of early initiation is registered; every large trial excluded MI within 60 or 90 days; prior-MI subgroups due by late 2027 describe chronic patients; the only synthesis (PMID 42387250) is not in a target or general journal.
- G4 Credible design: conditional pass. Clone-censor-weight emulation of timing has target-journal precedent (PMID 40240093); a naive comparison would not be cited, because published mortality estimates already exceed trial effects.
- G5 Not incremental: borderline pass, only in the narrowed form (timing strategies, excluded populations, single primary composite, calibration and negative controls).

## 6. Design

### Primary: target trial emulation of initiation timing with clone-censor-weight methods

- Eligibility: adults discharged alive after type 1 MI in a national MI registry linked to dispensing, hospital episodes and cause of death; type 2 diabetes (diagnosis or HbA1c of 48 mmol/mol or above) or body mass index of 27 or above within 12 months before admission; no incretin dispensing in the prior 90 days; no type 1 diabetes, pancreatitis within 180 days, medullary thyroid cancer or dialysis.
- Treatment strategies: (1) early, start within 30 days of discharge; (2) deferred, no incretin in days 0 to 90, then start between day 91 and month 12; (3) none within 12 months. Primary contrast early against deferred. In the diabetes stratum, a new-user emulation of early incretin against early DPP-4 inhibitor initiation, following PMID 41392024.
- Time zero: discharge, identical for all clones. Each patient is cloned into every strategy and censored when observed treatment deviates from it, following PMID 40240093.
- Outcomes: primary, first of cardiovascular death, recurrent MI, ischaemic stroke or heart failure hospitalisation. Secondary: components, all-cause death, total heart failure events. Safety: major bleeding (no mechanism, so safety only), pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, discontinuation. Negative control outcomes: lumbar radiculopathy and abdominal hernia, as in PMID 42556854. Negative control exposure: early against deferred DPP-4 inhibitor initiation.
- Confounders: baseline age, sex, deprivation, ethnicity where held, body mass index, HbA1c, eGFR, ejection fraction, MI type, in-hospital heart failure, revascularisation, prior cardiovascular disease, frailty, cancer, SGLT2 inhibitor, insulin and lipid therapy, prior healthcare contacts, calendar time and region. Time-varying for censoring weights: readmissions and events before initiation, new SGLT2 inhibitor use, HbA1c and weight, cardiac rehabilitation, diabetes or obesity service contacts, new contraindications.
- Estimand: per-protocol effect of adhering to each strategy, as 12-month and 3-year risk differences and ratios; hazard ratio secondary.
- Analysis: stabilised inverse probability of censoring weights from weekly pooled logistic models, truncated at the 1st and 99th centiles; weighted cumulative incidence with non-cardiovascular death as a competing event; patient-level bootstrap for confidence intervals. Pre-specified modifiers: age 75 or above, eGFR below 60, sex, ejection fraction below 50%, agent class.
- Calibration: first emulate a SELECT-like contrast (initiation against none after day 90 in non-diabetic obesity) and compare it with hazard ratio 0.80 (PMID 37952131). A much larger emulated effect indicates residual confounding and governs interpretation of the timing contrast.
- Sensitivity: grace periods of 14 and 60 days; deferral starting at day 61 to match the 60-day trial exclusions; exclusion of events in the first 14 days; E-values.

Power, inputs shown. Event rate: first-year MACE (death, MI or stroke) of 4.03 per 100 patient-years in SWEDEHEART patients not given ezetimibe (PMID 40240093), taken as a conservative input; EMPACT-MI placebo patients, selected for heart failure risk, had heart failure hospitalisation or death at 6.6 per 100 patient-years (PMID 38587237). Effects: hazard ratio 0.80 from SELECT (PMID 37952131) as optimistic, 0.90 as plausible for early against deferred, since strategies differ only in the first months. Two-sided alpha 0.05, power 80%, Schoenfeld's formula D = (1.960 + 0.842)^2 / (p(1 minus p) x (ln HR)^2):

- hazard ratio 0.80: 631 events with equal allocation, 986 with 1 to 4 allocation;
- hazard ratio 0.90: 2829 events with equal allocation, 4420 with 1 to 4 allocation.

At 4.03 per 100 patient-years, 986 events need about 24,500 patient-years and 4420 events about 110,000. The 0.90 scenario needs pooled Nordic registries or UK national linkage with data from 2023 onward. Cloning and weighting inflate variance, so these are lower bounds.

### Alternative: value-of-information model leading to a registry-based randomised trial

- Model: patient-level Markov microsimulation from discharge with event-free, recurrent MI, stroke, heart failure and death states, and 90-day tunnel states. UK health service perspective, lifetime horizon with a 12-month output, 3.5% annual discounting (a UK convention, an analytic choice). Inputs from PMIDs 40240093, 38587237 and 37952131, including discontinuation for adverse events of 16.6% against 8.2% in SELECT. Expected value of perfect, partial perfect and sample information for the early-period effect.
- Trial: open-label, early (before discharge or within 14 days) against deferred (from day 91) initiation, randomised within a national MI registry, registered on ClinicalTrials.gov or ISRCTN. Primary endpoint: heart failure hospitalisation or all-cause death at 12 months.
- Sample size: control 9.1% (EMPACT-MI placebo, median 17.9 months; PMID 38587237). With hazard ratio 0.80 (PMID 37952131) the intervention proportion is 1 minus (0.909 to the power 0.80) = 7.35%; alpha 0.05 two-sided, power 80%, two-proportion normal approximation: 3863 per group, 7726 in total. With hazard ratio 0.90 (8.23%): 16,358 per group, 32,716 in total. An unselected population and a 12-month horizon would need more. The model would decide whether either is worth funding.

### Why a weaker design will not do

Propensity-matched initiator comparisons already exist (PMIDs 41297711, 41862113, 42219874), with mortality hazard ratios from 0.48 to 0.70 that a guideline committee would discount. Landmark designs discard the early events that are the point of the question. A meta-analysis of these cohorts exists (PMID 42387250) and is fragile.

## 7. Data and collaborators

Needed: a national MI registry with body mass index, HbA1c and ejection fraction, linked to complete community dispensing (including privately paid prescriptions), hospital episodes and cause of death, from 2023 onward. Candidates: SWEDEHEART (PMID 40240093); Danish registries and the Western Denmark Heart Registry (PMID 41580697, which already holds a SELECT-eligible post-MI cohort); UK national MI audit linked to hospital, primary care and death data; Taiwan's national data (PMID 41392024). The investigators behind these papers are the natural collaborators, and the SELECT, SOUL and SURPASS-CVOT groups for prior-MI subgroup data.

Owner position, stated plainly. The QMUL UK Biobank extract holds too few early post-MI initiators for a timing contrast and serves only for code testing. The Harefield PCI cohort lacks dispensing data. The planned Danish research visit is the most realistic route to registry data. The owner delivers the decision model and value-of-information analysis and registry-embedded trial methodology, which cover the alternative design fully, and has UK Biobank experience with SGLT2 inhibitors. Feasibility of the primary design in the owner's own data is low (score 2).

Overlap with active projects: none. The STEMI non-culprit network meta-analysis and STEMI biomarker review address other decisions; the microaxial flow pump microsimulation could serve as a method template only.

## 8. Result scenarios

- Early beats deferred, with calibration close to SELECT and null negative controls: an association supporting discharge initiation in patients the trials excluded, and a sized case for a registry-based trial. Fit for a parent journal because it addresses the window every outcome trial excluded.
- No difference between early and deferred, with early against none favouring treatment: the decision can safely wait for clinic, which matters for capacity and supply, and it would temper the large effects reported in commercial networks.
- Excess intolerance or events with early initiation in older or CKD patients: a pre-specified safety finding relevant to discharge bundles.
- Calibration fails: the timing result is reported with that caveat and the parent-journal case weakens; the value-of-information analysis remains publishable.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Access and engagement confounding: early initiators are selected by income, specialist contact and health engagement, and published estimates (PMIDs 41297711, 42103200; the medRxiv preprint) exceed trial effects. De-risk with calibration, negative controls, the DPP-4 inhibitor comparator and E-values.
- Early-event and time-zero handling: PMID 43010688 found its estimate sensitive to exactly these choices. A fixed discharge time zero and pre-registered grace periods address this, with alternatives reported as sensitivity analyses.
- Exposure capture: privately paid prescriptions for weight loss are invisible in some national records and would misclassify early initiators as deferred. Choose registries with complete dispensing capture.
- Missing body mass index and HbA1c in registries: use registries that record them at admission; impute covariates, never eligibility.
- Short follow-up, since use rose from 2023: the 12-month primary horizon reflects this.
- Scoop risk: moderate to high. The store holds more than ten post-MI incretin cohorts and syntheses from 2025 and 2026, prior-MI trial subgroups are likely by late 2027, and a timing analysis is a natural next step for the SWEDEHEART group (PMID 40240093). The decision model can start at once and does not depend on data access.

Adversarial check summary (24 October 2026): survives with a narrower angle. The reviewer found no randomised trial or target-journal synthesis of early post-discharge initiation, but several lower-tier observational analyses in the same population (PMIDs 41297711, 42219874, 41862113, 42103200, and a nationwide Czech registry analysis that is not in the store and is not cited here) and a meta-analysis (PMID 42387250). Editor objections: the direction of effect is largely known; observational mortality estimates are implausibly large; bleeding was padding; registries record body mass index and HbA1c poorly; diabetes and non-diabetic obesity were mixed; parent-journal fit is weak for a descriptive emulation. Design objections: immortal time with a 12-month window, access confounding, an SGLT2 inhibitor comparator already used, invisible private prescriptions, small exposed groups before 2024, and a diluted multi-outcome estimand. The reviewer recommended the timing framing, a single primary composite, bleeding as safety, and one-point reductions in novelty and, for the diabetes stratum, equipoise; the register applies the novelty reduction. This brief adds the registry evidence that every large incretin outcome trial excluded MI within 60 or 90 days, and the new emulation (PMID 43010688) showing that early-event handling drives results; both support the narrowed angle.
