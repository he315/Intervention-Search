# Adversarial review of Q-031

Question reviewed: whether omitting aspirin at the time of primary PCI for STEMI increases ischaemic events, addressed by an updated IPD meta-analysis of P2Y12 inhibitor monotherapy trials with STEMI-specific estimates. Sources used: the local store (records and trial snapshot), PubMed E-utilities and the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, all queried in this session on 24 October 2026. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session. Abstract figures are as reported.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

Direct randomised evidence on immediate aspirin omission in STEMI has now been published, which is the main threat to the question.

- PMID 42670955, N Engl J Med, 29 August 2026: PREMIUM (NCT05709626), "Aspirin Omission at the Time of Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention in STEMI". Open-label randomised trial in Japan, 2216 patients in the full analysis population (1109 prasugrel monotherapy, 1107 DAPT). Death, stroke or MI at 12 months: HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.75, P = 0.40 for noninferiority. Major bleeding (BARC 3 or 5): HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.91. This is the exact comparison in the PICO, in the exact population, and it is a target-quality publication.
- PMID 40888723, N Engl J Med, 27 November 2025: NEO-MINDSET (NCT04360720), 3410 ACS patients, monotherapy begun within 4 days of admission versus 12-month DAPT. Primary ischaemic composite: Kaplan-Meier 7.0% versus 5.5%, absolute difference 1.47 percentage points, 95% CI -0.16 to 3.10, P = 0.11 for noninferiority. Stent thrombosis in 12 versus 4 patients.
- PMID 41384891, J Am Coll Cardiol, 27 January 2026: prespecified NEO-MINDSET STEMI versus NSTE-ACS analysis. Among 2119 STEMI patients the ischaemic composite was 8.2% versus 5.2% (HR 1.60, 95% CI 1.14 to 2.24); in NSTE-ACS the HR was 0.84 (0.53 to 1.35), P for interaction 0.030. Bleeding was lower with monotherapy in both strata (STEMI HR 0.37, 0.22 to 0.61). This already supplies a STEMI-specific effect estimate and the interaction test the proposal wants to generate.
- PMID 41886437, PLoS Med, March 2026: aggregate-data meta-analysis of seven trials (n = 27,743) with prespecified timing strata and trial sequential analysis. Immediate aspirin noninitiation or in-hospital cessation increased MI (HR 1.41, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.97), early post-discharge discontinuation did not (HR 0.97, 0.76 to 1.24). This predates PREMIUM and NEO-MINDSET STEMI results being added, so its immediate-omission stratum is now out of date, but the timing question has been framed.
- PMID 42080243, Crit Pathw Cardiol, 1 September 2026: three trials, 10,736 patients; aspirin discontinuation within 30 days associated with more MI (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.31 to 3.44) without a bleeding reduction in the first 30 days.
- PMID 42735966, JACC Cardiovasc Interv, 14 September 2026: ten trials (n = 29,232) of ultrashort DAPT in ACS; higher MACCE in STEMI but not NSTE-ACS; intensive abbreviation (up to 1 week DAPT or clopidogrel/aspirin monotherapy) HR 1.37 (1.11 to 1.69), interaction P = 0.003.
- PMID 42192090, Cardiovasc Interv Ther, July 2026: pairwise and network meta-analysis with time-to-event data (10 RCTs, 33,292 participants); aspirin discontinuation under 1 month ranked unfavourably for mortality.
- PMID 42507310 (Cardiovasc Drugs Ther, 27 July 2026; seven trials, 20,501 patients; stent thrombosis RR 1.72, 1.07 to 2.78), PMID 42769537 (J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv, September 2026; reconstructed IPD, five trials, 12,984 patients, excludes immediate withdrawal), PMID 42607744 (Am J Cardiol, 15 October 2026; nine studies, 31,505 patients), PMID 41671172 (Clin Appl Thromb Hemost, 2026; ten RCTs, 35,277 patients) and PMID 41926558 (J Cardiovasc Pharmacol, April 2026; 30 studies) are further 2026 meta-analyses of the same trial base. None is a target- or general-tier journal, but together they show that the aggregate-data space is saturated.
- Earlier IPD syntheses, all in ACS and all showing bleeding reduction without excess ischaemia after a 1 to 3 month DAPT lead-in: PMID 38750627 (Eur Heart J, 1 September 2024; TICO and T-PASS, 5906 ACS patients), PMID 37870970 (Circulation, 20 February 2024; TWILIGHT and TICO, 7529 patients, from 3 months), PMID 39153473 (Med, 13 December 2024; STEMI-only IPD from TICO and T-PASS, 2253 patients, ischaemic HR 0.88, 0.49 to 1.61, bleeding HR 0.56, 0.35 to 0.92) and PMID 39961108 (Ann Intern Med, April 2025; TICO, T-PASS, ULTIMATE-DAPT, 9130 patients including 3132 with STEMI, results consistent across ACS types). PMID 39382876 (JAMA Cardiol, 1 December 2024) is a network meta-analysis of short DAPT in ACS (15 RCTs, 35,326 patients, 11,339 with STEMI).
- Small randomised pilots of true aspirin-free strategy from the time of PCI in STEMI: PMID 42061600 (Am Heart J, 1 September 2026; 200 patients, 3-month MACCE HR 1.54, 0.26 to 9.24) and PMID 42200636 (EuroIntervention, 20 July 2026; STOP-IMH pilot, one-year MACCE HR 1.04, 0.26 to 4.17). Editorial PMID 42473747 (EuroIntervention, 20 July 2026), "Aspirin withdrawal after primary PCI for STEMI: timing matters", shows the message is already being communicated.

Overlap: the proposal's intervention ("from within 1 month, or from the time of PCI") merges two different strategies. The 1-month strategies are covered by existing IPD work (Med, Eur Heart J, Ann Intern Med) with STEMI strata. The from-PCI strategy now has two NEJM trials, the JACC STEMI-specific NEO-MINDSET analysis, the PLoS Med timing analysis and two pilots. I found no published IPD meta-analysis that pools NEO-MINDSET, PREMIUM and the pilot trials with a STEMI-specific timing analysis, in the PubMed searches run here (2025 to 2026, terms for aspirin-free, aspirin omission, monotherapy, with meta-analysis, IPD and pooled-analysis filters). That is the only residual gap.

## Ongoing trials

From the ClinicalTrials.gov snapshot in the store and the live registry listing for STEMI and aspirin:

- NCT05491200, COMPARE STEMI ONE: 1656 participants (estimated), recruiting, primary completion 2027-08-01. Tests prasugrel-based short DAPT (30 to 45 days) then monotherapy versus 12-month DAPT, factorial with OCT-guided completion. This is a 1-month de-escalation, not omission at PCI, and would add STEMI-specific data to the 1-month stratum.
- NCT05785897, STARS DAPT: 350 (estimated), recruiting, primary completion 2027-03-01. Polymer-free sirolimus stent with P2Y12 inhibitor aspirin-free single antiplatelet strategy versus DAPT in STEMI, co-primary MACCE and BARC 3 or 5. Too small to settle the question but eligible for pooling.
- NCT05986968, STOP-IMH: 200, active not recruiting, primary completion 2026-01-03; pilot results are published (PMID 42200636, 42061600).
- NCT07684742, SAPT-ACS: 1900 (estimated), not yet recruiting, primary completion 2031-07-01. Early P2Y12 monotherapy after IVUS-guided PCI in ACS including STEMI, primary MACE and clinically relevant bleeding. Outside the 24-month window.
- NCT05262803, Dan-DAPT: 2808, recruiting, primary completion 2028-12; shorter DAPT in high bleeding risk MI, primary BARC 2 to 5 bleeding and NACE; not an omission trial.
- NCT07257198, PANTHEON: 2570, recruiting, primary completion 2030-01-01; aspirin-free ticagrelor in medically managed MI, therefore not primary PCI.
- NCT06756945, LEAST: 3612, not yet recruiting, primary completion 2026-12; compares aspirin dosing after STEMI PCI, not omission.

No adequately powered, STEMI-specific, immediate-omission RCT other than PREMIUM is expected to report within 24 months; the existing PREMIUM and NEO-MINDSET trials have already reported. For the 1-month strategy, COMPARE STEMI ONE (2027-08) falls inside 24 months of today and would be a natural addition rather than a competitor.

## Editor objections

1. The headline question has been answered by randomised evidence in the NEJM (PMIDs 42670955 and 40888723). Both trials failed noninferiority for the ischaemic composite when aspirin was omitted at or soon after PCI, with a prespecified STEMI interaction in NEO-MINDSET (PMID 41384891). An IPD pooling of those trials would confirm a result that readers already have.
2. Fully pooled aggregate evidence already exists in at least eight 2026 meta-analyses (PMIDs 41886437, 42080243, 42735966, 42192090, 42507310, 42769537, 42607744, 41671172), so a new meta-analysis of the same trial base reads as a repeat. IPD adds value only if it resolves something aggregate data cannot, and the proposal does not yet name that something beyond a landmark analysis.
3. The number of trials with true omission from PCI in STEMI is very small (PREMIUM, NEO-MINDSET, two pilots, STARS DAPT when it reports). Time-varying effect and interaction analyses with so few trials and few events in the first 30 days would be underpowered, and trial-level heterogeneity (open-label designs, Japanese versus Brazilian populations, differing endpoints including urgent revascularisation in one and not the other, prasugrel low dose versus ticagrelor or prasugrel) cannot be estimated with random effects on treatment across two to four trials.
4. Industry funding of PREMIUM (Boston Scientific Japan, per the abstract) and the requirement for data-sharing agreements from academic trial groups raise access risk and make a Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, European Heart Journal or JACC main-article slot unlikely for an incremental pooled analysis.

## Design objections

- The design can technically work, but only for a narrower question. One-stage Cox models stratified by trial are appropriate for IPD. Random effects on treatment with REML and Hartung-Knapp adjustment are poorly behaved with fewer than about five trials; with two to four omission trials the heterogeneity estimate will be unstable, and a common-effect or Bayesian model with a weakly informative prior on tau would be more defensible.
- Pooling 1-month discontinuation trials (TICO, T-PASS, ULTIMATE-DAPT, STOPDAPT-3 style trials) with immediate-omission trials in a single treatment contrast is not interpretable, because the evidence points to different effects by timing (PMIDs 41886437, 42080243, 42735966). The primary analysis would need timing as the stratifier, which collapses the STEMI immediate-omission stratum to the few trials above.
- The STEMI-by-treatment interaction is already reported within NEO-MINDSET (P for interaction 0.030, PMID 41384891) and, for 1-month strategies, in the Med and Ann Intern Med IPD analyses (PMIDs 39153473, 39961108). A new interaction test adds precision only if PREMIUM IPD is obtained; PREMIUM reports a STEMI-only population, so it informs the STEMI stratum but not the interaction.
- The proposed endpoint set is feasible, but the harmonisation burden is real: PREMIUM's primary is death, stroke or MI, NEO-MINDSET adds urgent target vessel revascularisation, and bleeding definitions differ (BARC 3 or 5 versus BARC 2, 3 or 5 and clinically relevant). The proposal should name the common definitions.
- Follow-up truncation: the landmark analyses at 1 and 3 months need event-level dates for early MI and stent thrombosis. Stent thrombosis counts were 12 versus 4 in NEO-MINDSET, so the event numbers are too small for stable landmark hazard ratios in a pooled set of this size.

## Verdict

Partly scooped (narrower angle only; novelty and equipoise are materially reduced).

Reason: the clinical question as worded, whether omitting aspirin at the time of primary PCI for STEMI increases ischaemic events, has been tested directly in PREMIUM (PMID 42670955, hazard ratio 1.34, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.75, noninferiority not shown) and by the STEMI stratum of NEO-MINDSET (PMID 41384891, HR 1.60, 95% CI 1.14 to 2.24), and the timing distinction between immediate omission and 1-month discontinuation is already published in aggregate form (PMID 41886437) with a dozen further 2026 meta-analyses of the same trials. The one surviving angle is an IPD synthesis restricted to STEMI that pools every trial with aspirin withheld from the time of PCI or within days (NEO-MINDSET STEMI, PREMIUM, STOP-IMH pilot, the other pilot, and STARS DAPT when it reports in 2027), with timing against the 1-month trials as an explicit contrast, patient-level time-varying effects in the first 30 days, and heterogeneity of treatment effect by baseline ischaemic and bleeding risk. Whether a guideline committee would cite that over the two NEJM trials is doubtful unless the early-hazard and risk-stratification findings are genuinely new. I would keep the question as a low-priority candidate that waits for STARS DAPT and COMPARE STEMI ONE (primary completions 2027-03 and 2027-08), and not as a top-ten item now.

Suggested gate changes:

- G2 (equipoise): weaken to a conditional pass or fail for the original wording. Two NEJM randomised trials did not show noninferiority of omission; equipoise remains only for risk-stratified selection (high bleeding risk and low ischaemic risk) and for timing between day 0 and 1 month. I did not read guideline text in this review and make no statement on class or level.
- G3 (not about to be answered): the RCT clause is passed for new trials but fails in substance, because the principal trials have already reported. Record as "answered for the average STEMI patient by PMIDs 42670955 and 41384891; residual question is early-hazard timing and risk stratification". The meta-analysis clause is not triggered by a target- or general-tier review, since I found none in those tiers in the past 24 months that covers the omission trials, but the PLoS Med (41886437) and JACC Cardiovasc Interv (42735966) analyses cover the same evidence base and should be noted.
- G5 (not incremental): fail as currently framed (a repeat pooled analysis of trials already reported individually and in aggregate). It could pass only if framed around IPD-derived questions that aggregate data cannot answer (time-varying hazard, risk-based heterogeneity of treatment effect) and if PREMIUM IPD access is secured in advance.
- Novelty: reduce from the current score to 2 of 5.
- Equipoise: reduce to 2 of 5 for the average STEMI patient; 3 of 5 would be defensible only for the risk-stratified and timing question.
- Timeliness: unchanged or slightly lower; the two pending trials report in 2027, so a synthesis timed to their readout would be stronger than one done now.
