# Adversarial review: Q-026

Question: systematic coronary CTA (with invasive angiography for significant disease) plus protocolised secondary prevention versus usual care after adjudicated type 2 MI, in a registry-based randomised trial. Searches were run on the local store (about 50 in-scope type 2 MI records by title), PubMed (three queries, 24 October 2026) and the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Only records retrieved and read in this session are cited.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review, meta-analysis, trial emulation or large analysis that answers the question was found in a target or general journal for 2024 to 2026. Closest items:

- 36705027, J Investig Med, 2023-03: systematic review and meta-analysis comparing outcomes of type 2 versus type 1 MI. Prognosis only; no overlap with the intervention.
- 42882047, J Clin Med, 2026-09-30: systematic review with narrative synthesis of factors associated with invasive angiography use in type 2 MI. It excluded randomised treatment assignment and identified no trial evidence on strategy; it confirms the role of angiography is undefined.
- 39206549, Circulation, 2024-12-03: prespecified analysis of the MINT trial (NCT02981407) by type 1 or 2 MI (type 2 n=1955). Transfusion strategy, not coronary evaluation; no overlap.
- 37777947, J Am Coll Cardiol, 2023-10-24 (NCT04864119, n=50): prospective CTA and FFRCT study. Coronary plaque in 46 of 50 and stenosis of at least 50% in 42%. Supports prevalence of CAD, not outcomes.
- 35341327, Circulation, 2022-04-19 (NCT03338504, DEMAND-MI): prospective cohort of consecutive patients with type 2 MI, all imaged; the primary outcome was CAD prevalence. Same supporting role.
- 39983613, JACC Adv, 2025-03: utilisation of procedures and cardioprotective medicines in type 2 MI. Documents practice variation.
- 39658094, Eur Heart J, 2025-02-07, and 38506815, JAMA Cardiol, 2024-05-01: commentary pieces on diagnosis and management gaps. They state the need for trials.
- 41702823, Cardiovasc Revasc Med, 2026-06: review stating there are no trial-validated pathways for coronary evaluation in type II MI.
- 39983613 and 40886105 (J Am Heart Assoc, 2025-09-16) are observational and associational; none supports a causal answer.

Overlap: none answers the comparison. The appraised store is thin (about 1,000 of 51,000 records), so absence in unappraised records is limited by title and registry-field matching.

## Ongoing trials

No adequately powered outcome RCT of the same comparison was found. Relevant registrations:

- NCT05419583 (TARGET-Type 2, University of Edinburgh): pilot RCT, n=60, started 2022-11-14, primary completion 2024-06-30, completed. Published as 40305953 (JACC Adv, 2025-05-01): 4,127 screened, 60 randomised (28 versus 32), adherence 90.7% (95% CI 85.3%-96.1%); the authors state a multicentre trial is now required. The intervention targets coronary or structural heart disease, so it is a close precursor and the most likely route to a full trial. No registration for a definitive trial was found; this must be checked with the Edinburgh group before commitment.
- NCT06047392 (Glasgow, NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde): pilot RCT of CT coronary angiography in suspected type 2 MI, n=100, primary completion 2024-02, status unknown. Primary outcome is change in diagnosis, not clinical events.
- NCT06382402 (University of Louisville): RCT of CCTA-guided strategy in type 2 NSTEMI, 3-year MACE; withdrawn with no participants (2025-01-01 planned completion), change of design planned. A redesigned version could reappear.
- NCT04838808 (R2MI): rivaroxaban versus placebo, stopped early, 7 randomised (37180843, 2023). Drug question, not this one; its recruitment lessons (94.5% of type 2 MI patients had an exclusion criterion) are relevant to design.
- NCT05858112 (SuperTROPO, n=3000, status unknown): diagnostic accuracy, not strategy.

G3 holds on current evidence: nothing powered is expected to report within 24 months. Scoop risk comes from a possible multicentre TARGET-Type 2 follow-on, which would be unlikely to report before 2030.

## Editor objections

- Heterogeneity: type 2 MI covers sepsis, tachyarrhythmia, anaemia, hypoxia, hypotension and others, with high non-cardiac competing mortality. An intervention aimed at coronary disease may affect only a minority; the all-cause death and cardiovascular death outcomes will be diluted.
- Two interventions are bundled (imaging-led revascularisation decisions and protocolised prevention). An editor will ask which component acts; the design cannot separate them.
- Prevention without imaging is cheap and could be tested alone; a factorial or a prevention-only comparator may be more defensible.
- The pilot (40305953) shows only 10% of screened patients were eligible and 42% of those approached consented; generalisability and representativeness will be questioned.
- Adjudication of type 2 MI is inconsistent across sites and misclassification of type 1 versus type 2 is common; a registry-based design cannot supply adjudicated diagnoses.
- Interventional benefit has been argued from observational studies with selection bias, which is the case for a trial but also means the effect size is unknown and the sample size poorly anchored.
- Likely viewed as a subspecialty or acute-care paper unless the result is clearly positive on hard outcomes; a null result is publishable mainly in a target journal if the trial is large and pragmatic.

## Design objections

- A registry-based randomised trial depends on a registry capturing adjudicated type 2 MI at admission. Most national MI registries (and hospital-episode data) code type 2 MI poorly, so eligibility, randomisation and diagnosis would rely on local adjudication outside the registry, undermining the registry-based advantage.
- Outcomes (cardiovascular death, MI, HF hospitalisation, death) are available from registries, but recurrent type 2 versus type 1 MI is not reliably coded; the primary should be a composite that avoids MI subtype.
- Delivery of the intervention (CTA, invasive angiography, protocolised prevention) needs site-level protocol adherence and cannot be done through registry data alone; process fidelity needs separate capture.
- Sample size is uncertain. With high early mortality and competing non-cardiac death, event rates at 2 years are high but treatment effect on them is probably modest; a trial would need several thousand participants. No calculation is made here because no event rates were taken from a retrieved abstract.
- Cluster-randomised or stepped-wedge designs may be more feasible given the screening yield seen in 40305953, but would add contamination and diagnostic-labelling bias.
- Credibility: a pragmatic multicentre RCT, possibly using the Edinburgh pilot infrastructure, is the design a guideline committee would cite. A registry-based version is credible only in a country with mandatory troponin-linked MI registration and adjudicated MI types.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The question remains open: no synthesis or trial answers it, the supporting literature is observational or feasibility work, and the gap is repeatedly acknowledged in target-journal commentary (39658094, 38506815). It should not be promoted unchanged, because the intervention bundles two components, the population is heterogeneous, and the registry-based design is weak where diagnostic adjudication is the main requirement. A narrower angle would restrict the population to type 2 MI with a plausible coronary mechanism (for example patients with prior CAD or a high pre-test probability), compare CTA-led coronary evaluation alone or in a factorial with protocolised prevention, and use a pragmatic multicentre RCT with registry follow-up for outcomes, ideally in partnership with the TARGET-Type 2 group rather than in competition. Suggested changes: G3 stays pass but with a note on TARGET-Type 2 (NCT05419583) follow-on risk and the redesigned Louisville study; G4 should be marked conditional on how adjudicated type 2 MI is captured; G5 passes. Novelty score should be lowered by one point (the pilot and Glasgow study show the idea is already in testing), and equipoise should be kept (no outcome trial, guidelines silent on strategy per 41702823) with a note that the supporting evidence is observational only.
