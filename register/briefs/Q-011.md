# Q-011: Antithrombotic therapy after TAVI in patients with an atrial fibrillation or other oral anticoagulation indication, and leaflet thrombosis as a link to clinical events: individual patient data meta-analysis, with reconstructed data where curves allow and a pre-specified update for CT-guided therapy

Status: candidate. Created and last reviewed 24 October 2026. Domains D3 and D8. Target journal: European Heart Journal or JACC.

Narrowing. The register title for Q-011 was an all-strategy, reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis with a pre-specified update at the next trial readouts. The independent adversarial check of 24 October 2026 (register/briefs/Q-011_adversarial.md) found that the non-AF half of that question is already covered by several network meta-analyses, that the two largest new readouts have already been published, and that an all-strategy aggregate network would repeat trials that are already pooled. This brief is therefore built on the narrower question below, and the all-strategy network is demoted to a descriptive secondary analysis. The change is deliberate and is stated here so that nobody reads the brief as a defence of the original framing.

## 1. Question

In adults with a successful transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) who have atrial fibrillation or another indication for oral anticoagulation, does a direct oral anticoagulant, a vitamin K antagonist, or either with an added antiplatelet agent differ in all-cause death, stroke or systemic thromboembolism, major bleeding and net clinical outcome from 30 days to 1 year, and, across all randomised comparisons that imaged the valve, does the effect of an antithrombotic strategy on CT-defined leaflet thrombosis (hypoattenuated leaflet thickening, HALT) predict its effect on clinical events?

PICO. Population: adults after successful TAVI, analysed in two separate strata (oral anticoagulation indication; no indication), with the first stratum primary. Interventions: direct oral anticoagulant, vitamin K antagonist, single antiplatelet therapy, anticoagulant with or without added clopidogrel, and CT-guided therapy (as a pre-specified later update). Comparators: each other. Outcomes: all-cause death, stroke or systemic thromboembolism, major or life-threatening bleeding, a net clinical composite, and HALT on four-dimensional CT as a separate binary outcome. Timing: time from TAVI to 1 year, with a 30-day landmark and restricted mean survival time at 12 months.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision is the antithrombotic prescription at discharge after TAVI, taken by the heart team and the treating structural or interventional cardiologist, and revisited by guideline committees. Patients with an anticoagulation indication are the group in which the decision is hardest, because they carry the bleeding risk of anticoagulation and, in the trials that included them, are the group in which the comparison of anticoagulant strategies has rested on a few studies.

Population size with a source: not retrieved. The store contains no record from which a national or international count of TAVI procedures or of the proportion with atrial fibrillation can be copied, so none is stated. Documented practice variation with a source: not retrieved. Both items should be sourced from registry reports before a protocol is submitted (first action below).

Timeliness comes from three sources. Two small CT-imaging trials reported on 30 August 2026 (42669043 in JAMA and 42669071 in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology), which changes the HALT evidence base. A trial of CT-guided personalised therapy versus lifelong single antiplatelet therapy (NCT06168370, 2,500 participants, primary completion 1 December 2028) is registered and cannot be synthesised before it reports. Several antiplatelet trials in patients without an anticoagulation indication are due in 2026 and 2027 (section 4), which matters for the secondary non-AF analysis and not for the primary question.

Guideline positions have not been extracted for this question and no class or level is stated here. The most relevant consensus document in the store is an ESC Working Group and EAPCI consensus on antithrombotic management in TAVI (33822924, European Heart Journal, 14 June 2021); its recommendations have not been read beyond the abstract.

## 3. What is known

Randomised trials in patients with an anticoagulation indication (primary stratum):

- 34449183 (New England Journal of Medicine, 2 December 2021), edoxaban versus vitamin K antagonist in atrial fibrillation after TAVR, 1426 patients enrolled. Primary efficacy composite hazard ratio 1.05 (95% CI 0.85 to 1.31; P = 0.01 for noninferiority). Major bleeding hazard ratio 1.40 (95% CI 1.03 to 1.91). Death or stroke hazard ratio 0.85 (95% CI 0.66 to 1.11).
- 35583186 (European Heart Journal, 1 August 2022), the ATLANTIS trial of apixaban versus standard of care, 1500 randomised, stratified by the need for chronic anticoagulation. Primary outcome hazard ratio 0.92 (95% CI 0.73 to 1.16) with Pinteraction = 0.57 between strata. In Stratum 1 (n = 451), an exploratory analysis showed no difference for all endpoints between apixaban and vitamin K antagonist. This is the one trial that randomised both strata under a common protocol.
- 32223116 (New England Journal of Medicine, 30 April 2020), POPular TAVI cohort B, oral anticoagulation alone versus oral anticoagulation plus clopidogrel for 3 months, 157 and 156 patients. Bleeding in 21.7% versus 34.6% (risk ratio 0.63; 95% CI 0.43 to 0.90).
- NCT02735902 (AVATAR), anticoagulation alone versus anticoagulation and aspirin, 170 enrolled, completed; the registry entry is in the store but no abstract has been read for it.

Randomised trials in patients without an anticoagulation indication (secondary stratum):

- 31733180 (New England Journal of Medicine, 9 January 2020), GALILEO, rivaroxaban-based versus antiplatelet-based strategy, 1644 patients. Death or thromboembolic events hazard ratio 1.35 (95% CI 1.01 to 1.81); major, disabling or life-threatening bleeding hazard ratio 1.50 (95% CI 0.95 to 2.37).
- 32865376 (New England Journal of Medicine, 8 October 2020), POPular TAVI cohort A, aspirin alone versus aspirin plus clopidogrel, 331 and 334 patients. Bleeding in 15.1% versus 26.6% (risk ratio 0.57; 95% CI 0.42 to 0.77).
- 28527771 (JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions, 10 July 2017), ARTE, aspirin versus aspirin plus clopidogrel, 222 patients, stopped after 74% of the planned inclusion.
- 35373583 (Circulation, 9 August 2022), ADAPT-TAVR, edoxaban versus dual antiplatelet therapy, 229 patients in the intention-to-treat population. Leaflet thrombosis 9.8% versus 18.4% (absolute difference -8.5%; 95% CI -17.8% to 0.8%).
- 31733182 (New England Journal of Medicine, 9 January 2020), the GALILEO 4D CT substudy, 231 patients. Grade 3 or higher leaflet motion reduction in 2 of 97 versus 11 of 101.
- 42669043 (JAMA, 30 August 2026), ACASA-TAVI (NCT05035277), 360 participants aged 65 to 80 years, NOAC versus aspirin monotherapy for 12 months. HALT 16.2% versus 28.6% (risk ratio 0.55; 95% CI 0.37 to 0.82). Safety composite 7.5% versus 10.6% (risk difference -3.3%; 95% CI -9.5% to 2.8%).
- 42669071 (Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 30 August 2026), NOTION-4 (NCT06449469), 352 randomised. HALT at 1 year 32.2% versus 28.3% (risk difference -3.9%; 95% CI -14.4% to 6.6%); all-cause death, stroke or major or life-threatening bleeding 2.3% versus 8.2% (risk difference 5.9%; 95% CI 1.2% to 10.6%).

Existing syntheses (all outside the four target journals and the general tier):

- 29516608 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, 1 October 2018), single versus dual antiplatelet therapy, six studies, 840 patients; the last pairwise synthesis in the appraised subset and the one the register line cites.
- 33860685 (Journal of the American Heart Association, 20 April 2021), patient-level meta-analysis of aspirin alone versus dual antiplatelet therapy, 1086 patients in four trials. This is an IPD precedent for one comparison only.
- 38081213 (European Heart Journal Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, 14 August 2024), Bayesian network meta-analysis, 11 randomised trials, 6415 patients, three trials with an anticoagulation indication.
- 40286375 (JACC Advances, 1 May 2025), network meta-analysis of patients without an indication, 5,821 patients.
- 39991882 (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 24 February 2025), network meta-analysis of NOACs, four trials, 4808 participants, searched to 7 October 2023, both strata.
- 40615791 (BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, 4 July 2025), network meta-analysis, 11 studies, 6,547 patients.

None of these used reconstructed or individual patient time-to-event data in the anticoagulation-indication stratum, and none treated HALT as a trial-level predictor of clinical events. I read the abstracts of these records; the adversarial report identifies further syntheses (for example 36640149, 41019885 and 41571953) of which I read only titles, so they are not described here.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: conflicting trials, together with an evidence base in the anticoagulation-indication stratum that rests on a small number of trials.

- G1 Consequence: pass. Every TAVI patient needs a discharge antithrombotic decision, judged on death, stroke and major bleeding.
- G2 Equipoise: pass for the anticoagulation-indication stratum and for CT-guided therapy; weaker for the non-AF comparison, where pooled results in 38081213, 40286375 and 40615791 favour single antiplatelet therapy. Guideline positions are unverified, so equipoise rests on trial evidence only.
- G3 Not about to be answered: pass for the anticoagulation-indication stratum. For the non-AF stratum it is at risk, because NCT05283356 and NCT06007222 are due within 24 months. ACASA-TAVI (360) and NOTION-4 (352) add 712 participants (360 + 352) to the approximately 6,400 to 6,500 in the existing network meta-analyses (6415 in 38081213; 6,547 in 40615791), about 11% (712 divided by 6,415 is 11.1%), which does not by itself change the pooled evidence materially. The CT-guided arm cannot be answered by synthesis before NCT06168370 completes.
- G4 Credible design: pass only with true IPD for the main trials or at least for the ATLANTIS strata; a reconstructed aggregate analysis alone would probably not be cited as decisive by a guideline committee.
- G5 Not incremental: borderline for the original all-strategy network, passes for the narrowed design because it adds individual-level or reconstructed time-to-event data, a formal stratum comparison and a trial-level HALT-to-event analysis that no existing synthesis has done.

## 6. Design

Primary design: individual patient data meta-analysis of randomised trials in patients with an anticoagulation indication, with time-to-event data reconstructed for any trial whose investigators do not share data and whose curves allow it, and a separate trial-level analysis of HALT. Alternative design: a registry-based randomised trial of anticoagulant strategy in AF patients after TAVI within a national TAVI registry; it is slower and was judged the wrong primary because the existing trials already cover the comparisons and the open problem is how their evidence is combined.

Methodological choices, each with a reason:

- Two separate analyses, not one network. The AF or anticoagulation-indication analysis (DOAC versus vitamin K antagonist, anticoagulant with or without clopidogrel or aspirin) and the non-AF analysis are kept apart, because pooling them invites an invalid shared-comparator assumption. The non-AF network is a descriptive secondary analysis with an explicit transitivity table (age, follow-up, imaging schedule, valve type).
- Eligible trials in the primary stratum, from sections 3 and 4: 34449183 (1426), the ATLANTIS Stratum 1 (451, from 35583186), POPular TAVI cohort B (313, from 32223116) and AVATAR (170, from NCT02735902). Derived pooled sample: 1426 + 451 + 313 + 170 = 2360 randomised patients. Four trials is a small number, and the brief says so rather than presenting the analysis as a large network.
- IPD route. Data requests go to the sponsors or investigators of each trial; the ATLANTIS request matters most because it is the only trial with a stratified randomisation across both strata, which allows a within-trial treatment-by-stratum interaction instead of a between-trial comparison confounded by trial. A one-stage Cox model with a trial-stratified baseline hazard and treatment-by-stratum interaction is the primary model for ATLANTIS; a two-stage analysis combines trials.
- Reconstruction method where IPD is unavailable: digitise Kaplan-Meier curves and numbers at risk and apply the Guyot iterative algorithm, validated by comparing the reconstructed hazard ratio with the published one; trials without usable numbers at risk are excluded from the time-to-event analysis and kept in the binary-outcome analysis. Reconstruction degrades for rare events and truncated curves, so stroke and death are analysed with caution and the reconstruction error is reported.
- Effect measure and model: hazard ratio for time-to-event outcomes, risk ratio for binary outcomes. Random-effects model with REML estimation of tau-squared and the Hartung-Knapp adjustment by default. With four trials the Hartung-Knapp interval will be wide and tau-squared poorly estimated; a Bayesian random-effects model with a weakly informative prior on between-trial heterogeneity is the pre-specified sensitivity analysis, and the confidence intervals are not narrowed to look more informative.
- Zero events: no continuity correction in the primary binary analyses (binomial-normal generalised linear mixed model); a 0.5 correction and a Mantel-Haenszel analysis as sensitivity analyses.
- Landmark and restricted mean analysis: a 30-day landmark to separate early procedural events from later therapy effects, and restricted mean survival time at 12 months to avoid reliance on proportional hazards, which is doubtful for bleeding after a change of therapy.
- Competing risks: cause-specific hazards for stroke and bleeding with death as the competing event, and a Fine-Gray subdistribution analysis as sensitivity.
- Net clinical outcome: the trial-specific composites are heterogeneous (for example the composite in 34449183 includes valve thrombosis and major bleeding), so a harmonised composite of death, stroke and major bleeding is built from IPD where possible, and the trial-defined composites are reported alongside.
- HALT analysis. HALT is a binary imaging outcome at a fixed time and cannot be reconstructed as time-to-event, so it is pooled separately as a binary outcome by random-effects meta-analysis of risk ratios. Trials in scope from section 3: 35373583, 31733182, 42669043, 42669071 and any imaging substudy found by the search. Imaging time (3, 6 or 12 months) and the leaflet definition (grade 3 or higher motion reduction versus any hypoattenuated thickening) differ and are analysed as covariates, not pooled over.
- HALT as a link to clinical events. A weighted trial-level meta-regression of the effect on HALT against the effect on a thromboembolic and a bleeding outcome. With only a handful of trials this will have little power, and it is reported as hypothesis-generating unless IPD from the CT substudies allows an individual-level mediation analysis. The result is described as an association between effects, not as causal mediation, and HALT is not offered as a decision endpoint. NOTION-4 (42669071) shows an early effect at 3 months that attenuated by 12 months, so time since anticoagulant withdrawal enters the analysis.
- CT-guided therapy. No completed randomised trial exists in the store, so this arm enters only as a pre-specified living update when NCT06168370 reports (primary completion 1 December 2028), with the update rule and analysis code registered in advance.
- Protocol and registration: PROSPERO before data extraction; reporting by PRISMA-IPD; risk of bias by RoB 2; certainty by GRADE.
- Software: R, with meta and metafor for pooling and the owner's pre-positioned workflow with rerun function for updates.

Sample size: not applicable in the sense of a trial calculation, because the evidence base is fixed by the trials that exist. The derived total for the primary stratum is 2360 randomised patients (inputs above), and the HALT analysis has 229 + 231 + 360 + 352 = 1172 patients across four trials (inputs from 35373583, 31733182, 42669043 and 42669071; the first two give analysed or enrolled patients and the last two randomised participants, so the figure is indicative).

## 8. Result scenarios and publishability

- Anticoagulant strategies do not differ in efficacy but major bleeding is higher with a DOAC (consistent with the point estimates in 34449183), and the within-ATLANTIS interaction shows no stratum effect: this would give guideline committees a pooled time-to-event estimate with landmark and restricted mean results for the group the trials treat least well, and is publishable if the IPD analysis is credible.
- Added antiplatelet therapy increases bleeding without reducing thromboembolism in anticoagulated patients (consistent with 32223116): publishable as a confirmation with time-varying detail, weak for a parent journal alone.
- The trial-level HALT effect does not predict clinical events: this would argue against HALT as a surrogate and against HALT-driven prescribing, which is a useful negative result for the CT-guided strategy and a framing for NCT06168370.
- The HALT effect does predict a reduction in thromboembolism: this would support imaging-guided therapy but would be a trial-level association with few trials, and would need the 2028 update to be taken further.

Each scenario depends on the IPD route. Without IPD the work is a reconstructed-data synthesis and the most likely outlet is a specialty journal; the register records that explicitly.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Not new enough. At least five network or pairwise syntheses of the same trials exist, one of them Cochrane (39991882). De-risk: restrict the primary question to the anticoagulation-indication stratum and individual-level data, and do not present the all-strategy network as the headline.
- IPD refused or delayed. De-risk: begin with the reconstructed version, keep the protocol compatible with a later IPD upgrade, and approach the ATLANTIS and ENVISAGE-TAVI AF investigators first.
- Sparse evidence. Four trials in the primary stratum; Hartung-Knapp intervals will be wide and single-trial comparisons carry no heterogeneity estimate. De-risk: state this in the protocol, use the Bayesian sensitivity analysis, and avoid ranking claims.
- Surrogate endpoint. HALT is subclinical and reported at different times; de-risk by treating it only as a trial-level association and not as an endpoint.
- Open-label designs and varied composites. De-risk: harmonise outcome definitions from IPD or from the VARC definitions where reported, and use risk-of-bias and GRADE assessment.
- Scoop risk. High for any non-AF network analysis, because new analyses in specialty journals are frequent. Moderate for the AF and IPD angle. Mitigation: register early on PROSPERO and pre-specify the update rule.

Adversarial check, 24 October 2026: verdict survives with a narrower angle. No target- or general-journal synthesis covers the question, and no existing analysis combines AF stratification, time-to-event reconstruction and CT-defined leaflet thrombosis. Heavy trial-level overlap exists with 38081213, 40286375, 39991882 and 40615791, the non-AF comparison is largely settled in favour of single antiplatelet therapy in their results, and the two large new readouts (42669043, 42669071) have already occurred. Editor objections raised: a seventh network meta-analysis of the same trials; poor connection with single-trial edges; heterogeneous follow-up and populations; leaflet thrombosis as a surrogate; a probably stable headline; and registration of updates as a protocol device and not a contribution. Design objections raised: HALT cannot be reconstructed as time-to-event; reconstruction is weak for rare events; REML with Hartung-Knapp behaves poorly on sparse networks; effect modification by AF status is between-trial unless ATLANTIS IPD is obtained; and the two strata should be separate networks. This brief accepts the objections on novelty, network structure, strata and HALT handling and revises the design accordingly; it does not hold that the objection on stable headline applies to the anticoagulation-indication stratum, where the evidence base is small and no recent synthesis addresses it.
