# Q-006: Benchmarked target trial emulation of microaxial flow pump support in cardiogenic shock strata not covered by randomised evidence (age over 80, comatose or unwitnessed out-of-hospital arrest, mixed shock)

Status: candidate. Brief written 24 October 2026. Guideline positions have not been extracted for this question and no class or level is stated anywhere in this brief.

## Note on narrowing

The register line was created with the title "Transportability of the microaxial flow pump trial result to unselected AMI-CS: target trial emulation by DanGer Shock eligibility, age and sex". The independent adversarial check of 24 October 2026 (register/briefs/Q-006_adversarial.md) found that the eligible stratum, age and sex are already addressed by randomised data or randomised secondary analyses (PMID 38587239, PMID 39236726, PMID 39551167, PMID 41222528), and that eligibility-stratified registry descriptions exist in a target journal (PMID 41078250). This brief therefore drops age and sex as headline strata, treats the eligible stratum only as a calibration step, and builds the question on the strata that no completed or registered randomised comparison clearly covers. The narrowing is deliberate and is stated again in section 6.

## 1. Question

In adults with acute myocardial infarction-related cardiogenic shock who fall outside the DanGer Shock inclusion criteria, namely aged over 80 years, comatose or unwitnessed out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, and mixed shock, does early microaxial flow pump support, compared with standard care without a pump, change all-cause mortality at 30 and 180 days, once an emulation of the trial has first been shown to reproduce the randomised estimate in eligible patients?

- Population: AMI-CS patients in the non-eligible strata above, plus the eligible stratum for calibration.
- Intervention: microaxial flow pump started within a defined grace period of the revascularisation decision.
- Comparator: standard care without a microaxial flow pump.
- Outcomes: all-cause death at 30 and 180 days; severe bleeding and renal replacement therapy as secondary outcomes.
- Timing: time zero at shock diagnosis with the revascularisation decision, clone-censor-weight design.

## 2. Why it matters now

Decision. Shock teams and interventional cardiologists decide whether to place a microaxial flow pump in patients who would not have been randomised in DanGer Shock. DanGer Shock randomised 360 patients with STEMI and cardiogenic shock and reported death from any cause in 82 of 179 patients (45.8%) with the pump and 103 of 176 (58.5%) with standard care (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.99; PMID 38587239). The trial excluded comatose cardiac arrest (PMID 41222528, which describes enrolment as STEMI-CS excluding comatose cardiac arrest), and the same trial reported a composite safety event in 24.0% versus 6.2% (relative risk 4.74, 95% CI 2.36 to 9.55) and renal replacement therapy in 41.9% versus 26.7% (PMID 38587239).

Population size. The store contains no UK count of pump-treated AMI-CS patients; the number in BCIS, ICNARC or Harefield data was not retrieved and must be established before further investment (see section 10). Elsewhere, the J-PVAD registry identified 3975 AMI-CS patients treated with Impella between 2020 and 2023 (PMID 41078250), and a European multicentre cohort of microaxial pump-supported cardiogenic shock included 1043 patients across 11 centres between 2010 and 2023 (PMID 42403523).

Practice variation. In J-PVAD, eligible STEMI-CS accounted for 35.6% of Impella-treated AMI-CS, and 30-day mortality was 51.3% in the out-of-hospital arrest group (63.4% without return of spontaneous circulation), 39.8% with mechanical complications and 33.3% in NSTEMI-CS (PMID 41078250). In the CS Working Group registry (2020 to 2024), 19.3% of patients met DanGer Shock criteria, and only 20% of DanGer-like patients received Impella CP as first device (PMID 41946200). These figures show that pump use in practice extends well beyond the randomised population and that device choice is heterogeneous.

## 3. What is known

- PMID 38587239 (NEJM, 18 April 2024): DanGer Shock, 355 analysed, hazard ratio 0.74 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.99) for death at 180 days, with more bleeding and renal replacement therapy.
- PMID 39236726 (Lancet, 14 September 2024): individual patient data meta-analysis of nine reports (n=1114); early unselected mechanical circulatory support gave HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.74-1.03) for 6-month mortality, left ventricular unloading devices 0.80 (0.62-1.02), and patients with ST-elevation shock without risk of hypoxic brain injury 0.77 (0.61-0.97; the stored abstract is truncated after this point). This establishes the effect in the eligible stratum and gives no randomised estimate for the strata proposed here.
- PMID 40737493 (European Heart Journal Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes, 4 November 2025): network meta-analysis of 18 randomised trials (n=1907); Impella reduced 6-12 month mortality versus standard therapy (RR 0.81, P < 0.05) with more renal replacement therapy, limb complications and major bleeding.
- PMID 39551167 (JACC, 18 February 2025): exploratory age analysis of DanGer Shock; odds ratio 0.45 (95% CI 0.28-0.73) under 77 years and 1.52 (0.57-4.08) at 77 or over, P for interaction 0.028. The eldest quartile had median age 81 years (range 77 to 92) and 180-day mortality of 73%.
- PMID 41222528 (JACC, 17 February 2026): prespecified sex analysis, 74 women and 281 men; HR 1.01 (95% CI 0.58-1.79) in women and 0.66 (0.47-0.93) in men, P for interaction 0.18, described by the authors as hypothesis generating. An accompanying registry of excluded patients had 495 patients.
- PMID 39462276 (Circulation, 17 December 2024): prespecified renal analysis; renal replacement therapy 42% versus 27%, subdistribution hazard 1.67 (1.18-2.35), with lower mortality in the pump arm irrespective of kidney injury (P for interaction 0.84).
- PMID 41078250 (European Heart Journal, 24 January 2026): J-PVAD registry stratified by DanGer Shock eligibility, outcomes by stratum, no non-pump comparator.
- PMID 41946200 (JACC Advances, 1 May 2026): eligibility overlap of landmark shock trials in the CS Working Group registry, with no effect estimate.
- PMID 39387763 (JACC, 17 December 2024): "Using Selection Criteria From the DanGer Shock Trial in a Contemporary Cohort With Cardiogenic Shock". The store holds no abstract for this record, so its content is not characterised here.
- PMID 37342056 (JAMA Cardiology, 1 August 2023): Medicare claims comparison of 23,478 patients; the pump was associated with higher risk-adjusted 30-day mortality (risk difference 14.9%, 95% CI 12.9%-17.0%), and the authors noted possible confounding by illness severity not available in the data. This is the nearest precedent for the proposed comparison and shows how registry estimates of the pump can point away from the trial.
- PMID 41966345 (Journal of Heart and Lung Transplantation, 1 August 2026): 976 pump-treated patients, AMI-CS versus non-AMI-CS, 180-day mortality 32.9% versus 33.3% after matching (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.65-1.21); no non-pump comparator.
- PMID 42403523 (Annals of Intensive Care, published 24 June 2026 online): age quartiles among 1043 pump-supported patients; adjusted subdistribution hazard ratio for 30-day death 1.87 (1.34-2.62) in the oldest quartile versus the youngest; no non-pump comparator.

## 4. What is under way

All fields from the registry entries in data/trials.

- NCT07762560 (DOUBLE-SHOCK): not yet recruiting, estimated n=780, factorial design of routine microaxial pump versus no routine pump and of pulmonary artery catheter monitoring; primary outcome all-cause death at 180 days; primary completion 2030-10. The adversarial report states that it includes NSTEMI shock and excludes age over 80 and unwitnessed or prolonged out-of-hospital arrest; those eligibility details are not recorded in the store fields and should be confirmed from the registry entry. It would therefore address NSTEMI shock but, on that reading, not the strata proposed here.
- NCT05366452 (ULYSS): recruiting, estimated n=204, early Impella CP versus conventional therapy in ACS-related cardiogenic shock; primary outcomes include number of deaths from all causes at 1 month; primary completion 2026-06 (date passed, status still recruiting). It is underpowered for stratum-level effects but may report within 24 months.
- NCT06886113 (Corvad AMICS): not yet recruiting, n=269, a different device in STEMI shock, primary outcome death up to 6 months, primary completion 2026-08-31.
- NCT07817472 (PRIME-SHOCK): not yet recruiting, n=126, timing of the pump (routine pre-PCI versus selective post-PCI), primary completion 2029-08-31; not a pump versus no-pump comparison.
- NCT01633502 (DanShock): completed; reported as PMID 38587239.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: population excluded from trials; transportability.

- G1 Consequence, pass: pump use in shock is a high-mortality decision made routinely outside trial criteria (PMID 41078250, PMID 41946200).
- G2 Equipoise, pass: judged on trial evidence only. One trial exists, and it gives no randomised data for the strata proposed; the oldest-quartile result is a non-significant exploratory subgroup (PMID 39551167). Guideline position unverified.
- G3 Not about to be answered, pass with partial competitors: DOUBLE-SHOCK (n=780, primary completion 2030-10) lies beyond 24 months, and ULYSS (n=204, primary completion 2026-06 on the registry) may report earlier but is underpowered for strata. The Lancet individual patient data meta-analysis (PMID 39236726) covers early unselected support and the eligible stratum, not these strata.
- G4 Credible design, pass conditional: a benchmarked emulation is citable by a guideline committee only if it reproduces the randomised estimate; a plain registry comparison would not be, because of confounding by indication (PMID 37342056).
- G5 Not incremental, at risk: the register line records a pass, but the adversarial report marks it at risk, because a benchmarking-only version would add little to PMID 41078250 and PMID 39387763. The pass depends on delivering an effect estimate against a non-pump comparator in the non-eligible strata.

## 6. Design

Primary design: target trial emulation with a benchmarking step, in pooled registry data. The adversarial report recommends international registries or a confirmed UK linkage; the primary design therefore specifies UK data (BCIS, ICNARC, Harefield) as the base and an international consortium as the route to sufficient numbers.

Target trial specification.

- Eligibility: adults with AMI-CS (STEMI or NSTEMI) undergoing coronary angiography with a revascularisation decision. Calibration cohort: patients meeting the DanGer-type criteria used in PMID 41078250 (lactate 2.5 mmol/L or above, systolic pressure below 100 mmHg or catecholamine use, left ventricular ejection fraction below 45%, shock onset to first pump support 24 h or less) and not comatose after arrest. Extension cohorts: age over 80; comatose or unwitnessed out-of-hospital arrest; mixed shock. Reason: lactate and blood pressure thresholds were the most restrictive criteria (PMID 41946200), so they must be recorded to define the strata at all.
- Treatment strategies: pump started within a grace period after time zero versus no pump at any point in the grace period; the length of the grace period is set from timing of insertion in the data and pre-specified. Reason: pump insertion is decided around angiography, so immortal time must be handled by design.
- Time zero: the point at which eligibility is met and the revascularisation decision is made. Reason: this aligns exposure assignment with eligibility.
- Outcomes: all-cause mortality at 30 and 180 days (primary); severe bleeding and renal replacement therapy (secondary, with the caveat that registries ascertain them poorly).
- Confounders: age, sex, lactate, vasopressor dose, pH, SCAI stage, arrest characteristics and neurological status, left ventricular function, culprit vessel, renal function, comorbidity, centre and year.
- Estimand: per-protocol effect of the pump strategy versus no pump on 180-day risk difference and hazard ratio, with the clone-censor-weight approach and inverse probability of censoring weights. Reason: the per-protocol estimate is the contrast the randomised trial targeted.
- Analysis: Cox and pooled logistic models on weighted clones with bootstrap confidence intervals, competing risks sensitivity analyses for renal replacement therapy, negative-control outcomes, quantitative bias analysis for unmeasured severity (E-value), and a centre-level instrumental variable sensitivity analysis. Reason: unmeasured severity was the central problem in the Medicare analysis (PMID 37342056).

Benchmarking step and decision rule (stated before any extension). The emulation is run first in the calibration cohort. If the weighted hazard ratio's 95% confidence interval includes the DanGer Shock estimate of 0.74 (PMID 38587239) and the point estimate lies on the same side of 1, the extension to non-eligible strata proceeds; if it does not reproduce the trial, the extension is not reported as an effect estimate and the paper becomes a documented failure of transportability of method, which is itself informative. Reason: this is the only route by which a guideline committee could cite the extension.

Power. Inputs: DanGer Shock hazard ratio 0.74 (PMID 38587239); alpha 0.05 two-sided; power 80%; event proportion 185 of 355 deaths (52.1%; derived from 82 of 179 and 103 of 176, PMID 38587239). Schoenfeld events required at 1:1 allocation = 4 x (1.960 + 0.842)^2 / (ln 0.74)^2 = 4 x 7.85 / 0.0907 = about 346 events, which is about 664 patients at 52.1% mortality. At 1:3 pump to no-pump allocation the formula d = (z_a + z_b)^2 / (p(1-p) (ln HR)^2) gives about 462 events, about 886 patients. These are derived figures for 80% power in each stratum, before any loss of effective sample size from weighting. An interaction test between strata needs roughly four times as many events, which a UK-only dataset is unlikely to supply; no figure for UK pump-treated AMI-CS patients was retrieved, so feasibility of each stratum is unverified. Stratum-level estimates will be reported as estimates with intervals and not as tests of interaction unless numbers allow.

Alternative design: a registry-based randomised trial of pump use in non-eligible strata, embedded in the planned UK shock platform the owner aims to build. Primary endpoint all-cause death at 180 days (the endpoint used in NCT01633502 and NCT07762560); sample size to be calculated for the stratum event rates once the emulation gives them; registry platform to be chosen with the platform partners (not yet decided). This is the route to a result that could change practice if the emulation shows signals in the over-80 or arrest strata.

Secondary component, to be added only if the pump-treated numbers are confirmed: a value-of-information analysis feeding the owner's existing microsimulation (see section 7), using emulation estimates as inputs, to size the trial.

## 7. Data and collaborators

- BCIS AMI cardiogenic shock registry, ICNARC cardiogenic shock registry and their linkage: through collaborators; variable availability (lactate, blood pressure, left ventricular function, arrest status, device exposure, 180-day mortality) is unconfirmed.
- Harefield microaxial flow pump registry: held by the owner; it contains exposed patients, so it cannot supply the unexposed comparator on its own and would be used for pump characteristics, timing and validation.
- International registries that have published eligibility or pump-treated cohorts and are potential partners: J-PVAD (PMID 41078250), CS Working Group registry (PMID 41946200), the European multicentre pump cohort (PMIDs 42403523, 41966345). Whether any holds a non-pump comparator with comparable variables is unconfirmed; the CS Working Group registry reports varied device use (PMID 41946200).
- DanGer Shock UK involvement through a Harefield collaborator, which supports benchmarking against the trial's individual data if access is agreed.
- Owner already has: Harefield pump registry, the collaborator routes to BCIS and ICNARC data, DanGer Shock UK contact, meta-analytic and registry-embedded trial methodology.

## 8. Result scenarios

- The emulation reproduces the trial in eligible patients and shows a mortality difference in one or more non-eligible strata. This would be publishable in the target journals as the first benchmarked estimate in populations excluded from the trial, and would support the registry-based randomised trial.
- The emulation reproduces the trial and shows no difference or harm in the over-80 or arrest strata. This would be publishable because it would inform a common decision with a documented negative signal in older patients (PMID 39551167).
- The emulation does not reproduce the trial. This would be publishable as a methodological caution, probably in a specialty journal, and would argue for randomisation; the question would then be recorded as not promotable on its own.
- Strata too small. The question would be reduced to the calibration exercise and parked, because that version would be incremental (G5).

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Confounding by indication: severity at the pump decision is the main unmeasured confounder (PMID 37342056). De-risk through the benchmarking decision rule, negative-control outcomes and quantitative bias analysis.
- Data adequacy: eligibility moment, lactate and left ventricular function are rarely recorded together in BCIS (a PCI registry) or ICNARC (an intensive care audit); linkage is an assumption. De-risk by confirming variable lists with collaborators first.
- Numbers: UK pump-treated AMI-CS counts are unverified and are likely to be small, so the over-80 and arrest strata may be underpowered; an international partner is probably needed for a parent-journal paper.
- Competing randomised data: DOUBLE-SHOCK (2030-10) and ULYSS (primary completion 2026-06) will supply some randomised evidence; the emulation should be timed to report before them and to be updated against them.
- Editor objection: the headline is observational in a field with a randomised result; the answer is that the paper targets populations the trial excluded and uses the trial as validation.
- Scoop risk: moderate. Emulation methods are already used in the field at specialty level (PMIDs 40340843, 42661221), and a commentary asks whether emulation is valid for these devices (PMID 42772539).
- Overlap with the owner's active projects: partial overlap with the patient-level Markov microsimulation of microaxial flow pump support in STEMI-related shock, which lacks effect estimates for non-eligible strata and could consume this question's output. No overlap with the temporary right ventricular assist device SRMA; adjacent to, but separate from, the SRMA of shock care organisation and the AKI and kidney replacement therapy in cardiogenic shock SRMA.
- Feasibility for the owner: moderate to good for UK data access and for the method; weak for stratum-level numbers until confirmed. Feasibility carries little weight in the ranking by design.

Gate record: G1 pass; G2 pass; G3 pass with partial competitors (DOUBLE-SHOCK, ULYSS); G4 pass conditional on benchmarking; G5 recorded as pass in the register and marked at risk by the adversarial report. Subspecialty-only: no.

Adversarial check, 24 October 2026: verdict survives with a narrower angle. The reviewer found that the pump versus standard care effect in eligible patients is established (PMIDs 38587239, 39236726), that age and sex are covered by randomised secondary analyses (PMIDs 39551167, 41222528), and that eligibility-stratified registry descriptions exist in a target journal (PMID 41078250). Objections: observational pump comparisons have pointed the opposite way from the trial (PMID 37342056); UK numbers may be too small; DOUBLE-SHOCK and ULYSS will supply some randomised data. Recommended changes, applied here: narrow to over-80, comatose or unwitnessed arrest and mixed shock; add international data; make benchmarking a pre-specified go or no-go step; reduce novelty by one point (applied); treat the benchmarking-only version as incremental. The objection that DOUBLE-SHOCK weakens the NSTEMI stratum is accepted, and NSTEMI shock is therefore not a headline stratum. The reviewer's search for UK-specific pump effectiveness work found none, which is a search result and not proof of absence.
