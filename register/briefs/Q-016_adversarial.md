# Adversarial review of Q-016

Question: target trial emulation of TEER versus continued medical therapy in secondary MR patients outside COAPT criteria. Reviewer worked from the local store (51,000-record JSONL store and data/trials/current.jsonl.gz) and live PubMed E-utilities (reachable; searches for target trial emulation of TEER returned no hits). Every PMID and NCT below was read in this session from the store. Web search was not used.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 39217568, Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 10 December 2024: "Percutaneous Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair for Functional Mitral Regurgitation in Heart Failure: A Meta-Analysis of 3 Randomized Controlled Trials". The store holds no abstract, so only the title is confirmed. It pools MITRA-FR, COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 and covers the trial-eligible population only. Overlap: it closes the "is there a randomised effect at all" question; it does not address non-COAPT patients.
- PMID 41549691, EuroIntervention, 19 January 2026: reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis of MITRA-FR, COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 (1,422 patients; 703 TEER plus GDMT, 719 GDMT alone). Death or first HF hospitalisation at 24 months: one-stage HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.56-0.92), two-stage HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.51-1.00), I2 80.3%. Overlap: pooled randomised evidence exists, with high heterogeneity, which is the heterogeneity Q-016 would try to explain by phenotype, but only for trial-like patients.
- PMID 41868764, Journal of the Society for Cardiovascular Angiography and Interventions, 1 March 2026: updated meta-analysis of 3 RCTs and 5 propensity-matched studies (n = 2588). Specialty tier. Reported 12-month all-cause mortality RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58-0.95). Overlap: mixes observational and randomised data, not restricted to non-COAPT patients.
- PMID 42321618, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, 19 June 2026: reconstructed-survival meta-analysis of 16 studies (n = 4,570) comparing TEER, surgery and medical therapy. Low-tier journal, confounded by study-level case-mix.
- PMID 42657844, JACC: Advances, 1 August 2026: Bayesian meta-analysis of TEER for secondary MR. No abstract in the store; title only.
- PMID 42233929, Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 6 October 2026: "Estimating the Effects of MTEER in U.S. Practice: A Transportability Analysis of the COAPT Trial" (614 COAPT patients, 15,275 TVT Registry patients, of whom 7,289 were COAPT-eligible). It estimated 2-year absolute risk reductions of 17.0% for HF hospitalisation and 15.4% for all-cause death in the trial-eligible TVT population, and reported a similar HF hospitalisation effect in the broader "treatment-candidate" population regardless of eligibility (P for difference 0.90). This is the closest scoop in a target journal: it addresses transportability to patients treated outside trial criteria, using COAPT data and an assumption-based method. It does not use an untreated real-world comparator.
- PMID 40629531, European Heart Journal, 13 March 2026: OCEAN-Mitral (TEER, n = 441) versus REVEAL-AFMR (medical therapy, n = 640) in atrial functional MR, mean age 80.1 years. Overlap-weighted primary composite HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.43-0.99). This is an observational TEER-versus-medical-therapy comparison in a population largely outside COAPT (atrial MR) in a target journal. It is a partial scoop of the design, not of the ventricular non-COAPT population.
- PMID 40713981, JACC: Heart Failure, 1 September 2025: EXPANDed studies, 81 non-COAPT-like patients (LVEF <20%, LVESD >70 mm, or PASP >70 mmHg), single-arm, 1-year outcomes. No comparator, so it does not answer the comparative question. It shows the population is small within device registries.
- PMID 38923261, Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, 1 August 2024: single-centre, 394 patients, 14% COAPT-eligible; weighted 4-year survival higher with MitraClip than medical therapy among non-COAPT patients (91.5% versus 71.8%). Specialty tier and small, but it states the research question directly.
- Subgroup evidence on the proposed exclusion criteria: PMID 42600559 (JACC: Advances, 1 September 2026, OCEAN-Mitral, LVEF <20%, n = 103, no medical-therapy arm), PMID 41041987 (European Journal of Clinical Investigation, 1 January 2026, pulmonary hypertension, n = 144, no comparator), PMID 41878851 (JAHA, 7 April 2026, LVAD-eligible candidates, n = 129, no comparator). All single-arm.
- Randomised evidence in the broad population: PMID 39216092 (NEJM, 14 November 2024, RESHAPE-HF2, n = 505, moderate-to-severe MR, rate ratio 0.64, 95% CI 0.48-0.85 for first or recurrent HF hospitalisation or CV death at 24 months). PMID 36876756 (NEJM, 1 June 2023, COAPT five-year follow-up). PMID 30145927 and PMID 30280640 (NEJM, 13 December 2018, MITRA-FR and COAPT).
- Commentary showing the field is contested on case-mix: PMID 39320294 (JACC, 10 December 2024, volume-based framework reconciling COAPT, MITRA-FR and RESHAPE-HF2).

No published target trial emulation of TEER versus medical therapy in secondary MR was found in the store or in two PubMed searches (the second returned an unrelated narrative review, PMID 42652758).

- NCT03891823 (EVOLVE-MR) terminated with 9 patients; NCT03705312 (EVOLVE-MR, earlier registration) status unknown.

Verdict on timing: no adequately powered RCT of TEER versus medical therapy in advanced LV dilatation, severe PH or RV dysfunction is expected within 24 months; completion dates are 2028 to 2031 and the trials enrol broader populations.

## Editor objections

1. The COAPT transportability analysis (PMID 42233929) was published in JACC this month and already frames the "do trial results apply beyond criteria" question. An editor will ask what Q-016 adds beyond an untreated real-world comparator.
2. Novelty is moderate. OCEAN-Mitral and REVEAL-AFMR (PMID 40629531) already placed an observational TEER-versus-medical-therapy comparison in the European Heart Journal. A national-data emulation would need to be clearly larger or cleaner, with explicit handling of the non-COAPT strata, to justify a main-journal slot.
3. Population is a negative-selection group. Patients with severe PH, RV dysfunction or very large LV are those least likely to benefit (PMID 41878851 reports limited efficacy and no reverse remodelling with marked LV enlargement; PMID 41041987 reports worst outcomes in combined post- and pre-capillary PH). A null or harmful finding in an observational design would be dismissed as confounding by indication, and a positive one as residual confounding.
4. RESHAPE-HF2 (PMID 39216092) broadened the randomised evidence, so the "outside the trials" gap is smaller than before for moderate MR and milder phenotypes. The remaining gap is narrow (extreme phenotypes) and clinically fewer patients.
5. Health status as an outcome is rarely available in national registries and cannot be emulated credibly from administrative data.
6. A pooled outcome by exclusion criterion would produce small strata and wide intervals; the paper would likely be treated as hypothesis-generating, which fits a subspecialty-tier outlet.

## Design objections

1. Time zero and eligibility. "First echocardiogram meeting criteria" requires structured, quantified echo data (EROA or regurgitant volume, LVEDV, RV function, PASP) in a national linkage. Few national datasets hold quantified echo with linked procedure and HF-admission data across all centres. A Swedish or Danish echo-linked register, or a national dataset with structured reports, would be needed. This must be confirmed before the design is called credible.
2. Medical therapy as comparator. Whether GDMT was optimised, and whether patients were referred for TEER only after failing GDMT, cannot be assumed. The COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 effect depends on GDMT optimisation; registry usual care is not the same strategy. The target-trial protocol must specify the GDMT requirement as part of eligibility and as a post-baseline covariate.
3. Selection of patients for TEER within the non-COAPT group is driven by unmeasured factors (frailty, heart-team judgment, RV function details, willingness, anatomy suitability). Anatomic suitability for the device is not recorded for untreated patients, so positivity is questionable. Immortal time bias can be handled by clone-censor-weight, as proposed, but confounding by indication cannot be removed by weighting on recorded covariates.
4. A 90-day grace period is long for advanced HF patients with high early mortality, and the clone-censor-weight method needs strong time-varying covariates (NT-proBNP, NYHA, worsening HF events) that national datasets seldom hold.
5. Competing risks. Heart transplant, LVAD, and non-cardiac death are strong competing events in this group (PMID 41065256 reports 2-year cumulative incidence of 19% cardiac and 12% non-cardiac death in treated patients) and the estimand must address them.
6. The proposed outcomes include health status, which is not in national linked data; this should be dropped or restricted to a registry with PROMs.
7. Negative-control outcomes and a benchmarking step (emulating COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 inside the same data and checking that the trial effect is recovered) would be needed; this is feasible and strengthens credibility, and it is the strongest point in favour of the design.

## Verdict

Verdict: survives with a narrower angle

The question is not answered by any retrieved trial or synthesis. No randomised trial of the exact comparison is due before 2028, and no published target trial emulation was found. However, PMID 42233929 (JACC, 6 October 2026) and PMID 40629531 (European Heart Journal, 13 March 2026) have each taken a large share of the "beyond trial criteria" ground in target journals, and several of the proposed excluded phenotypes have only single-arm data, which is why a comparative analysis would still be new. The narrower angle is: restrict the emulation to the specific excluded phenotypes (LVEF <20%, advanced LV dilatation, severe PH, RV dysfunction) in a dataset with quantified echo, benchmark first by recovering the COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 results, and treat LVAD, transplant and non-cardiac death as competing events. Drop health status from the outcomes. The proposal should be reframed as complementing PMID 42233929 (benchmark plus extension to the excluded strata), not repeating it.

Suggested changes:
- G3: keep as pass for the exact PICO, but record that NCT07758023 (N 520, completion 2030-09-30), NCT07197021 (N 540, 2031-12-31) and NCT07414225 (N 400, 2030-02-25) will report after the 24-month window and enrol broader or atrial-MR populations; also record the 2024 to 2026 meta-analyses (PMID 39217568, PMID 41549691) as pooling randomised evidence only for trial-like patients, not overlapping the question.
- G5: mark as borderline, not clean. The design is not a repeat meta-analysis or single-centre series, but it risks being judged an incremental extension of PMID 42233929 and PMID 40629531 unless the benchmarking step and the narrow phenotype focus are explicit.
- Novelty: reduce by one point (from the likely 4 to 3).
- Equipoise: keep, or reduce by at most one point; RESHAPE-HF2 (PMID 39216092) shifted equipoise towards benefit for moderate-to-severe MR, but equipoise remains genuine for the extreme phenotypes. Suggested score 3 to 4, with the justification limited to the excluded strata.
- Subspecialty-journal risk: record it explicitly; the main-journal case depends on a large, well-benchmarked national dataset.
