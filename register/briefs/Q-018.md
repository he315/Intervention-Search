# Q-018: In asymptomatic adults with severe degenerative MR and preserved LV function, does the expected value of further randomised evidence on early surgical repair justify completing, enlarging or supplementing REVERSE-MR?

This brief is built on the narrowed question. The register title asked for a value-of-information analysis of early surgical or transcatheter intervention. The independent check (register/briefs/Q-018_adversarial.md, 24 October 2026) found it unscooped but narrowed it in four ways, all adopted here: the intervention is early surgical repair, with transcatheter repair only an exploratory scenario because it has no evidence in asymptomatic patients; the analysis is framed around whether the value of information justifies completing, enlarging or supplementing REVERSE-MR (NCT03389542); the observational inputs are bias-adjusted, with repair-rate thresholds and the watchful-waiting crossover rate built in; and the model is paired with a reconstructed-IPD synthesis of the observational cohorts, because a model alone is unlikely to reach a parent journal. Guideline positions have not been extracted for this register, so no class or level is stated.

## 1. Question in PICO form

- Population: adults with asymptomatic severe degenerative (primary) MR, preserved LV ejection fraction and no current class-defining trigger for surgery, managed in centres able to offer repair.
- Intervention: early surgical mitral repair, with the probability of successful repair modelled explicitly rather than assumed.
- Comparator: watchful waiting with structured surveillance and surgery when symptoms or LV, atrial or pulmonary triggers appear.
- Outcomes: all-cause and cardiovascular death, heart failure hospitalisation, reoperation, stroke, quality-adjusted life years and costs; for the research decision, the expected value of perfect, partial perfect and sample information.
- Timing: lifetime horizon for the clinical decision; for the research decision, the period over which a trial result would inform practice.

## 2. Why it matters now

The clinical decision is whether to refer an asymptomatic patient for repair now or to watch and wait; heart teams make it with the patient, and it recurs at every surveillance visit. The research decision is whether to complete, enlarge or add to the only randomised trial; funders, trial committees and guideline committees make it.

Population size. The store holds no national prevalence or incidence of asymptomatic severe degenerative MR. The nearest figures are cohort counts: a German claims analysis identified 56 577 patients with asymptomatic MR of any severity in one insurer (PMID 41996614), and the MIDA-Q registry enrolled 3274 men and 2331 women with at least mild degenerative MR (PMID 42813276). A UK estimate is a required model input.

Documented practice variation. In 2135 patients operated on for degenerative MR across 40 Canadian and French centres in 2019, only 17% were considered asymptomatic and early intervention was performed in 4%; the repair rate was 80%, and it was 68%, 77% and 84% in low-, intermediate- and high-volume centres (PMID 42092496). Asymptomatic women were referred for surgery less often than men with similar ERO (adjusted HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.69 to 0.94; PMID 42813276). In one US health system, 154 of 246 patients (62.6%) with asymptomatic severe primary MR received surveillance at the recommended interval, and adherent patients had surgery earlier and more often (PMID 34325105). In the Dutch AMR registry, 51% of the active surveillance group went on to facilitated surgery after ESC guideline triggers over a median of 5.1 years (PMID 41661461).

Why now. Two observational analyses of the past 15 months favour early surgery (PMID 40799133; PMID 42832200), a summary of the 2025 ESC/EACTS valvular guideline reports a changed recommendation for asymptomatic primary MR (PMID 42064867; class and level not extracted), and REVERSE-MR will not complete until 10 April 2033 (NCT03389542). The one dedicated model (PMID 21945713) found early surgery superior only above a repair rate of 84%, the rate in high-volume MITRACURE centres and above that in low- and intermediate-volume centres (PMID 42092496). Whether waiting eight years for a 424-patient trial is the right research investment can be answered now.

## 3. What is known

- PMID 40799133 (Circulation, 2025; NCT01703806): prospective single-centre cohort of 1063 patients (545 early surgery, 518 conventional), median follow-up 12 years, no operative deaths, repair in 97%. Cardiovascular death 8 (1.5%) against 54 (10.4%), HR 0.17 (95% CI 0.07 to 0.40); all-cause death 74 (13.6%) against 116 (22.4%), HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.52 to 0.99).
- PMID 42832200 (JAMA Network Open, 2026): clone-censor-weight emulation in three Korean hospitals, 802 patients (344 early surgery within a 6-month grace period, 458 watchful waiting), 27 and 103 deaths over a median of 7.4 years. Five-year restricted mean survival gain 66 days (95% CI 19 to 129); all-cause mortality HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.41 to 0.94).
- PMID 41661461 (Netherlands Heart Journal, 2026): Dutch AMR registry, 99 patients (71 surveillance, 28 early surgery), median 5.1 years; 51% of the surveillance group had facilitated surgery and 5 (7%) died. Small, but it gives the crossover rate.
- PMID 42525396 (JAMA Cardiology, 2026): 449 asymptomatic patients, median follow-up 26 years; five-year survival under medical therapy 88%, 72% and 55% for ERO below 20, 20 to 39 and at least 40 mm2, and ten-year incidence of surgery 18%, 64% and 88%.
- PMID 30678755 (JACC, 2019): MIDA registry, 2425 patients with flail leaflets; surgery was associated with better survival (adjusted HR 0.26, 95% CI 0.23 to 0.30), a comparison confounded by selection.
- PMID 36702625 (European Heart Journal, 2023): 2276 operated patients; the number of secondary outcome determinants was associated with post-operative mortality (adjusted HRs 1.56, 1.78 and 2.58).
- PMID 33419544 and PMID 42849598 (single high-volume centres): zero 30-day mortality and 100% freedom from reoperation at 10 years in 470 class I patients; five-year recurrent MR of at least 2+ in 20% after early surgery for bileaflet prolapse.
- PMID 42092496 (MITRACURE, 2026): in-hospital mortality 1.4% after repair and 6.2% after replacement.
- PMID 21945713 (International Journal of Cardiology, 2013): cohort Markov model from a US provider perspective. With 90% repair, early surgery gave 11.2 against 10.7 QALYs at $54,659 per QALY; at a 50% repair rate early surgery was worse, the threshold repair rate was 84%, and early surgery was more effective when watchful-waiting mortality exceeded 3.5% a year. The abstract reports no expected value of information.
- PMID 34767705 (NEJM, 2022): randomised trial in 401 patients having degenerative MR surgery; a source of randomised post-operative event rates, not the timing comparison.
- PMID 28663365 (Heart, 2018): meta-analysis of edge-to-edge repair by aetiology, 2615 patients; 1-year re-intervention 10% in degenerative MR. The only stored input for a transcatheter scenario, and not in asymptomatic patients.

No systematic review, meta-analysis or IPD synthesis comparing early surgery with watchful waiting, and no value-of-information analysis of this decision, was found in the store or by the independent check. The register field for the latest review is therefore empty.

## 4. What is under way

- NCT03389542 (REVERSE-MR): randomised, open-label, France, sponsor CHU Amiens, recruiting, estimated enrolment 424, primary completion 10 April 2033, last registry update 13 May 2026. Primary outcome as registered: death and occurrence of adverse cardiovascular events during 5 years. Surgery against clinical interview and echocardiography. The only randomised comparison of the same strategies.
- NCT01708265 (Dutch AMR randomised trial): terminated with 12 participants, primary completion February 2021. Evidence that recruitment to this comparison is hard.
- NCT03975998 (Dutch-AMR registry trial): estimated 500, primary completion October 2026 as registered, completion October 2031; primary outcome a time-to-event composite of cardiovascular mortality, congestive heart failure, hospitalisation and class I or IIa indication for surgery at 5 years. The registry entry was last updated on 6 June 2019, so its status is not current and no results are in the store. It is the nearest source of prospective crossover data.
- NCT07366723 (INTEGRATE): randomised, 260, primary completion 1 June 2029; tests cardiac magnetic resonance in surgical decisions in primary MR with a remodelling primary outcome. Relevant to risk stratification, not to the timing comparison on hard outcomes.
- Transcatheter trials relevant only to the scenario: NCT05051033 (PRIMARY, 450, 30 June 2029), NCT04198870 (REPAIR MR, 500, December 2027) and NCT03271762 (MITRA-HR, 330, December 2026). None targets asymptomatic patients on its registry fields.
- NCT07709208 (MARCER), listed in the register line, concerns exercise-induced secondary MR and does not bear on this population.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: underpowered trials. The only randomised trial is small for hard outcomes and reports in 2033; the observational evidence is consistent in direction but open to selection and immortal-time bias.

- G1 Consequence: pass. The decision is common and is judged on death and heart failure.
- G2 Equipoise: pass. The randomised answer is pending and the observational analyses are associations. Guideline wording was not extracted, so equipoise is judged on trial evidence only.
- G3 Not about to be answered: pass. REVERSE-MR completes in April 2033, beyond 24 months, and no meta-analysis of the comparison in a target- or general-tier journal was found in the past 24 months. The analyses of 2025 and 2026 change the observational base materially, adding 1865 patients (1063 plus 802) and 320 deaths (74 plus 116 in PMID 40799133; 27 plus 103 in PMID 42832200).
- G4 Credible design: conditional pass. A model alone is unlikely to be cited for the clinical decision, but it can be cited for the research decision, and the paired synthesis gives the first pooled, bias-adjusted estimate.
- G5 Not incremental: conditional pass, provided the patient-level structure, bias-adjusted inputs and value-of-information outputs are the stated advance over PMID 21945713.

## 6. Design

### Primary: bias-adjusted evidence synthesis feeding a patient-level microsimulation with value-of-information analysis

Component A, reconstructed-IPD synthesis of the observational comparisons.

- Eligibility: cohorts and emulations comparing early surgery with watchful waiting in asymptomatic severe primary MR with preserved LV function, reporting Kaplan-Meier curves for death. Candidates in the store are PMID 40799133, PMID 42832200 and PMID 41661461; whether each prints usable curves must be checked in the full texts.
- Reconstruction: digitise published Kaplan-Meier curves and reconstruct time-to-event data with the Guyot algorithm, using reported numbers at risk and total events as constraints, and check each reconstruction against the reported hazard ratio and event counts.
- Effect measure: hazard ratio for all-cause death as the primary measure, with restricted mean survival time difference at 5 and 10 years as a measure robust to non-proportional hazards; cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalisation as secondary measures where reported.
- Model: random-effects meta-analysis with REML estimation of tau-squared and Hartung-Knapp adjustment; with so few studies, a Bayesian random-effects model with a weakly informative prior on tau is reported alongside. Time-varying effects are examined with flexible parametric models on the pooled reconstructed data, because operative risk comes first and benefit accrues late.
- Zero events: not expected for death; for a sparse secondary outcome, a treatment-arm continuity correction in sensitivity analysis.
- Bias adjustment: each study's log hazard ratio is shifted and widened by prespecified elicited distributions for confounding by indication, immortal time and centre selection, so the model carries this uncertainty. Unadjusted and adjusted estimates are both reported.

Component B, patient-level Markov microsimulation.

- Structure: patients drawn from a baseline distribution of age, sex, ERO and secondary outcome determinants move through surveillance, trigger reached, successful repair, replacement or failed repair, recurrent MR or reoperation, heart failure, stroke and death. Crossover in watchful waiting is calibrated to 51% over a median of 5.1 years (PMID 41661461) and to surgery incidence by ERO (PMID 42525396).
- Inputs from the store: natural history by ERO (PMID 42525396); post-operative risk by phenotype (PMID 36702625); operative mortality 1.4% after repair and 6.2% after replacement, and repair rate 68% to 84% by centre volume (PMID 42092496); durability (PMID 42849598, PMID 33419544); the relative effect from component A. Costs and utilities are not in the store and will come from NHS reference costs and published EQ-5D values.
- Perspective: UK NHS and personal social services. Horizon: lifetime. Discount rate: 3.5% a year for costs and outcomes, the UK convention, as an analytic choice rather than a stored figure.
- Repair rate as a structural parameter: the model will report the threshold repair rate and the threshold operative mortality at which early surgery stops being preferred, updating the 84% threshold of PMID 21945713 with bias-adjusted inputs.
- Uncertainty: probabilistic sensitivity analysis over all parameters, with the bias-adjusted posterior for the treatment effect.
- Value of information: expected value of perfect information per patient and for the UK population over a stated horizon; partial perfect information for the effects on death and heart failure, repair rate, durability and crossover; sample information for the REVERSE-MR design at 424 patients and larger, and for a registry-embedded trial, by nonparametric regression; expected net benefit of sampling against trial cost, giving the optimal size.
- Transcatheter scenario: TEER in the early arm, with re-intervention from PMID 28663365 and its limits stated.

### What the trial in hand can resolve: inputs shown

The registry gives REVERSE-MR as 424 participants with a 5-year composite primary outcome (NCT03389542). No 5-year composite event rate for watchful waiting is in the store, so the following check uses death alone, which understates the event rate of the registered composite and therefore overstates the detectable effect. Formula: normal approximation for two proportions, two-sided alpha 0.05, power 80%, 212 per arm, no loss to follow-up. Hazard ratios are applied to cumulative proportions as an approximation.

- Control proportion 7% (5 of 71 surveillance deaths over a median of 5.1 years, PMID 41661461): the smallest detectable intervention proportion is about 1.5%, a relative risk of about 0.22.
- Control proportion 10.4% (54 of 518 cardiovascular deaths over a median of 12 years, PMID 40799133): smallest detectable proportion about 3.5%, relative risk about 0.34.
- Control proportion 22.5% (103 of 458 deaths over a median of 7.4 years, derived from PMID 42832200): smallest detectable proportion about 12.2%, relative risk about 0.54.
- To detect the all-cause effect suggested by PMID 40799133 (HR 0.72) at those three control proportions would need about 2296, 1503 and 616 patients per arm; for HR 0.69 (PMID 42832200), about 1842, 1206 and 496 per arm.

On mortality alone, a 424-patient trial detects only effects much larger than the observational estimates. The registered composite will have a higher event rate; the EVSI analysis makes this formal.

### Alternative

A Bayesian synthesis of the cohorts feeding a design simulation for a registry-embedded randomised trial, without the cost-effectiveness layer. It is simpler but does not tell funders whether the trial is worth its cost.

### Why a weaker design will not do

A further single-centre cohort adds another association from a selected high-volume centre. A conventional meta-analysis of hazard ratios would pool differently biased designs without modelling time-varying effects or bias. A deterministic cohort model repeats PMID 21945713. None of these answers whether more randomised evidence is worth buying.

## 7. Data and collaborators

Needed: full texts and Kaplan-Meier curves of the cohorts; the REVERSE-MR protocol (not in the store); NHS costs and EQ-5D utilities; a UK population estimate. Collaborators who would strengthen the work: the investigators behind PMID 40799133 and PMID 42832200 (individual data would replace reconstruction), the MIDA-Q investigators behind PMID 36702625 and PMID 42813276 for natural-history and phenotype inputs, the Dutch-AMR group (NCT03975998, PMID 41661461) for prospective crossover data, and the REVERSE-MR steering committee (NCT03389542), whose interest in the analysis would decide whether it informs the trial.

Owner position, stated plainly. The owner delivers reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis from published Kaplan-Meier curves and patient-level Markov microsimulation with probabilistic sensitivity analysis, which are the two components of the primary design. No new data access is needed for a first version. Expected value of sample information goes beyond the probabilistic sensitivity analysis the owner lists and would benefit from a health economist experienced in it. Feasibility is high (score 5).

Overlap with active owner projects: none in population or question. The owner's state-of-the-art review of acute MR and cardiogenic shock concerns a different population. The Markov microsimulation of microaxial flow pump support in STEMI-related shock is a separate model whose code and validation approach could serve as a method template.

## 8. Result scenarios

- The optimal trial size is close to 424: the analysis supports completing REVERSE-MR as planned against pressure from the observational papers, the first quantified case for an ongoing valve-timing trial.
- The optimal size is much larger than 424: the analysis argues for enlarging REVERSE-MR or adding a registry-embedded trial, and sizes it. This is the scenario most likely to interest a parent journal, because it changes what funders do.
- Expected value of perfect information is low because the bias-adjusted effect is large: further trials add little under current uncertainty, a result publishable with its observational caveat.
- The decision turns on repair rate, with MITRACURE rates (PMID 42092496) on both sides of the threshold: a quantitative basis for restricting early surgery to high-repair-rate centres, whatever the trial shows.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Inputs are observational and from selected centres (97% repair in PMID 40799133). De-risk with the explicit bias model, by reporting results across repair rates, and by stating that the clinical conclusion is conditional on the inputs.
- Kaplan-Meier curves may be unavailable or unreadable in some papers. De-risk by contacting authors for summary data early and by keeping a hazard-ratio-only synthesis as a fallback.
- Costs, utilities and UK population size are not in the store. De-risk with wide distributions, per-patient values and a range of population sizes.
- Journal fit: the check judged the likely home to be below the four target parents unless the synthesis and the trial framing are strong. Engagement of the REVERSE-MR investigators would strengthen the case.
- Scoop risk: low to moderate. No value-of-information analysis or synthesis of this comparison was found; the Korean and Dutch groups and the MIDA-Q investigators are active, and a pooled observational analysis from one of them is the most likely competitor. The Dutch-AMR registry readout (NCT03975998) may appear soon and should be used as an input rather than treated as a threat.
