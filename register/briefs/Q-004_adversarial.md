# Adversarial review of Q-004: PCI versus CABG in patients aged 75 years or older, target trial emulation

Searches were run on 24 October 2026 against PubMed E-utilities, the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 and the local store. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session. Where only a title and journal were seen (no abstract), that is stated. None of the PMIDs below were checked against data/records, so any that are cited in a brief will need add-records.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 38102297, Scientific Reports, 15 December 2023 (Kirov et al.). Meta-analysis of 14 studies with 17,942 octogenarians with left main or multivessel disease, PCI versus CABG. Late mortality (over 5 years) was lower with CABG (HR 1.23, 95% CI 1.05-1.44 for PCI versus CABG as reported), with a pooled Kaplan-Meier HR of 1.08 (95% CI 1.02-1.41), a PCI advantage in the first 30 days (HR 0.72, 0.64-0.82) and a crossover after about 21.5 months. Same population and comparison as Q-004, but observational studies pooled, in a non-target journal. This is the closest direct overlap and shows the question has been synthesised, though not answered with a target-journal-grade design.
- PMID 34082905, J Am Coll Cardiol, 8 June 2021. Pre-specified age analysis of the SYNTAX Extended Survival study, 575 of 1,800 randomised patients aged over 70 years with three-vessel and/or left main disease. Ten-year mortality 44.1% (PCI) versus 41.1% (CABG), HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.84-1.40); five-year MACCE HR 1.18 (0.90-1.56); life expectancy difference 0.2 years favouring CABG (95% CI -0.4 to 0.7). Randomised evidence in the elderly, but one trial, a cut-off of 70 rather than 75, and an older-generation stent comparator (the abstract does not state the stent generation).
- PMID 34613579, Gen Thorac Cardiovasc Surg, 2022. CREDO-Kyoto registry, 527 patients aged 80 years or over with three-vessel or left main disease (CABG 151, PCI 376); adjusted all-cause death HR 0.61 (95% CI 0.43-0.86) for CABG relative to PCI. Small, single-registry, confounded.
- PMID 39067814, J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg, July 2025 (online 25 July 2024). De Filippo et al., meta-analysis of PCI versus CABG for left main disease according to age. The PubMed record returned no abstract text, so the findings were not read; the title shows the age-stratified left main comparison has been synthesised in a surgical journal.
- PMID 41879579, J Am Coll Cardiol, 28 April 2026. Updated meta-analysis of left main PCI versus CABG using reconstructed individual-participant survival data. The record has no abstract (it appears to be a short article), so its content and any age stratum were not verified.
- PMID 34793745, Lancet, 18 December 2021. IPD meta-analysis of SYNTAX, PRECOMBAT, NOBLE and EXCEL, 4,394 patients with left main disease. The abstract excerpt read did not reach the results, so I report design only. PMID 38465592 (Circulation, 23 April 2024) is a pooled analysis of the same four trials by diabetes status, and PMID 37256598 (JAMA Cardiology, 1 July 2023) by acute coronary syndrome status. Neither is age-specific on the title, but together they show that the trial-based left main IPD pool is already mined by subgroup in target journals and that an age split of it is the obvious next paper.
- PMID 37288564, Eur Heart J, 7 August 2023. SWEDEHEART left main registry, 11,137 patients (CABG 9,364; PCI 1,773, 2005 to 2015), IPW and instrumental variable analysis; IV mortality HR 1.5 (95% CI 1.1-2.0) favouring CABG. PMID 39601339, Eur Heart J, 7 February 2025, SWEDEHEART NSTEMI with multivessel disease, 57,097 patients, double-robust IV analysis, aOR for death with PCI 1.67 (95% CI 1.54-1.81), with the benefit of CABG said to diminish with shorter life expectancy and to be greatest under 70 years. These show that large national-registry PCI versus CABG analyses with causal-inference methods are already published in the target journal, but neither is restricted to age 75 years or over.
- PMID 41638385, Am Heart J, June 2026 (online 2 February 2026), High-Risk REVASC protocol. An emulated-trial design using Hospital Episode Statistics linked to ONS, multivessel revascularisation, CABG versus PCI, in a high-risk population defined by at least one of age over 75 years, female sex, ACS, heart failure, CKD, peripheral vascular disease or intermediate frailty; five-year outcomes; latent class analysis plus instrumental variable analysis, then matching to RCT IPD. Registration given as NCT05853536, which the registry lists as "In Silico Trials of Surgical Interventions", status UNKNOWN, estimated enrolment 13,977,257, estimated primary completion 1 August 2023. This is the most important competitor: a funded, published protocol using the same data idea, with age over 75 years as a named stratum. It is a protocol only, no results.
- Observational age-specific items seen on title only (not appraised): PMID 36576658 (Egypt Heart J, December 2022), PMID 34798427 (J Surg Res, February 2022), PMID 38988147 (Catheter Cardiovasc Interv, August 2024, left main distal bifurcation PCI in elderly people).

Searches found no target- or general-journal 2024 to 2026 paper that answers PCI versus CABG specifically in patients aged 75 years or over with a modern-DES, registry-based target trial emulation.

## Ongoing trials

No registered randomised trial restricted to patients aged 75 years or over comparing PCI with CABG was found in ClinicalTrials.gov (searches by intervention and condition with age, elderly, octogenarian and frail terms). Trials found that overlap partially:

- NCT05534698, "Coronary Artery Bypass Grafts or Percutaneous Coronary Intervention for Revascularization in Moderate to High Risk Patients With Ischemic Heart Disease and Reduced Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction", not yet recruiting, N 1,550 estimated, primary completion December 2032; minimum age 18, LVEF below 45%; primary outcome a composite of all-cause mortality, stroke, MI and hospitalisation for HF.
- NCT05532631 (PROVERB), recruiting, N 1,040 estimated, primary completion 16 July 2030; LVEF 35% or below, multivessel disease, minimum age 18.
- NCT06399705 (RECHARGE: Minorities) and NCT06399692 (RECHARGE: Women), recruiting, N 600 each, primary completion 15 March 2033; hierarchical composite of death and quality of life; adults, with no upper age cut-off seen. Not age-defined.
- NCT05831085, recruiting, N 1,500 estimated, primary completion 31 December 2029; diabetes-centred revascularisation strategy trial (title truncated in the listing), minimum age 20.
- NCT05761067 (STICH-3.0 consortium, N 2,800, primary completion 14 September 2029), NCT05427370 (STICH3C, N 754, April 2029) and NCT07269366 (STICH 3.0-NL, N 358, May 2034): PCI versus CABG in reduced LV function; not age-restricted.

None will answer the question for patients aged 75 years or over within 24 months, and none is powered for an age stratum on its own. Completed older-patient RCTs seen in the registry were medical-versus-invasive or PCI-versus-medical designs (NCT02086019, NCT02126202, NCT02198716), not PCI versus CABG. NCT03052036 (SENIOR-RITA, age 75 years or over, N 1,518, primary completion 31 March 2024) is an invasive versus conservative NSTEMI trial and is not a comparator for this question.

## Editor objections

- The central result is likely to be predictable. The pooled observational estimate (PMID 38102297) and the elderly stratum of the randomised SYNTAX extension (PMID 34082905) already point to a late survival advantage of CABG offset by early PCI benefit, with a small absolute difference in life expectancy. A new registry estimate in the same direction would be seen as confirmatory.
- Immortal time and selection are visible in the core population: patients "suitable for either strategy" at 75 years or over are a small, unusual subset (the registry papers show PCI patients are older and sicker). A registry has no record of the heart-team judgement of equal suitability, so the population the question names cannot be identified.
- Prior high-profile registry analyses in the same journal (PMIDs 37288564, 39601339) already apply instrumental variable methods; editors will ask what clone-censor-weight adds over them.
- Competition from the High-Risk REVASC emulation programme (PMID 41638385), which names age over 75 years explicitly and has a trials-unit and RCT-IPD linkage.
- Likely to be judged a subgroup paper of an established comparison; a subspecialty-journal fit (for example an interventional or geriatric title) is the realistic ceiling unless the design is strengthened.

## Design objections

- Clone-censor-weight is the right tool for strategy comparison with a grace period, but it is built for sustained strategies; PCI versus CABG is a one-off treatment choice with a short decision window (angiogram to procedure), so the method adds little unless a long waiting period for surgery is a real feature of the data. A plain target trial emulation with time zero at the decision, or an instrumental variable design, may be more appropriate and more defensible.
- Time zero at "heart-team decision or index angiogram" is only usable if the heart-team decision is recorded. Most national PCI and cardiac surgical audits record the procedure, not the decision, and not the unselected pool that was offered either option. Time zero at angiography requires angiography registry linkage to both treatment audits (SWEDEHEART provides this; many UK and US sources do not).
- Unmeasured confounding by frailty, cognition, anatomy (SYNTAX score), conduit suitability, and life expectancy is the main threat in this age band and is poorly captured in administrative or audit data. Negative control outcomes and E-values would be required but do not remove the problem.
- Competing risk of non-cardiac death is large at 75 years or over; the proposed five-year restricted mean survival handles this reasonably, but repeat revascularisation and MI need cause-specific or subdistribution treatment, and quality of life, the outcome older patients weigh most, is not captured by the registries.
- Positivity: in patients over 80 years, almost all receive PCI in some countries, leaving thin overlap for the weighted comparison.
- Sample size: national surgical audits will give only small numbers of CABG patients aged 75 years or over with left main or multivessel disease and a registry-recorded equal-suitability flag, so precision for the five-year differences may be limited.

## Verdict

Verdict: survives with a narrower angle

Reason: no 2024 to 2026 target-journal paper answers this question in this population with a registry trial emulation, and no registered RCT will do so, so it is not scooped. It is, however, partly covered: an observational meta-analysis in octogenarians (PMID 38102297), an age-stratified analysis of the SYNTAX randomised cohort (PMID 34082905), several left main IPD pools by subgroup in target journals, and a published, funded emulation protocol that names age over 75 years as a stratum (PMID 41638385). The strongest remaining angle is not another point estimate of mortality but one the above cannot supply: a causal estimate of life-expectancy and time-varying effects (early PCI advantage, late CABG advantage, crossover at about 21 months) by baseline frailty and life-expectancy strata in an unselected population with modern DES, with explicit positivity checks and negative controls, ideally in SWEDEHEART or an equivalent linked source, or a reconstructed-IPD synthesis of the randomised age strata combined with registry data to show transportability. Recommended changes: G3 stays a pass for RCTs (nothing age-specific reports within 24 months) but the note should record that NCT05853536/PMID 41638385 is a competing emulation and that PMID 41879579 is a 2026 target-journal left main IPD update that could be extended by age; G5 should be marked borderline pass, since a plain registry estimate would be incremental to PMID 38102297 and PMID 34082905, and the pass depends on the frailty and life-expectancy heterogeneity design; reduce Novelty by one point (from its present value) and keep Equipoise unchanged, because the randomised elderly stratum shows no significant mortality difference and the observational data disagree (so equipoise is real). Replace clone-censor-weight as the default with a standard emulation or instrumental variable design unless a documented waiting-time grace period exists in the data.
