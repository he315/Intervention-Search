# Q-028: In adults aged 45 years or older after major noncardiac surgery, does a defined protocolised response to surveillance-detected myocardial injury reduce major adverse cardiovascular events and death at 1 year, and is routine troponin surveillance worth testing at all?

This brief is built on the narrowed question. The register title asked whether routine surveillance with a protocolised response reduces cardiovascular events, to be tested by a stepped-wedge registry-based trial. The independent check (register/briefs/Q-028_adversarial.md, 24 October 2026) found the question unanswered but the proposed design weak, because the effect of surveillance depends wholly on a response that rests on one randomised trial, and because a stepped-wedge trial using hospital episode data would struggle with exposure capture, event capture and secular trends. The brief therefore describes a staged programme: a decision model with value-of-information analysis and a target trial emulation using staggered adoption of surveillance, to size the effect and define the response bundle, followed by a pragmatic randomised trial of the defined response in patients with detected injury. Guideline positions have not been extracted for this register, so no guideline class or level is stated.

## 1. Question in PICO form

- Population: adults aged 45 years or older having major inpatient noncardiac surgery.
- Intervention: routine postoperative high-sensitivity troponin surveillance on postoperative days 1 to 3, followed by a defined response bundle (for example anticoagulant, statin and cardiology review) for patients with myocardial injury.
- Comparator: troponin testing on clinical suspicion only (stage 1 and 2); usual care without the bundle among injury-positive patients (stage 3).
- Outcomes: major adverse cardiovascular events and all-cause death at 1 year.
- Timing: testing on postoperative days 1 to 3; follow-up 1 year.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision is whether hospitals should fund and adopt systematic postoperative troponin surveillance, and what they should do for patients in whom it finds injury. It is made by perioperative physicians, anaesthetists, surgeons and cardiologists.

Burden. In a prospective cohort of 2018 patients undergoing 2546 surgeries, perioperative myocardial injury followed 397 surgeries (16%), with crude 30-day mortality of 8.9% (95% CI 5.7 to 12.0) against 1.5% (95% CI 0.9 to 2.0) without injury, and 1-year mortality of 22.5% against 9.3% (PMID 29203498). In 7754 patients aged 45 to 98 years, injury or infarction occurred in 1016 (13.1%), at least one MACE in 684 (8.8%) and death in 818 (10.5%) within 1 year; outcomes differed by aetiology (PMID 36705050). In 21 842 participants, an absolute hsTnT change of 5 ng/L or more was associated with 30-day mortality, adjusted HR 4.69 (95% CI 3.52 to 6.25; PMID 28444280). In English audit data linked to hospital episodes, type 1 perioperative MI occurred in 2821 of 533 026 episodes (0.5%) and was associated with 30-day mortality, adjusted HR 1.34 (95% CI 1.21 to 1.49; PMID 42880712). All of these are observational associations. They show that injury marks a high-risk group; they do not show that finding it changes outcome.

Practice variation. Surveillance uptake is low where it is recommended but not funded: troponin surveillance was done in 13% and then 14% of patients before and after a national guideline at one centre (OR 1.08, 95% CI 1.02 to 1.15; PMID 37973786). Different guidance selects very different populations: Canadian and European approaches selected 78% and 79% of 2350 patients, an American approach fewer, and the Canadian and European approaches had sensitivity of 0.85 (95% CI 0.74 to 0.92) for postoperative MI with low specificity (PMID 38233301). Applying the 2022 ESC guidance at one Swiss hospital would have required testing in 1089 patients, 17% of 6413 surgeries (PMID 42022610). An editorial reports growing uptake of monitoring across Canadian centres while stating that management strategies and outcomes need further research (PMID 41882681). The American Heart Association statement states that surveillance is warranted in high-risk patients (PMID 34601955). Practice is therefore moving ahead of evidence on effect.

## 3. What is known

- PMID 28444280 (JAMA): prospective cohort of 21 842 patients; postoperative hsTnT thresholds and change were associated with 30-day mortality.
- PMID 29203498 (Circulation): prospective diagnostic study of routine screening; injury in 16% of surgeries, mostly without ischaemic symptoms, and associated with higher 30-day and 1-year mortality.
- PMID 36705050 (European Heart Journal): within an active surveillance and response programme, 13.1% had injury or infarction, and 1-year MACE and death differed starkly by aetiology.
- PMID 29900874 (Lancet, MANAGE): randomised, placebo-controlled trial of dabigatran 110 mg twice daily in 1754 patients within 35 days of MINS (NCT01661101). The primary efficacy composite occurred in 97 of 877 (11%) on dabigatran and 133 of 877 (15%) on placebo (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.93); the primary safety composite occurred in 29 (3%) and 31 (4%) (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.55 to 1.53). Study drug was permanently discontinued in 401 (46%) and 380 (43%). It is the only randomised support for a treatment in injury-positive patients, and the abstract reports no confirmatory trial.
- PMID 34601955 (Circulation, AHA scientific statement): sets out diagnostic criteria, surveillance strategies and treatment approaches. Its recommendations were not extracted.
- PMID 40884771 (Circulation, ivabradine, NCT05279651): 2101 patients; MINS in 178 of 1050 (17.0%) against 159 of 1051 (15.1%), relative risk 1.12 (95% CI 0.92 to 1.37); stopped at interim analysis for futility.
- PMID 40511609 (Circulation, remote ischaemic preconditioning): 1213 patients; myocardial injury in 38.0% against 37.4%, relative risk 1.02 (95% CI 0.88 to 1.18).
- PMID 37640035 (Lancet, COP-AF, NCT03310125): 3209 patients; MINS in 295 (18.3%) on colchicine and 325 (20.3%) on placebo, HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.76 to 1.05).
- PMID 37935833 (European Heart Journal, SPACE): 262 patients; myocardial injury odds ratio 0.77 (95% CI 0.45 to 1.31) for continuing against stopping RAS inhibitors.
- PMID 38213306 (Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica): retrospective before-and-after cohort of 558 patients (NCT05992961). Thirty-day mortality was 13.8% (95% CI 8.7% to 18.9%) in controls and 11.1% (95% CI 8.0% to 14.3%) with screening; absolute risk difference -2.7% (95% CI -8.7% to 3.3%). Of 81 patients with injury, no intervention or alteration was made in 67 (82.7%).
- PMID 37973786: single-centre before-and-after study of guideline publication in 36 386 patients; adjusted ORs 0.83 (95% CI 0.54 to 1.27) for MI and 0.88 (95% CI 0.64 to 1.19) for in-hospital mortality, with low uptake.
- PMID 31587565, PMID 30170853 and PMID 38011922: the High-STEACS stepped-wedge implementation work in suspected acute coronary syndrome, which reported no measured change in treatment or outcome with high-sensitivity troponin implementation (as summarised in the D7 landscape; not a perioperative population).

No systematic review, meta-analysis, trial emulation or randomised trial of surveillance with a response against suspicion-only testing was found by the independent check in the store, PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov. No review PMID is therefore given for the register field.

- NCT05812248 (PREVENGE-CB-2): observational, 20 000 estimated, primary completion 2027-11; primary outcome MACE within 30 days.

Their registered primary outcomes are mostly injury or composite events at 30 days, so none tests surveillance.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: no evidence. The observational cohorts show prognosis, and the randomised trials test prevention or a single treatment, none of which isolates the value of detection plus response.

- G1 Consequence: pass. Very common surgery, and the outcomes are 1-year death and MACE.
- G2 Equipoise: pass. Surveillance is promoted in some guidance (PMIDs 34601955, 42022610, 38233301) but its effect has not been tested, and the response rests on one trial with high discontinuation (PMID 29900874). Guideline wording was not extracted, so equipoise is judged on trial evidence only.
- G3 Not about to be answered: pass. No adequately powered randomised surveillance trial is expected within 24 months, and no meta-analysis of the comparison was found. The 2027 cohorts (NCT02573532, NCT05812248) do not answer the comparison.
- G4 Credible design: pass, as conditioned by the check. The original stepped-wedge registry form was judged plausible rather than demonstrably credible; the staged programme replaces it.
- G5 Not incremental: conditional pass. It is not a repeat meta-analysis, but it would be weak if reduced to another single-region before-and-after analysis, as PMID 38213306 already shows.

## 6. Design

### Primary: staged programme

Stage 1, decision model with value-of-information analysis.

- Structure: patient-level Markov microsimulation from surgery, with states for no injury, injury detected and treated, injury detected and untreated, injury undetected, non-fatal cardiovascular event, and death.
- Perspective: UK health service, with a secondary societal perspective if data allow. Horizon: lifetime, with a 1-year short-run output matching the trial endpoint. Discount rate: 3.5% a year for costs and outcomes, as a UK convention; this is an analytic choice, not a stored figure.
- Inputs from the store: injury prevalence of 13.1% and 1-year MACE of 8.8% and death of 10.5% (PMID 36705050); the share of surgeries needing testing under 2022 ESC guidance, 17% in one hospital (PMID 42022610), and the 78% to 79% selected by Canadian and European approaches (PMID 38233301); response effect from MANAGE, HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.93) with 46% discontinuation (PMID 29900874); the proportion of injury-positive patients receiving any intervention, 17.3% in the screening arm of PMID 38213306 (14 of 81, derived as 81 minus 67 from the abstract). Costs would come from national tariffs and are not in the store.
- Uncertainty: probabilistic sensitivity analysis; expected value of perfect information and of partial perfect information for the response effect, the uptake of the response and the detection yield; expected value of sample information to size stage 3.
- Purpose: show whether the uncertainty justifies a trial and which parameter drives it.

Stage 2, target trial emulation using staggered adoption of surveillance.

- Eligibility: adults aged 45 years or older having major inpatient noncardiac surgery, excluding those with troponin measured preoperatively for another indication only if that cannot be separated; exact exclusions to be fixed in the protocol.
- Treatment strategies: admission to a hospital that has adopted routine postoperative troponin surveillance with a documented response pathway, against a hospital not yet adopted, using the date of adoption in each hospital.
- Time zero: the date of surgery.
- Outcomes: 1-year all-cause death (primary, robust in linked national registers) and MACE (validated definition), with myocardial injury detection rate and treatment uptake as process outcomes.
- Confounders: age, sex, comorbidity, surgical specialty and urgency, calendar time, hospital and anaesthetic practice changes.
- Estimand: intention-to-treat effect of hospital-level policy, estimated with a staggered-adoption difference-in-differences or stacked design with hospital and calendar-time effects, and event-study plots to test pre-trends. Instrumental-variable analysis using adoption date is an alternative. The analysis would be reported as associations, not causal effects, unless the identification assumptions are defended.
- Data needs: linked postoperative laboratory results with timing, which hospital episode data alone do not hold (adversarial design objection 1).

Stage 3, pragmatic randomised trial of a defined response in injury-positive patients.

- Design: individually randomised, open-label with blinded outcome adjudication, in patients with surveillance-detected injury, response bundle against usual care, embedded in a registry or audit platform for outcome capture. Registry platform: ISRCTN or ClinicalTrials.gov, to be chosen with the sponsor.
- Primary endpoint: composite of MACE (as defined in stage 2) or all-cause death at 1 year.
- Sample size, with inputs shown: control event proportion 133/877 = 15.2% and intervention proportion 97/877 = 11.1% (MANAGE, PMID 29900874, primary efficacy composite, reported over follow-up of up to 2 years and so not identical to a 1-year composite). Two-sided alpha 0.05, power 80%. The normal-approximation formula for two proportions gives 1061 patients per group, 2122 in total, before loss to follow-up. If the true effect is half that seen in MANAGE (control 15.2%, intervention 13.1%), the requirement rises to 4523 per group, about 9046 in total. With injury in about 13% of the surveillance population (PMID 36705050), 2122 to 9046 injury-positive patients means screening roughly 16 000 to 70 000 patients. These figures assume individual randomisation and no clustering; any cluster design would need an intracluster correlation that the store does not provide and would inflate the numbers.

### Alternative

A cluster-randomised or stepped-wedge trial of surveillance itself, run only after stage 1 and 2 define the response and show that exposure and event capture are feasible. The check raised secular trends, contamination of control clusters and consent as main threats.

### Why a weaker design will not do

A before-and-after cohort cannot separate the effect of detection from the effect of treatment and was inconclusive when tried (PMID 38213306, PMID 37973786). A prognostic cohort cannot show benefit from acting on the finding.

## 7. Data and collaborators

Needed: linked postoperative troponin results with timing, surgical episode data, medication data and national death and MACE records; a response protocol drawn from MANAGE (PMID 29900874) and the AHA statement (PMID 34601955); cohort investigators from PMID 28444280, PMID 29203498 and PMID 36705050 for model inputs and for external validation; the audit and episode linkage used in PMID 42880712 shows that national audit linked to hospital episodes exists, but it holds infarction cases rather than troponin results.

Owner position, stated plainly. The owner delivers decision modelling with patient-level Markov microsimulation and probabilistic sensitivity analysis, registry-embedded trial methodology and trial design training, which cover stage 1 and the methods of stage 3. Stage 2 needs linked laboratory data that the owner does not hold. The owner's data (UK Biobank extract, Harefield cardiogenic shock and PCI cohorts, shock registries) do not contain perioperative troponin series. Stage 3 needs a multi-hospital perioperative collaboration. Feasibility is therefore low (score 2) but not disqualifying under the rule that large questions are listed with what they would take.

Overlap with active owner projects: none. The owner's current projects concern STEMI, cardiogenic shock and right ventricular support, and the Markov microsimulation of microaxial flow pump support is a different model that could be reused as a method template only.

## 8. Result scenarios

- Stage 1 shows a high expected value of information for the response effect: this would justify and size stage 3 and would be publishable as a value-of-information analysis that tells funders where a trial is worth running.
- Stage 2 shows lower mortality and MACE after adoption, with a credible pre-trend test: an association supporting a trial, and the first adjusted estimate at national scale.
- Stage 2 shows no difference, in keeping with PMID 38213306 and PMID 37973786: publishable if it is large, linked and well identified, and it would sharpen the argument that the response, not detection, is the missing step.
- Stage 3 reduces events: it would give a randomised basis for a surveillance-and-response pathway and would be of interest to a parent cardiology journal. A neutral result would be informative only if adherence to the bundle is high, because PMID 38213306 found that most injury-positive patients had no change in management.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- The response is weak: MANAGE is a single trial with 46% and 43% discontinuation (PMID 29900874), and the later trials were neutral for prevention (PMIDs 40884771, 40511609, 37640035). De-risk by running stage 1 first and by letting the model say how much effect is needed.
- The bundle is undefined: PMID 38213306 found no change in management in 82.7% of injury-positive patients, so adherence will drive results. De-risk by specifying the bundle and an adherence measure in the protocol.
- Dilution: background testing in the control arm is not known. Stage 2 should measure it.
- Exposure and event capture with administrative data are unreliable (check, design objection 1). De-risk by requiring linked laboratory data before committing to stage 2.
- Secular trends and contamination as surveillance spreads (PMID 41882681, PMID 42022610). Event-study checks and a cluster of late adopters help.
- Ethics: guidance already recommends surveillance in some settings, so a trial withholding it needs justification; stage 3 avoids this by randomising only the response.
- Fit with a cardiology parent journal is uncertain because the topic is perioperative; strength of design and hard outcomes would be needed.
- Scoop risk: moderate. The leading perioperative group states that management and outcomes need further research (PMID 41882681), and the 2027 cohorts (NCT02573532, NCT05812248) will add prognosis data but not the comparison.

Adversarial check summary (24 October 2026): the question survives with a narrower angle. No synthesis, emulation or randomised trial of surveillance with a response was found; the only direct attempts are small single-centre before-and-after studies (PMID 38213306, PMID 37973786). Editor objections were the dependence on a single-trial response, an undefined bundle, unstated dilution from background testing, absent power calculation, the health-services rather than cardiology fit, and cost. Design objections were unreliable registry capture of troponin, secular trends and contamination in a stepped-wedge, and the complexity of cluster consent. The check recommended lowering equipoise by about one point and design credibility by one point, which are applied, and replacing the stepped-wedge trial with the staged programme described here. The caveat that the registry search was keyword-based stands.
