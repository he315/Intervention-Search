# Adversarial review of Q-036

Prospective pre-registered synthesis of catheter-directed and mechanical therapies in intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism on hard clinical outcomes. Review date 24 October 2026. Every identifier below was retrieved from the local store (data/records, data/trials/current.jsonl.gz) or from a PubMed esearch/esummary call in this session, and title, journal and date are copied from those sources.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review, network meta-analysis, IPD analysis or large registry or emulation study of this question appears in Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, European Heart Journal, JACC, NEJM, Lancet, JAMA or BMJ in 2024 to 2026 in either the store or a PubMed search restricted to meta-analysis and systematic review publication types. The risk of a target-journal scoop is therefore low today, but the design itself has already been registered and published by others.

- PMID 41643878, American Heart Journal, published 1 May 2026 (epub 3 February 2026): "Reperfusion strategies for acute pulmonary embolism: Design and rationale of RECONNECT-PE-A living systematic review and meta-analysis." PROSPERO CRD420251207053. It is a living review of RCTs of reperfusion strategies against standard of care in high- and intermediate-risk PE. The primary analysis is restricted to trials designed for hard outcomes (death, haemodynamic deterioration), with a secondary analysis of surrogate-outcome trials, stratification by PE severity and a frequentist network meta-analysis. As of 28 May 2025 it included 23 RCTs and named PEITHO-3, HI-PEITHO, PEERLESS II, PE-TRACT and PRAGUE-26 as ongoing trials for later inclusion. This is the same question, the same update-as-trials-report logic and the same hard-outcome focus as Q-036. The only differences are Bayesian rather than frequentist modelling and the proposed common hierarchical scale.
- PMID 42469156, Acta Anaesthesiologica Scandinavica, published 1 August 2026: "Catheter-Based Therapies for Intermediate-High Risk Pulmonary Embolism: A Protocol for a Systematic Review With Bayesian Pairwise and Network Meta-Analyses." Protocol for a Bayesian pairwise and network meta-analysis of RCTs of catheter-directed thrombolysis, ultrasound-assisted thrombolysis and catheter embolectomy against anticoagulation with or without systemic thrombolysis, primary outcome all-cause mortality, secondary ISTH major bleeding at 7 days. This matches the proposed Bayesian NMA almost exactly, and it was published before Q-036 could be registered.
- PMID 42580844, Open Heart, 11 August 2026: catheter-directed thrombolysis versus anticoagulation, 18 studies and 16 126 patients (RCTs and observational), PROSPERO CRD420261373193. All-cause mortality RR 0.41 (95% CI 0.32 to 0.54), major bleeding RR 1.78 (95% CI 0.85 to 3.71). Observational data dominate and confounding limits it, but it occupies the pairwise question in print.
- PMID 42844669, Cardiology in Review, 8 October 2026: six RCTs, 886 patients, CDT plus anticoagulation versus anticoagulation alone, PROSPERO CRD420261402147. Early cardiorespiratory deterioration RR 0.36 (95% CI 0.19 to 0.68), major bleeding RR 1.52 (95% CI 0.71 to 3.24); the abstract states that mortality did not differ. It searched to May 2026 and so predates the two NEJM trials below.
- PMID 41865356 (Cardiovascular and Interventional Radiology, June 2026), PMID 41363677 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, February 2026), PMID 40269600 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, July 2025), PMID 41076852 (Thrombosis Research, November 2025, network meta-analysis of systemic thrombolysis in intermediate- and high-risk PE), PMID 38176962 (Cardiovascular Revascularization Medicine, May 2024, NMA of clot-dissolving therapies) and PMID 41920339 (single-arm pooled analysis of thrombectomy, 13 studies, 1001 patients) are further pairwise or network syntheses in specialty or lower-tier journals. I read only titles and journal details for these, not their abstracts, so their overlap is stated as a title-level judgement.
- Large observational analyses exist but are subspecialty-level: PMID 42219798 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, 1 August 2026), TriNetX propensity-matched mechanical thrombectomy versus anticoagulation in intermediate-risk PE, 2717 and 1150 pairs per arm in two risk strata, 1-year mortality HR 0.642 (95% CI 0.556 to 0.741) and 0.631 (95% CI 0.475 to 0.839), described by its authors as hypothesis-generating.

Randomised evidence that has now reported and changes the pooled base materially:

- PMID 41910345, NEJM, 28 May 2026 (epub 28 March 2026): "Ultrasound-Facilitated, Catheter-Directed Fibrinolysis for Acute Pulmonary Embolism" (HI-PEITHO, NCT04790370), 544 patients. Primary composite (PE-related death, cardiorespiratory decompensation or collapse, symptomatic recurrence within 7 days) 4.0% versus 10.3%, RR 0.39 (95% CI 0.20 to 0.77). Major bleeding within 7 days 4.1% versus 2.2%, P = 0.32.
- PMID 42678032, NEJM, 31 August 2026: "Catheter-Directed Thrombolysis in Intermediate-High-Risk Pulmonary Embolism" (NCT05493163, record title PRAGUE-26 in the trial snapshot), 558 patients. Primary composite at 7 days 0.7% versus 6.8%, RR 0.10 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.44). Major bleeding 1.4% versus 2.2%.
- PMID 39470698, Circulation, 4 February 2025: PEERLESS (NCT05111613), 550 patients, large-bore thrombectomy versus catheter-directed thrombolysis, hierarchical win-ratio primary endpoint, win ratio 5.01 (95% CI 3.68 to 6.97), driven mainly by ICU use and clinical deterioration, with no difference in mortality, intracranial haemorrhage or major bleeding.
- PMID 42610339, EuroIntervention, 17 August 2026 (BETULA, 60 patients, surrogate primary endpoint) and PMID 42473023 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, 19 July 2026, 20 patients) are small surrogate-endpoint RCTs.

The two NEJM trials mean the central pairwise question (catheter-directed thrombolysis versus anticoagulation alone on a decompensation-based composite) has been answered in a general-tier journal. Both used the same kind of composite, dominated by haemodynamic decompensation, with very few deaths (HI-PEITHO and the second NEJM trial each report only a handful of deaths in the abstracts I read). A synthesis of these two trials plus the six RCTs in PMID 42844669 is an obvious and imminent paper for RECONNECT-PE-A or the Bayesian protocol authors.

## Ongoing trials

All from data/trials/current.jsonl.gz, status as at the last snapshot.

- PEERLESS II, NCT06055920: FlowTriever versus anticoagulation alone, N 1200 (estimated), primary completion 2026-07-30, win-ratio hierarchy through discharge or 30 days, status active not recruiting, last updated 8 October 2026. This is the largest trial that bears directly on mechanical thrombectomy against anticoagulation. It will probably report within 24 months and sits inside Q-036's comparator set.
- PE-TRACT, NCT05591118: catheter-directed therapy versus anticoagulation, N 500, primary completion 2027-03-29, co-primary outcomes peak VO2 at month 3, NYHA class at month 12 and major bleeding at day 7. Functional rather than mortality endpoints.
- PEITHO-3, NCT04430569: reduced-dose systemic alteplase versus placebo, N 800, primary completion 2026-04-13, 30-day composite of death, haemodynamic decompensation or recurrent PE. Not a catheter trial, but a systemic-lysis comparator for any network.
- STRATIFY-II, NCT06453876: embolectomy, low-dose ultrasound-assisted thrombolysis or heparin, N 210, primary completion 2028-06-05, thrombus burden primary outcome.
- RAPID-PE, NCT06433011, N 500, primary completion 2028-04-25; PERSEVERE, NCT06588634, N 200, 2027-11; ULTRA-PE, NCT07452991, N 300, 2029-09-10; ESCADlys-PE, NCT06487052, N 100, 2027-01; PMT-LDCDT-PE, NCT07865572, N 150, 2030-12-31; NCT07869875, N 225, 2030-12-31; NCT07032025 (Tendvia versus systemic thrombolysis, N 200, 2027-07-01); and the high-risk trials CATCH-PE II (NCT06672081, N 210, 2027-06-30), TORPEDO-NL (NCT06833827, N 111, 2028-01) and REDSTEM (NCT07816978, N 400, 2030-01-31). Several of these name device-specific or surrogate primary outcomes, so they contribute little to a hard-outcome synthesis.

PEERLESS II, PE-TRACT and the smaller trials above are exactly the "registered trials that report" the Q-036 design would wait for. Their primary outcomes are heterogeneous (win ratio, VO2, NYHA, thrombus burden), which weakens the claim that a common hierarchical scale is achievable from published data alone.

## Editor objections

1. Already done in the same design space. A living, Bayesian, hard-outcome synthesis of reperfusion trials in intermediate-risk PE is registered and published (PMID 41643878, PMID 42469156), so the novelty claim does not survive. An editor would ask what the Q-036 review adds over a living review with the same trigger for updates.
2. The central pairwise result now exists in NEJM. With HI-PEITHO and the second NEJM trial reporting, a meta-analysis of catheter-directed thrombolysis against anticoagulation would be read as confirmatory unless it has IPD to test effect modification.
3. Thin hard-outcome base. The abstracts of the reported trials show very few deaths, and the composites are dominated by decompensation, a soft and partly clinician-driven component in open-label trials. A pooled all-cause mortality estimate will be imprecise for years, and the pooled composite adds little to the individual trials.
4. Open-label, mostly industry-funded trials (the HI-PEITHO abstract states funding by Boston Scientific). A synthesis inherits this bias and cannot correct it.
5. Prospective-protocol papers and living-review updates are rarely accepted in a parent journal; the most likely home is a specialty or methods journal, which fails the owner's target-journal standard.

## Design objections

- A common hierarchical outcome scale cannot be reconstructed from aggregate data. PEERLESS used a five-tier win ratio including ICU stay, HI-PEITHO and the second NEJM trial used composites of death, decompensation and recurrence, and PE-TRACT uses VO2 and NYHA class. Win ratios do not pool across differently constructed hierarchies without patient-level data, so the design is credible only as an IPD meta-analysis, which needs sponsor cooperation (Boston Scientific, Inari and the academic trial groups).
- Transitivity is doubtful. Comparators are anticoagulation alone, systemic thrombolysis at different doses, standard catheter-directed lysis and large-bore thrombectomy, with different populations (intermediate-risk defined by RV/LV ratio, troponin, sPESI or distress indicators) and device generations. A network linking thrombectomy to anticoagulation largely rests on one trial (PEERLESS) through catheter-directed thrombolysis, with PEERLESS II as the first direct link.
- Pre-registration after the competing protocol (PMID 42469156) and the earlier living review (PMID 41643878) removes the claim to be first.
- A Bayesian random-effects model with few trials and few events is dominated by the prior for between-trial heterogeneity, so the choice of prior will drive credible intervals for mortality.
- Timing: the review would be updated as each trial reports, but the main contributor (PEERLESS II) had a primary completion date of 30 July 2026, so the first update may arrive before registration is complete.
- A more defensible angle is an IPD synthesis testing heterogeneity of treatment effect by baseline risk (RV/LV ratio, troponin, sPESI, age, cancer) and time-varying effects, plus long-term functional and mortality follow-up, which no aggregate-data review can address. That angle depends on data-sharing agreements and is not obviously within the owner's current access.

## Verdict

Verdict: partly scooped

The pairwise and network question, in its aggregate-data form, is already occupied by a published living review (PMID 41643878, Am Heart J, 1 May 2026) and a published Bayesian pairwise and network meta-analysis protocol (PMID 42469156, Acta Anaesthesiol Scand, 1 August 2026), and the two NEJM trials (PMID 41910345, PMID 42678032) have answered the main catheter-directed thrombolysis versus anticoagulation comparison on a decompensation-based composite. What remains open is an IPD or patient-level meta-analysis of the catheter-based trials (HI-PEITHO, the second NEJM trial, PEERLESS, PEERLESS II and PE-TRACT when they report) designed to test effect modification by baseline risk, mortality and functional outcome at longer follow-up, and the thrombectomy-versus-anticoagulation comparison once PEERLESS II reports. Suggested changes: G3 should be recorded as failed for the aggregate-data design (a living meta-analysis already covers the evidence base and PEERLESS II, with 1200 patients, is expected to report within 24 months) and could pass only for a re-scoped IPD question; G5 should be marked as at risk because the aggregate version is a repeat synthesis of the same trials; novelty should fall from its current value to 2 for the aggregate design (3 if re-scoped to IPD effect modification); equipoise should fall to 3, since two NEJM RCTs favour catheter-directed thrombolysis on decompensation but leave mortality, thrombectomy against anticoagulation and long-term function unresolved. The question should not be promoted in its current form.
