# Adversarial review of Q-015

Question as received: does the benefit of TEER plus medical therapy versus medical therapy alone in heart failure with moderate to severe or severe secondary MR vary by baseline risk and over time, answered by a reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis of the randomised trials. Searches were run on 24 October 2026 against the local store (title and abstract scan, local trial snapshot), PubMed E-utilities and ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session. Four records (39217568, 40484281, 40551302, 42657844) have no abstract in PubMed or the store, so their content is judged from title and journal only and is flagged as unverified.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 41549691, EuroIntervention, 19 January 2026: "Transcatheter edge-to-edge repair plus guideline-directed medical therapy versus guideline-directed medical therapy alone for symptomatic functional mitral regurgitation: a comprehensive, up-to-date meta-analysis of randomised trials". It pooled MITRA-FR, COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 (1,422 patients) using mixed-effects Cox models on reconstructed time-to-first-event individual patient data (one-stage) alongside a two-stage study-level analysis, to 24 months. One-stage HR for death or first HF hospitalisation 0.72 (95% CI 0.56-0.92); two-stage HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.51-1.00), I-squared 80.3%. Leave-one-out analysis excluding MITRA-FR made death significant. This is the same reconstructed-IPD method on the same three trials. It is a specialty-tier journal, but it removes most of the methodological novelty of the pooled main-effect analysis. Overlap: high for the pooled effect to 24 months; it did not report time-varying hazards, RMST, landmark or competing-risk analyses, or effect modification by baseline risk (as far as the abstract shows).
- PMID 42321618, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, 19 June 2026: reconstructed survival data meta-analysis of 16 studies (n = 4,570) of MTEER, surgical repair and medical therapy in functional MR, using the Liu method, Cox and time-varying Cox models, landmark, time-restricted and RMST analyses up to 60 months. It is mostly observational, mixes randomised and non-randomised data, and is not a randomised-only analysis. Its methods overlap closely with the proposed analysis set (time-varying Cox, landmark, RMST), so the method is not novel, although the evidence base differs.
- PMID 39217568, Journal of the American College of Cardiology, 10 December 2024: "Percutaneous Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair for Functional Mitral Regurgitation in Heart Failure: A Meta-Analysis of 3 Randomized Controlled Trials". Same three trials, target-tier journal. No abstract is available, so the methods and results are unverified. The title alone shows that a target-journal editor has already published a three-trial pooled analysis.
- PMID 39918181, Monaldi Archives for Chest Disease, 2 March 2026 (epub 6 February 2025): study-level random-effects meta-analysis of COAPT, MITRA-FR and RESHAPE-HF2 (1,426 participants). At 24 months, first HF hospitalisation HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.45-0.96), all HF hospitalisation HR 0.63 (95% CI 0.49-0.81), all-cause death HR 0.76 (95% CI 0.57-1.01), CV death HR 0.77 (95% CI 0.56-1.06). It concludes that LV enlargement may explain heterogeneity.
- PMID 41868764, JSCAI, 1 March 2026: updated meta-analysis of three RCTs plus five propensity-matched studies (n = 2,588), 12-month all-cause mortality RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58-0.95). Mixed designs.
- Other three-trial pooled analyses with no abstract available (unverified content): 40484281 (Hellenic Journal of Cardiology, 2026, title states "time-to-event meta-analysis of randomized trials", which suggests reconstructed time-to-event pooling), 40551302 (Journal of the American Heart Association, 1 July 2025, "Meta-Analysis of Randomized Trials"), 42657844 (JACC: Advances, 1 August 2026, "Bayesian Meta-Analysis"). Further study-level or mixed syntheses in the store include 40452619 (CCI, 2025) and 42723699 (REC Interv Cardiol, 2026). The field has at least seven to eight pooled analyses of the same three trials since 2024.
- Effect modification and heterogeneity analyses already published: PMID 42120116 (JACC Cardiovasc Interv, 11 May 2026) clustered patients in COAPT (n = 614) and validated in MITRA-FR, finding three phenotypes with different 2-year benefit from M-TEER; PMID 41416702 (Eur Heart J, 18 May 2026) examined RESHAPE-HF2 outcomes by baseline KCCQ tertile (HR 0.71, 0.50 and 0.73 across tertiles, P-trend 0.53); PMID 39217574 (JACC, 10 December 2024) reported an interaction by prior HF hospitalisation (P-interaction 0.03 for the composite). Trial-level baseline-risk heterogeneity is therefore being addressed with real patient-level data by the trial groups themselves.
- Transportability and large-scale analysis: PMID 42233929 (JACC, 6 October 2026), transportability analysis of COAPT (614 patients) to the STS/ACC TVT Registry (15,275 patients, 7,289 COAPT-eligible), estimating 2-year absolute risk reductions of 17.0% for HF hospitalisation (95% CI -28.7% to -5.7%) and 15.4% for all-cause death (95% CI -26.6% to -5.2%). This is a target-tier paper that already answers part of the "does benefit vary with baseline risk and case mix" angle for COAPT.
- Primary trial: PMID 39216092 (N Engl J Med, 14 November 2024), RESHAPE-HF2, 505 patients, rate ratio 0.64 (95% CI 0.48-0.85) for recurrent HF hospitalisation or CV death at 24 months (NCT02444338).
- No IPD meta-analysis with trial-group data (as opposed to reconstructed curves) of COAPT, MITRA-FR and RESHAPE-HF2 was found in PubMed or the store.

## Ongoing trials

No ongoing randomised trial of TEER plus medical therapy against medical therapy alone in ventricular secondary MR was found that would change the pooled base within 24 months. Trials read in the local snapshot or the ClinicalTrials.gov API:

- NCT07758023 (ACHILLES-HF), 520 participants, primary completion 2030-09-30, not yet recruiting: percutaneous treatment of secondary MR to achieve optimal medical therapy in HFrEF. Would add data after 24 months.
- NCT07197021 (TIMER), 540 participants, primary completion 2031-12-31, not yet recruiting: TEER in dynamic severe functional MR.
- NCT07709208 (MARCER), 246 participants, primary completion 2029-06, recruiting: TEER in exercise-induced severe MR.
- NCT07414225 (STAR), 400 participants, primary completion 2030-02-25, recruiting, and NCT07741253 (PRECISE-AFMR I), 182 participants, primary completion 2027-12: atrial functional MR, a different population.
- NCT03142152 (EMPOWER, Carillon), 300 participants, primary completion 2026-12-31: a different device.
- NCT07243158 and NCT04733404 (Dragonfly system, 148 and 120 participants): different device.
- NCT06282042 (EMCAMI), 250 participants, primary completion 2027-06-16: early repair after myocardial infarction.

None of these reports within 24 months on the exact comparison in the stated population with a sample that would materially change the 1,422 to 1,426-patient pooled base. G3 therefore passes on the trial criterion. It fails the meta-analysis half of G3 as written: several pooled analyses of the same three trials appeared in the past 24 months, including one in a target-tier journal (39217568, December 2024) and one using reconstructed IPD (41549691, January 2026). No trial published since would change the pooled sample (the three trials are unchanged).

## Editor objections

- Three trials, about 1,420 patients, have now been pooled at least seven times. An editor at Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, EHJ or JACC will see a fourth or fifth pooled analysis of identical data, with the main effect already settled at the level that influences guidelines. A trial-level meta-analysis cannot give new patient-level information beyond what the trial groups publish.
- Heterogeneity between MITRA-FR and COAPT is already explained in the literature by LV size, MR proportionality, prior HF hospitalisation and phenotype clustering, all from patient-level data held by the trial groups (42120116, 41416702, 39217574).
- Reconstructed curves cannot support credible effect-modification analyses. Subgroup curves are rarely published, and the model would rest on a handful of published subgroup HRs, which is what the trial groups' own analyses already report.
- Only three randomised trials exist, so a treatment-covariate interaction across trials is confounded by trial (device generation, medical therapy, follow-up, design and eligibility). Any conclusion about baseline-risk variation would be ecological.
- Observational data on TEER outcomes (EuroSMR 5-year, 39537275, and the TVT-based transportability work in 42233929) already speak to generalisability.
- The proposed analysis would be better received if it used true IPD from COAPT, MITRA-FR and RESHAPE-HF2, which is a consortium project rather than a reconstruction project.

## Design objections

- Reconstructed IPD from published Kaplan-Meier curves reproduces only time-to-first-event outcomes and the published number at risk. It cannot support competing-risk analysis of death and HF hospitalisation without cause-specific curves, and recurrent events (the primary endpoint of RESHAPE-HF2, 39216092) are lost.
- Treatment-covariate interaction "using published subgroup data" is aggregate-data meta-regression, not IPD analysis, and with three trials has essentially no power; it cannot establish heterogeneity of treatment effect by baseline risk.
- Time-varying hazards and RMST are feasible from reconstructed curves, and the same toolkit has been applied in 41549691 and 42321618, so the technical contribution is not distinctive.
- Crossover from control to TEER and background therapy differences (the likely source of the MITRA-FR and COAPT discordance) cannot be handled without patient-level data.
- A credible version needs trial-group IPD, or at least the patient-level risk distribution (for example a risk score such as the one validated for COAPT patients), and ideally the 5-year COAPT follow-up. Without these, the design does not meet G4 for the baseline-risk part of the question.

## Verdict

partly scooped

Reason: the main-effect and time-to-event pooling of the three randomised trials with reconstructed IPD has already been published (41549691, EuroIntervention, January 2026), alongside a target-tier three-trial meta-analysis (39217568, JACC, December 2024, abstract unverified) and several further pooled analyses; the effect-modification questions are being answered with patient-level data by the trial groups (42120116, 41416702, 39217574), and transportability to US practice has appeared in JACC (42233929). The remaining angle is narrow: a true patient-level IPD pooling of COAPT, MITRA-FR and RESHAPE-HF2 (or a multi-trial landmark/RMST analysis with long-term COAPT follow-up) examining time-varying effects and competing risks by baseline risk, which requires trial-group collaboration, not reconstruction. Suggested changes: (1) re-scope Q-015 to a trial-group IPD collaboration with the stated narrow angle, and demote the reconstructed-IPD design to an alternative; (2) G3 should be recorded as failing on its meta-analysis limb as currently written (the pooled evidence base has not changed since the 2024 to 2026 syntheses, so the "trials published since" exception does not apply) and passing on the trial limb; if the question is re-scoped, G3 can pass only with an explicit statement that no prior analysis used patient-level data on competing risks and time-varying effects; (3) G5 should be marked at risk or failed for the reconstructed-IPD version, because it is a repeat meta-analysis of the same three trials; (4) novelty should fall from its current level to 2 (or 3 for the IPD collaboration version); (5) equipoise should be reduced by one point (to 3 at most), because RESHAPE-HF2 (39216092) with COAPT now point the same way for HF hospitalisation, and only the mortality effect and patient selection remain contested.
