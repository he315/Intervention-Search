# Q-026: Does CT-led coronary evaluation, alone or with protocolised secondary prevention, improve outcomes in type 2 myocardial infarction with a plausible coronary mechanism?

Narrowing note. The register title is the original framing (systematic coronary CTA plus protocolised prevention versus usual care after any adjudicated type 2 MI, in a registry-based randomised trial). The independent adversarial check (register/briefs/Q-026_adversarial.md, 24 October 2026) found that the question survives only in narrower form, because the intervention bundles two components, the population is heterogeneous, and a registry cannot supply adjudicated type 2 MI. This brief is built on the narrowed question: a pragmatic multicentre randomised trial in type 2 MI patients with a plausible coronary mechanism, testing CT-led coronary evaluation and protocolised prevention as separate factors, with registry linkage used for outcome follow-up rather than for diagnosis or randomisation.

## 1. Question in PICO form

- Population: adults with type 2 MI adjudicated against the Fourth Universal Definition during the index admission, restricted to those with a plausible coronary mechanism (for example known coronary disease, or a pre-specified high pre-test probability of obstructive disease).
- Intervention: factorial. Factor A, CT coronary angiography-led coronary evaluation (with invasive angiography and physiology for significant disease). Factor B, protocolised secondary prevention (lipid lowering, antithrombotic and heart failure therapy according to imaging and echocardiographic findings).
- Comparator: usual care without systematic coronary evaluation (Factor A) and without a protocol for prevention (Factor B).
- Outcomes: primary, time to first cardiovascular death, MI or heart failure hospitalisation; secondary, all-cause death and each component, with non-cardiovascular death handled as a competing event.
- Timing: randomisation during the index admission; follow-up of at least 2 years.

## 2. Why it matters now

Decision. Whether to image the coronary arteries and start long-term prevention after type 2 MI, or to treat only the precipitating illness. The decision is made by cardiologists and acute medicine physicians.

Population size. Type 2 MI accounted for 3.0% (n = 3,609) of 120,734 patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome in an individual patient data meta-analysis of 9 cohorts (PMID 41553312). In a US claims database, 18,606 of 140,344 patients with MI (13%) had type 2 MI (PMID 39983613). A stepped-wedge implementation trial reported that high-sensitivity troponin raised type 2 MI diagnoses by 22% (205/916) (PMID 31587565). A UK-wide or European annual count was not retrieved.

Practice variation. In the US claims cohort, patients with type 2 MI were less likely than those with type 1 MI to undergo coronary angiography (11.7% versus 76.6%; adjusted OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.09-0.11) and to fill new prescriptions for statins (19.1% versus 59.1%; OR 0.51, 95% CI 0.47-0.55) within 6 months (PMID 39983613). A systematic review of factors associated with angiography in type 2 MI found three studies, very low certainty for every factor, and heterogeneous selection (PMID 42882047). A young-adult cohort reported that patients with type 2 MI were significantly less likely to receive cardiovascular medications at discharge (PMID 32138959).

Why now. Unrecognised and untreated coronary disease is common (section 3), a 2026 individual patient data meta-analysis has defined the competing-risk structure that a trial must handle (PMID 41553312), and a UK pilot has shown randomisation is feasible and has called for a multicentre trial (PMID 40305953). The target-journal commentaries state that trials are needed (PMID 39658094; PMID 38506815).

## 3. What is known

Prognosis (observational associations only).
- PMID 41553312 (J Am Coll Cardiol, 2026): individual patient data meta-analysis, 17 studies of 9 cohorts, 120,734 patients. Type 2 MI MACE rate 51.7 per 1,000 patient-years and non-cardiovascular death rate 60.1 per 1,000 patient-years; subdistribution HR for MACE 3.36 (95% CI: 2.92-3.86) against no myocardial injury. The authors conclude that in type 2 MI the apparent risk is reduced by a substantially greater competing risk of non-cardiovascular death.
- PMID 29020401 (Eur Heart J, 2017): prospective cohort of 1,548 patients; 1-year mortality 13.8% in type 2 MI versus 9.4% in type 1 MI.
- PMID 32138959 (J Am Coll Cardiol, 2020): 3,829 adults aged 50 or younger; type 2 MI associated with higher all-cause mortality (HR 1.8, 95% CI 1.2 to 2.7) and cardiovascular mortality (HR 2.7, 95% CI 1.4 to 5.1) than type 1 MI.
- PMID 31902228 (Circulation, 2020): community cohort of 1,054 patients with type 2 MI (Olmsted County), incidence and outcomes.
- PMID 36705027 (J Investig Med, 2023): systematic review and meta-analysis of type 2 versus type 1 MI outcomes; prognosis only.

Coronary disease yield (cohorts, no outcome comparison).
- PMID 35341327 (Circulation, 2022; NCT03338504): 100 patients with a provisional diagnosis of type 2 MI, all imaged. Coronary disease in 68% (63 of 93), obstructive in 30% (28 of 93); previously unrecognised coronary disease in 60% (38 of 63), of whom 33% (21 of 63) were on evidence-based treatment.
- PMID 37777947 (J Am Coll Cardiol, 2023; NCT04864119): 50 participants; coronary plaque in 92%, stenosis of at least 50% in 42%, obstructive disease in 26%, haemodynamically significant FFRCT stenosis in 26%.

Treatment-strategy evidence.
- No randomised trial of coronary evaluation or prevention with clinical outcomes in type 2 MI was found in the store.
- PMID 31587565 (Circulation, 2019; NCT01852123): after high-sensitivity troponin implementation, the abstract states that increased recognition of type 2 MI did not lead to changes in investigation, treatment or outcomes, and that trials of secondary prevention are urgently required.
- PMID 39206549 (Circulation, 2024; NCT02981407): prespecified analysis of a transfusion trial by MI type (type 2 n = 1955); death or MI RR 1.05 (95% CI 0.85-1.29) for restrictive versus liberal transfusion in type 2 MI, interaction P = 0.16. It concerns transfusion, not coronary evaluation.
- PMID 41702823 (Cardiovasc Revasc Med, 2026): review stating that guidelines give limited, largely diagnostic guidance and no trial-validated pathways for coronary evaluation or revascularisation in type II MI, and that the proposed framework is expert opinion.

Guideline position. Guideline extracts have not been retrieved for this system, so no class or level is stated here. The statement above is the review authors' characterisation (PMID 41702823), not a retrieved recommendation.

## 4. What is under way

- NCT05419583 (TARGET-Type 2, University of Edinburgh): pilot randomised trial, N 60 (actual), primary completion 2024-06-30, completed; primary outcome registered as trial process outcomes at 90 days. Published as PMID 40305953: 4,127 screened, 403 (10%) met inclusion criteria, 60 (42% of those approached) consented and randomised (28 intervention, 32 standard care), adherence 90.7% (95% CI: 85.3%-96.1%); the authors state a multicentre trial is now required.
- NCT06047392 (NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde): pilot randomised trial of CT coronary angiography in suspected type 2 MI, N 100 (estimated), primary completion 2024-02, status unknown; primary outcome change in primary diagnosis.
- NCT06382402 (University of Louisville): randomised trial of a CCTA-guided strategy in type 2 NSTEMI, N 0 (actual), withdrawn, planned completion 2025-01-01; registered primary outcomes include MACE within 3 years. The adversarial report notes that a change of design is planned; a redesigned registration was not found.
- NCT04838808 (R2MI, University of Alberta): rivaroxaban 2.5 mg versus placebo, feasibility design, N 8 (registry field, actual); the publication (PMID 37180843) reports 7 of 276 screened patients (2.5%) randomised and that 94.5% of 916 adjudicated type 2 MI patients in a chart review had an exclusion criterion.

No adequately powered outcome trial of the same comparison appears in the trials file or in the adversarial searches.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: no evidence (no outcome trial), with a feasibility literature that has only just begun.

- G1 Consequence: the decision is common and the outcomes are hard (death, MI, heart failure hospitalisation). Pass.
- G2 Equipoise: no randomised answer; supporting claims are observational and a review states that trial-validated pathways are absent (PMID 41702823). Guideline position unverified. Pass on trial evidence only.
- G3 Not about to be answered: no powered trial expected within 24 months; the individual patient data meta-analysis (PMID 41553312) is prognostic. Residual risk is a multicentre TARGET-Type 2 follow-on or a redesigned Louisville study (NCT06382402), neither of which was found registered. Pass, with that note.
- G4 Credible design: conditional on how adjudicated type 2 MI is captured, which the narrowed design addresses by local adjudication with central review. Pass, conditional.
- G5 Not incremental: first randomised outcome question on strategy in type 2 MI, although the idea is already in pilot testing. Pass.

## 6. Design

Primary design: pragmatic, multicentre, open-label, blinded-endpoint 2 x 2 factorial randomised trial, with randomisation and event ascertainment supported by linkage to national registries and hospital-episode data where available.

Why this design. A factorial separates the two components that the adversarial check showed could not be separated in a bundled intervention, and prevention alone is cheap and may carry most of any benefit. A weaker design would not do because the observational literature shows selection into angiography and prevention is strongly confounded (PMID 39983613; PMID 42882047), so an emulation cannot settle the question for a guideline committee. Registry linkage is used only for outcomes because registries code type 2 MI poorly and cannot supply adjudicated diagnosis (adversarial report).

Key methodological choices.
- Eligibility: type 2 MI adjudicated locally by two clinicians with a centrally reviewed sample of at least 10%, plus a pre-specified coronary-mechanism criterion (known coronary disease or a risk score fixed in the protocol). Exclusions kept to the minimum, because 94.5% of type 2 MI patients had an exclusion criterion in R2MI (PMID 37180843) and only 10% of screened patients met inclusion in the pilot (PMID 40305953).
- Time zero: randomisation during the index admission.
- Factor A (CT-led evaluation): coronary CTA in the index admission or within 30 days, with invasive angiography and physiology for significant disease; the comparator is usual care, with any imaging recorded as a process measure.
- Factor B (prevention protocol): fixed rules for statin, antithrombotic and heart failure therapy driven by imaging and echocardiography; the comparator is usual care.
- Primary outcome: time to first cardiovascular death, MI or heart failure hospitalisation, which avoids reliance on the coding of MI subtype. Non-cardiovascular death is a competing event, as the competing-risk structure in PMID 41553312 requires.
- Estimand: effect of assignment (intention to treat) on the cause-specific hazard and on cumulative incidence at 2 years, for each factor, with a pre-specified test for interaction.
- Analysis: Cox model for cause-specific hazards with Fine-Gray subdistribution hazards as a sensitivity analysis; stratification by site and by precipitant group; pre-specified subgroups by prior coronary disease and by precipitant.
- Process fidelity: imaging uptake, revascularisation and prescribing at 90 days captured on a short case report form, since registries cannot supply them.
- Registry platform: ClinicalTrials.gov and ISRCTN, with outcome ascertainment through national mortality and hospital-episode linkage.

Sample size (illustrative; every input is labelled).
- Control event rate input: MACE (MI or cardiovascular death) of 51.7 per 1,000 patient-years in type 2 MI (PMID 41553312). Over 2 years the cumulative control risk is 1 - exp(-0.0517 x 2) = 9.8% (derived). The registered composite adds heart failure hospitalisation, so this is a conservative input for the primary outcome.
- Assumed effect (an assumption, not taken from the store): hazard ratio 0.80 for each factor, two-sided alpha 0.05, power 80%, no interaction, 1:1 allocation within each factor, no loss to follow-up.
- Required events (Schoenfeld): 4 x (1.96 + 0.84)^2 / (ln 0.80)^2 = about 630 events.
- Treated-arm risk at 2 years: 1 - (1 - 0.098)^0.80 = 7.9% (derived); mean risk 8.9%.
- Total sample size: 630 / 0.089 = about 7,100 participants. Each factor is tested on all participants in a factorial.
- Sensitivity: hazard ratio 0.75 gives about 380 events and 4,400 participants; hazard ratio 0.70 gives about 250 events and 2,900. If the restricted population has twice the control event rate (an illustrative assumption with no source), 7,100 falls to about 3,700 at hazard ratio 0.80.
- The calculation ignores competing non-cardiovascular death, which would raise the required number; the pilot's recruitment yield (60 randomised of 4,127 screened, PMID 40305953) implies screening about 490,000 troponin-positive patients (7,100 / 60 x 4,127, derived) for 7,100 participants if the pilot yield held, which is why the trial needs many sites and relaxed exclusions. These figures should be re-derived from the pilot's outcome data when available.

Alternative design: target trial emulation of CT or invasive coronary evaluation within 30 days versus none in linked national data (for example hospital-episode data linked to national MI audit and mortality records), with time zero at the index discharge or day 30 landmark, clone-censor-weight handling of the grace period, and a negative-control outcome. It would suffer confounding by indication and could not serve as the guideline-grade answer, but it would size the effect and test feasibility.

## 7. Data and collaborators

- Trial group: the Edinburgh TARGET-Type 2 investigators (NCT05419583; PMID 40305953) are the natural partners and the most likely competitors; collaboration is preferable to competition. The Glasgow group (NCT06047392) and the investigators of PMID 37777947 and PMID 39983613 are further possible partners.
- Registry and linkage: a national MI audit and hospital-episode or population registers for outcomes; the High-STEACS population (PMID 31587565; PMID 38011922) shows that linked Scottish data can ascertain outcomes in a type 2 MI population.
- Owner already has: UK Biobank extract (for emulation and event-rate checking), registry-embedded randomisation methodology, reconstructed-IPD and meta-analytic methods, decision modelling, and trials training (Oxford MSc place accepted). The owner does not hold a type 2 MI registry, imaging cohort or CT infrastructure.

## 8. Result scenarios

- Positive on cardiovascular events for either factor: first randomised evidence to support a strategy in a large population treated inconsistently, suitable for a target journal on the strength of the question and a pragmatic design.
- Prevention protocol effective, imaging not: would define a low-cost bundle without the need for CT, and would be informative for health systems.
- Neither factor effective: a large pragmatic null result would stop routine imaging and prescribing programmes aimed at this population and would be publishable if the trial is large and powered for hard outcomes, which the adversarial report notes is the main route to a target journal for a null finding.
- Imaging increases revascularisation without benefit: informs harm and cost, with a pre-specified economic evaluation.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Heterogeneity of precipitants dilutes cardiovascular benefit; mitigated by the coronary-mechanism restriction and precipitant stratification.
- Recruitment: 2.5% randomised in R2MI (PMID 37180843) and 1.5% of screened patients in the pilot (60 of 4,127, derived from PMID 40305953); mitigated by minimal exclusions, automated troponin-based screening and dedicated research staff.
- Adjudication inconsistency: local adjudication with central review; sensitivity analysis restricted to centrally confirmed cases.
- Competing mortality from non-cardiovascular causes: competing-risk analysis, and a note that the primary outcome may need cardiovascular death alone if event numbers are low.
- Cannot be separated from the TARGET-Type 2 programme: engage the Edinburgh group before commitment; confirm whether a definitive trial is registered.
- Scoop risk: moderate for a multicentre follow-on, unlikely to report before 2030 (adversarial report, an estimate); low for other groups, as no other powered trial was found.
- Subspecialty risk: likely to be read as an acute-care paper unless clearly powered for hard outcomes.

Adversarial check summary (24 October 2026). Verdict: survives with a narrower angle. No synthesis, emulation or trial answering the question was found; the closest items are prognostic or feasibility studies (PMIDs 36705027, 42882047, 39206549, 37777947, 35341327, 39983613). Editor objections were heterogeneity of precipitants, bundled interventions, low eligibility and consent in the pilot, inconsistent adjudication, an unanchored sample size, and subspecialty framing. Design objections were that a registry-based design depends on registry coding of type 2 MI, that delivery of the intervention needs site-level fidelity, and that cluster or stepped-wedge designs add contamination. Response: this brief narrows the population, separates the two components in a factorial, uses registry linkage for outcomes only, and plans the partnership with the TARGET-Type 2 group.
