# Q-016: Benchmarked target trial emulation of TEER versus continued medical therapy in the excluded phenotypes of secondary mitral regurgitation (LVEF below 20%, advanced LV dilatation, severe pulmonary hypertension, right ventricular dysfunction)

Status: candidate. Domain: D4. Target journal: Circulation or JACC. Brief written 24 October 2026.

Narrowing. The register title is the original framing: "Target trial emulation of TEER versus medical therapy in secondary MR patients outside the COAPT criteria". The independent adversarial check (register/briefs/Q-016_adversarial.md) found that PMID 42233929 (JACC, 6 October 2026) and PMID 40629531 (European Heart Journal, 13 March 2026) had already taken much of the broad "beyond trial criteria" ground. This brief therefore builds on the narrower angle that survived: the emulation is restricted to the specific excluded phenotypes, it is benchmarked first by recovering the COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 results in the same data, it treats LVAD, transplant and non-cardiac death as competing events, and it drops health status as an outcome. It is framed as complementing PMID 42233929, not repeating it.

## 1. Question

In adults with heart failure and moderate to severe or severe secondary MR who have at least one of LVEF below 20%, advanced LV dilatation, severe pulmonary hypertension or right ventricular dysfunction, does mitral TEER initiated within a 90-day grace period after the first echocardiogram meeting criteria, compared with continued medical therapy without TEER, change 3-year all-cause death and heart failure hospitalisation, with LVAD, transplant and non-cardiac death handled as competing events?

- Population: as above, with quantified echocardiography and a guideline-directed medical therapy requirement specified in eligibility.
- Intervention: TEER within 90 days of eligibility.
- Comparator: continued medical therapy without TEER.
- Outcomes: all-cause death and heart failure hospitalisation (primary composite and components); cardiovascular death with non-cardiac death, LVAD and transplant as competing events.
- Timing: time zero at the first qualifying echocardiogram; follow-up to 3 years.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision. Heart teams and heart failure clinicians decide whether to offer TEER to patients who would have been excluded from the randomised trials, and commissioners decide whether to fund it. The decision is made often: in the TVT Registry, 2,954 of 6,675 patients (44.3%) treated for functional MR would have been COAPT-ineligible on the criteria used (PMID 38267110). Those patients had a higher 1-year risk of death or heart failure hospitalisation (HR 1.73, 95% CI 1.57 to 1.91) and a similar proportion with a clinically meaningful health status improvement at 30 days (78.9% versus 77.0%). The registry has no medical-therapy comparator, so the data cannot say whether the worse outcome reflects the disease or an absence of benefit.

Population size. Severe secondary MR was present in 1,317 of 13,223 patients (10%) with secondary MR in a Viennese community cohort and was associated with excess mortality across heart failure subtypes (PMID 34193442).

Practice variation. The share of treated or referred patients who meet trial criteria differs by setting: 34.5% COAPT-eligible in EuroSMR (353 of 1,022, PMID 34886961) and 14% of 394 consecutively admitted patients at one centre (PMID 38923261). In the same Viennese cohort, transcatheter repair was used in 4% of patients with severe secondary MR, surgical repair in 7% and replacement in 5% (PMID 34193442), so uptake is low and the evidence needed to widen or restrict it is missing.

Timing. The randomised evidence in trial-like patients has just consolidated (PMID 39216092, PMID 41549691), a transportability analysis in trial-eligible and broader treatment-candidate patients has just appeared (PMID 42233929), and no randomised comparison focused on the extreme phenotypes reports before 2030 (section 4). Guideline positions have not been extracted in this system, so no guideline class or level is stated in this brief.

## 3. What is known

- PMID 30145927 (MITRA-FR, NEJM): 304 patients with severe secondary MR, LVEF 15 to 40%; death or unplanned heart failure hospitalisation at 12 months 54.6% versus 51.3% (OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.73 to 1.84).
- PMID 30280640 (COAPT, NEJM): 614 patients; all heart failure hospitalisations within 24 months 35.8% versus 67.9% per patient-year (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.70); death within 24 months 29.1% versus 46.1% (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.46 to 0.82).
- PMID 36876756 (COAPT five-year follow-up, NEJM): all-cause mortality 57.3% versus 67.2% (HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.58 to 0.89).
- PMID 39216092 (RESHAPE-HF2, NEJM): 505 patients; first or recurrent heart failure hospitalisation or cardiovascular death at 24 months, rate ratio 0.64 (95% CI 0.48 to 0.85).
- PMID 41549691 (EuroIntervention, reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis of the three trials, 1,422 patients): death or first heart failure hospitalisation at 24 months, one-stage HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.56 to 0.92), two-stage HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.51 to 1.00), I2 80.3%. PMID 39217568 (JACC, 10 December 2024) is a meta-analysis of the same three trials; the store holds only its title.
- PMID 41868764 (JSCAI, specialty tier): 3 randomised trials and 5 propensity-matched studies, n = 2,588; 12-month all-cause mortality RR 0.74 (95% CI 0.58 to 0.95). It mixes designs.
- PMID 33243380 (COAPT, JACC): in the 184 of 528 analysed patients with PASP of 50 mm Hg or more, TEER reduced death or heart failure hospitalisation irrespective of baseline PASP (p for interaction 0.45).
- PMID 38267110 (TVT Registry, JACC): COAPT-ineligible patients described in section 2.
- PMID 34886961 (EuroSMR, JACC): lower mortality in COAPT-eligible than COAPT-ineligible patients (P < 0.001); NYHA class IV (HR 2.29, 95% CI 1.53 to 3.42), log NT-proBNP (HR 1.47, 95% CI 1.24 to 1.75) and right ventricular-to-pulmonary arterial coupling (HR 0.10, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.57) were independent predictors.
- PMID 37730284 (COAPT-PAS, JACC, 5,000 patients, single-arm): 1-year heart failure hospitalisation 18.9% overall; the study compared with trial arms only descriptively.
- PMID 42233929 (JACC, 6 October 2026): transportability of COAPT to 15,275 TVT Registry patients (7,289 COAPT-eligible). Estimated 2-year absolute risk reductions of 17.0% for heart failure hospitalisation (95% CI -28.7% to -5.7%) and 15.4% for all-cause death (95% CI -26.6% to -5.2%) in the trial-eligible population, and a similar heart failure hospitalisation effect in the broader treatment-candidate population (P for difference 0.90). The conclusion depends on conditional exchangeability and positivity of trial participation, and it does not use an untreated real-world comparator.
- PMID 40629531 (European Heart Journal, 13 March 2026): atrial functional MR, 441 TEER patients (OCEAN-Mitral) versus 640 medically managed (REVEAL-AFMR); overlap-weighted primary composite HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.43 to 0.99). It is observational, in an atrial rather than ventricular population.
- PMID 40713981 (EXPANDed, JACC: Heart Failure, single-arm): 81 non-COAPT-like patients with 1-year mortality and heart failure hospitalisation rates similar to COAPT-like patients; no comparator.
- PMID 38923261 (single centre, specialty tier): among non-COAPT patients, weighted 4-year survival 91.5% with MitraClip versus 71.8% with optimised medical therapy (p = 0.027); small and confounded, but it states this exact question.
- Single-arm subgroup data on the proposed exclusions: PMID 42600559 (LVEF below 20%, 103 patients, primary composite HR 1.24, 95% CI 0.82 to 1.88 against LVEF 20 to 40%, cardiovascular death HR 2.00, 95% CI 1.08 to 3.68); PMID 41041987 (pulmonary hypertension, 144 patients, worst outcomes with combined post- and pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension); PMID 41878851 (potential LVAD candidates, 129 patients, symptom improvement limited and reverse remodelling not achieved with markedly enlarged left ventricles).
- PMID 41065256 (GIOTTO, JAHA): 2-year cumulative incidence of cardiac and non-cardiac mortality 19% and 12% in 1,185 treated patients, which supports a competing-risk estimand.
- PMID 39320294 (JACC editorial) proposes a volume-based framework reconciling the three trials.

No published target trial emulation of TEER versus medical therapy in secondary MR was found in the store or in the two PubMed searches reported in the adversarial check.

- G4 Credible design, pass conditional on data: an emulation with a benchmarking step is the design a guideline committee could cite when no trial is imminent, and randomisation in this group is unlikely to be funded. It depends on identifying a dataset with quantified echocardiography (section 7).

The register line records G5 as pass; this brief follows the adversarial report, and the benchmarking step and narrow phenotype focus are what keep it clear of the incremental label.

## 6. Design

Primary design: target trial emulation using linked echocardiography, hospital, procedure and mortality data, with clone-censor-weight handling of the grace period.

Target trial protocol.
- Eligibility: adults with heart failure, moderate to severe or severe secondary MR on a quantified echocardiogram (effective regurgitant orifice area or regurgitant volume recorded), receiving or having received a documented trial of guideline-directed medical therapy, and at least one excluded feature: LVEF below 20%, advanced LV dilatation, severe pulmonary hypertension or right ventricular dysfunction. Exclude cardiogenic shock, prior mitral intervention and anatomy documented as unsuitable. Eligibility is mapped criterion by criterion to the COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 definitions so that the same machinery can be run on the benchmark populations. The numerical thresholds for LV dilatation, pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular dysfunction are fixed in the protocol before outcomes are examined.
- Treatment strategies: TEER within 90 days of eligibility, plus medical therapy; versus medical therapy without TEER during follow-up. Medical therapy optimisation is specified as part of eligibility and as a time-varying covariate, because registry usual care is not the same strategy as the trial control arms.
- Assignment: emulated randomisation conditional on baseline covariates.
- Time zero: the first echocardiogram meeting eligibility.
- Follow-up: 3 years, ending at death, end of data or 3 years. Competing events are LVAD, transplant and non-cardiac death for cardiovascular outcomes.
- Outcomes: all-cause death or heart failure hospitalisation (primary); the components; cardiovascular death with competing events. Health status is dropped because it is not available in national linked data (adversarial check).
- Causal contrast: per-protocol effect, estimated as the risk difference and risk ratio at 1, 2 and 3 years from cloned, censored and weighted data; and the hazard ratio as a secondary summary.
- Confounders: age, sex, ischaemic aetiology, LVEF, LV volumes, EROA or regurgitant volume, right ventricular function, PASP, atrial fibrillation, kidney function, natriuretic peptides and NYHA class where held, heart failure admission history, frailty indices, device therapy and medication classes, with time-varying updates after time zero.
- Analysis: clone-censor-weight with inverse probability of censoring weights fitted on time-varying covariates; pooled logistic or Cox models with bootstrap confidence intervals; stratification by excluded phenotype with an overall estimate; E-values and a quantitative bias analysis for unmeasured confounding by anatomical suitability and frailty.
- Benchmarking: before any analysis in the excluded strata, emulate COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 in the same dataset and check that the published effect estimates are recovered (PMID 30280640 HR 0.53 for heart failure hospitalisation, PMID 39216092 rate ratio 0.64, with their stated intervals). Failure to recover them stops the analysis and is reported.
- Negative-control outcomes (for example non-cardiac admissions unrelated to heart failure) and a positivity check with propensity score overlap plots by stratum.

Power. Strata will be small, so precision is the practical limit. As an illustration with stated inputs, detecting a hazard ratio of 0.72 (the pooled randomised estimate in PMID 41549691) with two-sided alpha 0.05, 80% power and equal groups needs d = 4 x (1.96 + 0.84)^2 / (ln 0.72)^2 = 4 x 7.84 / 0.1079 = about 291 events in the weighted analysis. Weighting and the clone-censor-weight design reduce the effective sample size below the raw count, so the dataset must hold well over 291 events in the pooled excluded population. This is a planning figure for feasibility counting, not a pre-specified calculation.

Alternative design: Bayesian transport of the COAPT and RESHAPE-HF2 effect estimates to the excluded phenotypes with explicit assumptions, as an analysis within a reconstructed-IPD synthesis. Reason it is secondary: transportability analyses rest on conditional exchangeability and on trial participation positivity, which cannot hold for phenotypes with no trial representation (PMID 42233929 states these assumptions).

Why a weaker design would not do: a registry-only comparison of eligible versus ineligible treated patients (PMID 38267110, PMID 34886961) cannot estimate the effect of TEER, and single-arm series (PMID 40713981, PMID 42600559) have no comparator.

## 7. Data and collaborators

Required: population-level echocardiography with MR grade and LV dimensions, right ventricular function and PASP; linked hospital admissions and mortality; medication records; TEER procedure codes; LVAD and transplant records.

Candidate sources, taken from the store and not yet confirmed as accessible:
- Large echocardiography-linked community or national data, of the kind described in the Viennese cohort of 13,223 patients with secondary MR (PMID 34193442), which includes patients who did not receive an intervention.
- TVT Registry linked to claims for treated patients and a medically managed comparator (PMID 38267110, PMID 42233929).
- EuroSMR (PMID 34886961), OCEAN-Mitral and REVEAL-AFMR (PMID 40629531), GIOTTO (PMID 41065256) and EXPANDed (PMID 40713981) for treated cohorts; REVEAL-AFMR shows that a medically managed registry sharing a definition with a TEER registry exists in atrial MR, and an analogue for ventricular MR would be sought.

What the owner already holds: none of these datasets. The owner's UK Biobank extract and the Harefield microaxial pump registry and primary PCI cohort do not contain the required echocardiographic and TEER data. Planned research visits in Denmark, Germany and the USA are the plausible route to a linked echocardiography source and to a collaborator with untreated cohorts.

## 8. Result scenarios

- TEER associated with a lower 3-year risk of death or heart failure hospitalisation in one or more excluded strata after the benchmark is recovered. Publishable in the target journal because it would support extending use to patients currently excluded, with the first comparative estimate against medical therapy.
- No difference or imprecise estimates in the extreme strata with narrow intervals excluding large benefit. Publishable because it would give a basis for restraint in patients in whom registries show worse outcomes and single-arm data suggest limited remodelling (PMID 41878851), and it would inform the trials listed in section 4.
- Benchmark not recovered. This is a reportable methodological finding that the data cannot support emulation of these trials; it would be reported with the dataset description and would probably not reach a target-tier journal.
- Heterogeneity by phenotype, for example benefit with LVEF below 20% but not with markedly enlarged ventricles. Publishable as the first comparative evidence that could explain the heterogeneity seen across the three trials (I2 80.3%, PMID 41549691), within the limits of observational data.

Observational findings will be reported as associations.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Data access is the main risk. A linked national dataset with quantified echocardiography is rare, and the work cannot start without one. De-risk through a feasibility count of patients meeting each excluded criterion and the number of events in untreated eligible patients before committing.
- Confounding by indication and anatomical suitability, which is not recorded in untreated patients, cannot be removed by weighting on recorded covariates. Mitigation: benchmarking, negative controls, quantitative bias analysis, and a clear statement that findings are associations.
- A null or harmful result in the group least likely to benefit would be dismissed as confounding by indication, and a positive result as residual confounding (adversarial objection 3).
- Small strata and wide intervals could leave the paper hypothesis-generating and suited to a specialty-tier journal (adversarial objection 6).
- A 90-day grace period in advanced heart failure with high early mortality needs strong time-varying covariates such as natriuretic peptides, NYHA class and worsening heart failure events, which national datasets seldom hold.
- Competing events (LVAD, transplant, non-cardiac death) must be reflected in the estimand (PMID 41065256).
- Scoop risk: moderate. The topic is active in JACC and the European Heart Journal (PMID 42233929, PMID 40629531, PMID 42600559), and the TVT Registry teams could extend the transportability work with an untreated comparator. The trials in section 4 complete in 2030 or later and do not scoop the question.
