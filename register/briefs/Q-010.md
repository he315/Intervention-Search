# Q-010: Dobutamine versus milrinone as first-line inotrope in heart failure-related and infarct-related cardiogenic shock, tested in a registry-embedded, phenotype-stratified randomised domain with a pre-specified prospective meta-analysis of the vasopressor and placebo-controlled trials

Written 24 October 2026. Status: candidate. Composite score 73.0.

## Narrowing from the original question

The register title for Q-010 is "First-line inotrope and vasopressor strategy in SCAI stage C to D cardiogenic shock: registry-embedded multi-arm randomised trial". The independent adversarial check of 24 October 2026 found that framing too broad, and this brief is built on the narrower angle that survived it. Three changes follow.

1. The vasopressor arms are dropped as arms of the new trial. Noradrenaline against adrenaline is under test in PANDA (planned 1,155 patients, PMID 41679570) and noradrenaline against dopamine in ACTION-CS (NCT07562932). A fourth randomised comparison of the same agents would be incremental.
2. The randomised question is restricted to the inotrope-agent comparison (dobutamine against milrinone), analysed separately in heart failure-related and infarct-related shock. The vasopressor and placebo-controlled trials enter through a pre-specified prospective meta-analysis with harmonised outcomes, not through new arms.
3. The observational "usual care" reference arm is dropped, because confounding by indication is what randomisation removes. A target trial emulation is retained only as a feasibility step.

## 1. Question

In adults with cardiogenic shock (SCAI stage C or D) due to acute myocardial infarction or to acute or acute-on-chronic heart failure, who need a first inotrope or inodilator, does starting milrinone rather than dobutamine change all-cause mortality at 30 days, analysed separately in each phenotype, in a randomised domain embedded in a national shock registry?

- Population: adults with cardiogenic shock requiring an inotrope or inodilator, stratified by infarct-related and heart failure-related aetiology.
- Intervention: milrinone as the first inotrope.
- Comparator: dobutamine as the first inotrope.
- Outcomes: all-cause mortality at 30 days (primary); refractory shock, ventricular and atrial arrhythmia, renal replacement therapy, and escalation to mechanical circulatory support (secondary).
- Timing: at the first prescription of an inotrope or inodilator.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision. Every patient with cardiogenic shock who needs a first inotrope receives one of a small set of agents, and the choice is made by the treating intensivist or cardiologist without comparative mortality data. A clinical primer published in Circulation on 25 August 2026 states that medical management with vasoactive agents is the initial strategy in virtually all patients with cardiogenic shock and describes the available comparative effectiveness data as the basis for a selection framework (PMID 42636314). The NORshock design paper states that noradrenaline is the guideline-recommended first-line vasopressor (PMID 42703766). Guideline classes and levels for inotropes have not been extracted in this system and none is stated here.

Population size. The stored sources give registry denominators, not a UK incidence. The American Heart Association Cardiogenic Shock Registry holds 6,847 admissions across 84 sites from 2022 to 2024, of which 3,460 (50.5%) received two or more vasoactive agents, and dobutamine was used in 31.6% (PMID 41147380). The Critical Care Cardiology Trials Network analysis holds 3,282 admissions from 37 cardiac intensive care units from 2019 to 2023 (PMID 42093634). A UK annual incidence was not retrieved and is not estimated here.

Practice variation. In the Critical Care Cardiology Trials Network cohort, 35.3% of the variation in the choice of dobutamine against milrinone was attributed to patient-level factors and 32.6% to the random effect of the individual unit (PMID 42093634). The American Heart Association registry reports greater inodilator use in acute-on-chronic heart failure shock and greater inopressor use in infarct-related shock (PMID 41147380). Variation of this size between units, unexplained by patient factors, is what makes cluster or individual randomisation inside a registry credible.

## 3. What is known

Randomised evidence.
- PMID 34347952 (NEJM, 2021, registered as NCT03207165): 192 participants, milrinone against dobutamine, double-blind. Primary composite 49% against 54% (relative risk 0.90, 95% CI 0.69 to 1.19); in-hospital death 37% against 43% (relative risk 0.85, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.21). The registry entry for NCT03207165 is titled as a heterogeneous population of critically ill patients, so the result is not specific to either phenotype. The trial was not sized for mortality.
- PMID 29976291 (JACC, 2018, NCT01367743, OptimaCC): 57 patients with infarct-related shock, epinephrine against norepinephrine. Refractory shock occurred in 10 of 27 (37%) against 2 of 30 (7%) (p = 0.008), and the study stopped early. This is a vasopressor comparison, outside the narrowed question.
- PMID 41324946 (JAMA, 2026, NCT04728932, LEVOECMO): 205 randomised patients on VA-ECMO, levosimendan against placebo; weaning 68.3% in each arm (risk difference 0.0%, 95% CI -12.8% to 12.7%); ventricular arrhythmias 17.8% against 8.7%. Not a first-line comparison.

Syntheses.
- PMID 41737078 (Cureus, 2026): network meta-analysis of 14 studies (N = 5,157; six randomised, eight observational) in infarct-related shock. Milrinone against dobutamine in-hospital mortality odds ratio 0.90 (95% CI 0.69 to 1.19); the abstract reports high certainty for that contrast, which sits uneasily with a single 192-patient trial as its randomised base.
- PMID 41978758 (Global Cardiology Science and Practice, 2026): dobutamine against milrinone, in-hospital mortality odds ratio 1.56 (95% CI 1.01 to 2.39) overall, 1.24 (95% CI 0.70 to 2.19) in randomised studies and 1.63 (95% CI 1.02 to 2.59) in observational studies. The divergence between study types is the signature of confounding by indication.
- PMID 41377633 (Circulation Reports, 2025): two randomised trials (n = 337) of noradrenaline against other vasopressors, very low certainty.
- PMID 36195825 (Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia, 2022): network meta-analysis of 17 randomised trials in infarct-related shock; milrinone odds ratio 0.52 (95% CI 0.19 to 1.39) and dobutamine 0.67 (95% CI 0.30 to 1.49) against placebo, both low certainty; only two vasopressor trials, too few to pool.

Observational comparisons.
- PMID 40900181 (Clinical Research in Cardiology, 2025): 739 patients with infarct-related shock; before matching 30-day mortality 50.6% with milrinone against 41.5% with dobutamine (p = 0.018), after matching 46.5% against 41.9% (p = 0.362). Milrinone patients were sicker at baseline.
- PMID 40960311 (Annals of the American Thoracic Society, 2026): 10,447 patients with shock and sepsis in a claims database; hospital mortality 41.6% against 42.7% (risk difference -1.4%, 95% CI -3.7% to 1.5%).
- PMID 39415431 (European Heart Journal - Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, 2025): SWEDEHEART, 16,214 patients, inotropes against none, adjusted hazard ratio 1.72 (95% CI 1.26 to 2.35) for 30-day mortality. This asks inotrope against no inotrope and not agent against agent.

Phenotype and staging context. The Cardiogenic Shock Working Group registry reports that 57% of 3,268 patients had heart failure-related shock and 27% infarct-related shock (PMID 39217545), and that stage definitions track mortality (PMID 35835491). These support stratification by aetiology and SCAI stage at randomisation.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: underpowered trials; strategy question that industry is unlikely to fund. Both agents are generic.

- G1 Consequence: pass. The choice is made for most patients in shock and the outcome is death at 30 days.
- G2 Equipoise: pass for the inotrope contrast. The only randomised comparison was not sized for mortality (PMID 34347952), and the randomised and observational pooled estimates diverge (PMID 41978758). Equipoise is lower for noradrenaline against adrenaline, which is why those arms are excluded.
- G3 Not about to be answered: conditional pass, as the adversarial check advised. Restricted to dobutamine against milrinone, no adequately powered randomised comparison is registered in the store, and DOREMI-2 (NCT05267886) tests inotrope against placebo. The vasopressor arms would fail this gate because of PANDA and ACTION-CS, which is the reason they are excluded. No target- or general-tier meta-analysis of the inotrope contrast was found; the syntheses are in lower-tier journals (PMIDs 41737078, 41978758, 36195825).
- G4 Credible design: pass. A registry-embedded randomised domain is citable by a guideline committee. Observational estimates would not be, because milrinone patients were sicker at baseline (PMID 40900181) and the randomised and observational pooled estimates differ (PMID 41978758).
- G5 Not incremental: conditional pass. It holds only if the design stays domain-structured, drops the observational reference arm and is powered for mortality by phenotype. A trial of the vasopressor arms already under test would be incremental.

## 6. Design

Primary design: a randomised domain within a national cardiogenic shock registry, with individual patient randomisation at the first inotrope or inodilator prescription, open-label for drug allocation with blinded outcome adjudication from registry linkage. This is a domain of a multi-domain platform, so the vasopressor domain can later be added without a new infrastructure.

- Eligibility: adults with cardiogenic shock of SCAI stage C or D (registry-recorded hypotension or hypoperfusion on vasoactive support), aetiology infarct-related or heart failure-related, needing a first inotrope or inodilator. Exclusions: post-cardiotomy and cardiac arrest with stage E, as the phenotypes in which agents may behave differently and where recruitment would be least tractable; contraindication to either agent.
- Arms: milrinone against dobutamine at registry-standardised starting doses with titration left to the clinician.
- Randomisation: stratified by aetiology (infarct-related, heart failure-related), SCAI stage (C, D) and enrolling centre, using the registry's electronic case report form.
- Primary endpoint: all-cause mortality at 30 days, obtained from registry linkage to national death records. 30-day mortality is the co-primary or primary endpoint in the registered shock trials in this brief (PANDA, 28-day; LevoHeartShock, 30-day), which supports harmonisation.
- Secondary endpoints: refractory shock, ventricular and atrial arrhythmia, renal replacement therapy, mechanical circulatory support escalation, length of stay, 12-month mortality.
- Analysis: intention to treat; Bayesian hierarchical model across the two phenotypes with the stratum-specific posterior probability of benefit reported alongside the frequentist estimate; the pre-specified stopping rules and any response-adaptive allocation fixed in the protocol before enrolment. Response-adaptive allocation is optional and would be used only if the monitoring committee judges the outcome stable enough.
- Crossover and co-treatment: 50.5% of admissions in the American Heart Association registry received two or more vasoactive agents (PMID 41147380), so early addition of a second agent is expected. It is handled by an intention-to-treat primary analysis, and by recording the dose and duration of every vasoactive agent in the registry fields.
- Prospective meta-analysis: a protocol, registered on PROSPERO before DOREMI-2 reports (primary completion registered as 2026-12), pre-specifying 30-day mortality, refractory shock and arrhythmia as shared outcomes across this domain, DOREMI-2, LevoHeartShock and the vasopressor trials. Effect measure: risk ratio with a random-effects model, REML estimation of tau-squared and the Hartung-Knapp adjustment; zero-event cells handled with a continuity correction of 0.5 in sensitivity analysis and a binomial-normal model as the primary approach where events are sparse. Because the contrasts differ (agent against agent, agent against placebo, vasopressor against vasopressor), pooling would be within contrast and a network meta-analysis would be used across them only after transitivity checks on aetiology, stage and baseline vasopressor use, with design-by-treatment inconsistency testing. This synthesis falls within the owner's established methods and can be started now, ahead of any funding.

Sample size, with inputs.
- Control-arm risk: 43% in-hospital death with dobutamine (PMID 34347952).
- Treatment effect: 37% in-hospital death with milrinone, a relative risk of 0.85 (PMID 34347952, the abstract gives 0.85 with 95% CI 0.60 to 1.21). Milrinone risk under the assumption is 0.43 x 0.85 = 0.3655.
- Assumption (not from a source): in-hospital death is a proxy for 30-day mortality; two-sided alpha 0.05; power 80%.
- Formula: n per arm = (1.96 + 0.84)^2 x [0.43 x 0.57 + 0.3655 x 0.6345] / (0.43 - 0.3655)^2 = 7.84 x (0.2451 + 0.2319) / 0.004160, which gives 900 per arm and 1,800 per phenotype.
- Dilution assumption (not from a source): 10% crossover or non-adherence dilutes the effect to a relative risk of 0.865, giving 1,115 per arm and 2,230 per phenotype. Powering both phenotypes separately would need about 4,460 patients.
- Sensitivity: with a relative risk of 0.90 (the primary composite ratio in PMID 34347952) and the same control risk, 2,048 per arm and 4,095 per phenotype are needed at 80% power. This is why the trial is a large registry-embedded design and not a conventional one, and why a Bayesian hierarchical model that borrows strength across phenotypes is proposed to reduce the total.
- The observed relative risk of 0.85 is itself a wide estimate (95% CI 0.60 to 1.21) and is likely optimistic; the calculation illustrates scale and the protocol would use a value agreed with a clinical advisory group.

Registry platform. The trial would be registered on ISRCTN and mirrored on ClinicalTrials.gov, with randomisation and data capture on the BCIS AMI cardiogenic shock registry, extended to heart failure-related shock through linkage to the ICNARC cardiogenic shock registry.

Alternative design: target trial emulation in linked BCIS and ICNARC data as a feasibility and sample-size step, not as the answer.
- Eligibility: as above.
- Treatment strategies: initiate milrinone, or dobutamine, within 6 hours of the first vasoactive prescription (the American Heart Association registry used a 6-hour window, PMID 41147380).
- Time zero: the first prescription of either agent; immortal time avoided by assigning time zero at initiation.
- Outcomes: 30-day all-cause mortality and the secondary outcomes above.
- Confounders: age, aetiology, SCAI stage, lactate, vasopressor dose, cardiac arrest, renal function, mechanical support, and centre; centre-level preference as an instrument for the sensitivity analysis, following the approach in PMID 39415431.
- Estimand: per-protocol effect of initiating each strategy, with clone-censor-weight methods for treatment switching.
- Analysis: inverse probability weighting and doubly robust estimation, with negative control outcomes.
- Reason it is not the primary design: in the observational literature milrinone patients were sicker at baseline (PMID 40900181) and estimates diverge from randomised ones (PMID 41978758).

## 8. Result scenarios

- Milrinone lowers mortality in one phenotype and not the other: a phenotype-specific recommendation, publishable in a target journal as the first adequately powered randomised agent comparison in shock and as evidence of effect modification that the 192-patient trial could not test (PMID 34347952).
- No difference in either phenotype with a narrow credible interval: this would settle a question that currently rests on one underpowered trial and divergent observational estimates (PMID 41978758), and would let practice follow cost, availability and adverse-event profile. A well-powered null on a near-universal decision is publishable in a target journal.
- Dobutamine is superior, or milrinone increases arrhythmia or renal replacement therapy: the observational signal in PMID 41978758 and the secondary findings in PMID 40960311 (longer inotrope duration, more antiarrhythmic use with milrinone) would receive randomised confirmation or refutation, with direct safety implications.
- Inconclusive after early stopping rules: the harmonised data still feed the prospective meta-analysis, so the work yields a publishable synthesis even if the trial does not.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Scoop risk: moderate. DOREMI-2 reports around December 2026 and PANDA about 2029; neither tests dobutamine against milrinone, but a large inotrope-versus-placebo result would reshape equipoise. Mitigation: pre-specify the prospective meta-analysis now and review the DOREMI-2 protocol.
- Editor risk: a platform from a single national system may be directed to a specialty journal before results exist. Mitigation: publish the prospective meta-analysis protocol and the trial results in a parent journal, and keep the vasopressor and placebo-controlled trials in the synthesis.
- Heterogeneity: aetiologies may respond differently; the design stratifies and reports each phenotype.
- Open-label bias and crossover: addressed through intention to treat, blinded outcome ascertainment and registry-recorded dose and duration.
- Platform risk: the UK platform has not been verified to exist or to be funded. If it does not materialise, the fallback is the prospective meta-analysis plus the target trial emulation.
- Emergency consent: needs an approved deferred-consent pathway; PANDA uses paramedic randomisation (PMID 41679570), which shows the approach exists in a related setting.
- Effect size risk: the sample size depends on a point estimate with a wide interval (PMID 34347952).

Adversarial check summary (24 October 2026). The independent review found no target- or general-tier synthesis answering the agent comparison; the only target-family analysis addressed inotrope against none (PMID 39415431), and the randomised milrinone trial was underpowered for mortality (PMID 34347952). It concluded the question survives with a narrower angle, because noradrenaline against adrenaline and against dopamine are under test in PANDA and ACTION-CS, and inotrope against placebo in DOREMI-2 and LevoHeartShock. Editor objections were that the question is not new to the literature, that a single-system platform paper is hard to place in a parent journal, that the all-cause shock population is heterogeneous, that the observational reference arm adds little, and that novelty depends on the arms. Design objections were that vasopressors and inodilators answer different decisions, that a sample size calculation was missing, that blinding and crossover are difficult, and that the UK platform could not be verified. The narrowing and the sample size calculation above respond to these points; the platform and consent objections remain open risks. Full report: register/briefs/Q-010_adversarial.md.
