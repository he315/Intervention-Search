# Q-001: Do the effects of routine revascularisation on myocardial infarction and death, compared with initial medical therapy in chronic coronary syndrome, change over follow-up? A time-varying (restricted mean survival time and flexible parametric) synthesis of long-follow-up randomised trials

Narrowing, stated openly. The register title frames this as a reconstructed-IPD network meta-analysis with prespecified effect modification by ischaemia burden, anatomy and LV function. The independent adversarial check (register/briefs/Q-001_adversarial.md, 24 October 2026) found that framing too broad and partly occupied. This brief is built on the narrowed question: the time-varying effect of revascularisation on all-cause death, cardiac death and myocardial infarction in randomised trials with long follow-up, analysed separately for PCI and CABG where the data allow, with effect modification limited to what trial-level or published subgroup data can support, and with an IPD route (ISCHEMIA, REVIVED-BCIS2, STICHES) held as the planned upgrade.

## 1. Question in one sentence

In adults with chronic coronary syndrome randomised to routine revascularisation (PCI or CABG) plus medical therapy or to initial medical therapy alone, how do the hazard ratio and the restricted mean survival time difference for all-cause death, cardiac death and non-procedural myocardial infarction evolve from randomisation to the longest available follow-up (up to 15 years)?

- Population: adults with chronic coronary syndrome in randomised trials with at least 5 years of reported follow-up.
- Intervention: routine revascularisation (PCI, including FFR-guided PCI, or CABG) plus medical therapy.
- Comparator: initial medical therapy alone.
- Outcomes: all-cause death, cardiac death, spontaneous (non-procedural) MI, with procedural MI reported separately.
- Timing: landmarks at 1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 years, and the full observed range.

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision is whether to offer revascularisation for prognosis, and not only for symptoms, in stable coronary disease, and how to counsel patients on an early procedural hazard set against later spontaneous-MI benefit. It is made by interventional cardiologists, heart teams and guideline committees.

- Population size: not retrieved. The store holds no registry or national statistic on annual elective PCI or CABG volumes, so none is quoted.
- Practice variation: not retrieved from the store, and none is asserted.
- Two pooled analyses of the same decision disagree. A meta-analysis of 14 trials and 14,877 patients reported death RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.90-1.09) and non-procedural MI RR 0.76 (0.67-0.85), with procedural MI RR 2.48 (1.86-3.31) (PMID 32794407). A meta-analysis of 25 trials and 19,806 patients reported cardiac death RR 0.79 (0.67-0.93) and spontaneous MI RR 0.74 (0.64-0.86), with a cardiac death effect that was linearly associated with follow-up duration (PMID 34002203). The authors of the first called for longer follow-up of trials (PMID 32794407).
- Longer follow-up has since appeared. ISCHEMIA extended follow-up reported 7-year all-cause mortality of 12.7% versus 13.4% (adjusted HR 1.00, 95% CI 0.85-1.18), cardiovascular mortality of 6.4% versus 8.6% (adjusted HR 0.78, 0.63-0.96) and non-cardiovascular mortality of 5.6% versus 4.4% (adjusted HR 1.44, 1.08-1.91) (PMID 36335918). FAME 2 long-term follow-up, at a median of 11.2 years, reported a win ratio of 1.25 (95% CI 1.01-1.56) favouring PCI, driven by urgent revascularisation, with a win ratio of 0.88 for all-cause death (0.66-1.17) and 1.50 for myocardial infarction (0.98-2.31) (PMID 41540107).

## 3. What is known

- PMID 32794407 (Circulation, 2020): aggregate meta-analysis, 14 trials, 14,877 patients, weighted mean follow-up 4.5 years. Death RR 0.99 (0.90-1.09). No time-varying modelling.
- PMID 34002203 (European Heart Journal, 2021): 25 trials, 19,806 patients. Cardiac death RR 0.79 (0.67-0.93); all-cause mortality 0.94 (0.87-1.01), P = 0.11. Meta-regression on follow-up duration. A European Heart Journal commentary questioned its biases (PMID 34110408).
- PMID 32227755 (NEJM, 2020), ISCHEMIA: 5179 patients, median 3.2 years. Death HR 1.05 (0.83-1.32). The abstract states that findings were sensitive to the definition of myocardial infarction.
- PMID 36335918 (Circulation, 2023), ISCHEMIA extended follow-up: results above. The abstract reports no heterogeneity of treatment effect in prespecified subgroups.
- PMID 36027563 (NEJM, 2022), REVIVED-BCIS2: 700 patients with LVEF 35% or less. Primary composite of death or heart failure hospitalisation HR 0.99 (0.78-1.27) over a median of 41 months.
- PMID 38015442 (NEJM, 2023), ORBITA-2: 301 patients randomised in the abstract; angina symptom score outcome at 12 weeks. A symptom trial, so it contributes little to time-to-event synthesis.
- PMID 41540107 (Nature Medicine, 2026), FAME 2 long-term: results above.
- PMID 40139890 (JACC, 2025), ISCHEMIA CTO analysis: in participants with CTO, 5-year difference in cardiovascular death or MI -3.5% (95% CI -7.8% to 0.8%), more procedural MI (2.5%; 1.0%-4.0%) and fewer spontaneous MI (-6.3%; -9.7% to -3.2%).
- PMID 30596995 (European Heart Journal, 2019): IPD meta-analysis of three trials of FFR-guided PCI versus medical therapy, 2400 patients. Cardiac death or MI HR 0.72 (0.54-0.96), driven by MI. Two of the three trials enrolled non-culprit lesions after acute coronary syndrome.
- PMID 40048661 (European Heart Journal, 2025): IPD analysis of REVIVED-BCIS2 and STICHES, 1912 participants. Occupies the ischaemic LV dysfunction stratum with long follow-up (median 118 months in STICHES).
- PMID 37949356 (Hellenic Journal of Cardiology, 2023): 28 trials, 20,692 patients. Cardiovascular mortality RR 0.79 (0.69-0.90); follow-up duration a significant moderator (p = 0.001).
- PMID 39166630 (European Journal of Clinical Investigation, 2024): 19 trials, 8616 patients. CV death OR 0.77 (0.62-0.96); benefit larger beyond 3 years (p = 0.04).
- PMID 36265647 (International Journal of Cardiology, 2023): network meta-analysis of 18 trials. CABG IRR for cardiovascular death 0.76 (0.64-0.89) and for all-cause death 0.87 (0.77-0.99); this already occupies an aggregate network structure.
- PMID 41210668 (International Journal of Angiology, 2025): 10 trials, 14,171 participants. PMID 41130952 (Critical Pathways in Cardiology, 2026): 28 trials, MI OR 0.84 (0.74-0.96). Both aggregate and lower-tier.
- PMID 41949523 (JACC, 2026): post-hoc pooled analysis of EUROCTO and DECISION-CTO, 518 patients; cardiac death or non-fatal MI 2.7% versus 5.1% (P = 0.17) over a mean of 3.1 years. A CTO subset only.

Not covered by the store and so not cited: other 2023 and 2025 syntheses named in the adversarial report as out-of-store publications. The adversarial report states they exist, but this brief does not rely on them.

Guideline positions have not been extracted for this question, and no class or level is stated here.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: conflicting trials, with an overlay of evidence outdated by long-term follow-up.

- G1 Consequence, pass. PCI and CABG for stable disease are common procedures, and the endpoints are death and MI.
- G2 Equipoise, pass on trial evidence only. Pooled analyses disagree on cardiac death (PMIDs 32794407, 34002203, 37949356, 39166630). Guideline extracts have not been retrieved, so guideline silence or discordance is unassessed.
- G3 Not about to be answered, pass with annotation. No adequately powered whole-population RCT is expected within 24 months (section 4). The last target-journal aggregate synthesis of this decision that the adversarial review could verify is PMID 32794407, published in 2020 and so more than 24 months old, but several lower-tier syntheses since 2023 overlap it (PMIDs 37949356, 39166630, 41210668, 41130952, 36265647). The pooled-evidence test therefore requires the first analysis step to state how much new follow-up and how many new events the reconstruction adds. That quantity has not been computed and no figure is offered.
- G4 Credible design, pass. Restricted mean survival time and flexible parametric models address non-proportional hazards, which fixed relative risks cannot. A conventional meta-analysis would repeat the existing disagreement.
- G5 Not incremental, conditional. The adversarial check recorded this gate as at risk. The aggregate pooled comparison is close to a repeat of existing meta-analyses of the same trials. The case rests on the time-varying estimand and the long-term follow-up data, and the question should be dropped if the first step shows that the added follow-up is modest.

## 6. Design

Primary design: reconstructed-IPD meta-analysis of randomised trials of routine revascularisation versus initial medical therapy, with time-varying estimands. Why this and not a weaker design: a repeat aggregate meta-analysis would restate the disagreement between PMIDs 32794407 and 34002203 without resolving whether effects depend on follow-up time.

Eligibility and scope
- Randomised trials of routine PCI or CABG plus medical therapy versus initial medical therapy in chronic coronary syndrome, with at least 5 years of reported follow-up and published Kaplan-Meier curves with numbers at risk.
- Excluded: symptom-only or sham-controlled short trials (PMID 38015442 contributes no time-to-event data), and non-culprit lesion trials in STEMI, which belong to the owner's active STEMI project. This also removes two of the three trials in PMID 30596995 from scope.
- The CTO trials (NCT01760083, NCT01078051) and the LV dysfunction trials (NCT01920048) are analysed as separate strata, not pooled into the headline.

Effect measures
- Primary: difference and ratio in restricted mean survival time at 5, 7 and 10 years, for all-cause death and for the composite of cardiac death or non-procedural MI where curves exist.
- Secondary: time-varying hazard ratio curves from flexible parametric (Royston-Parmar) one-stage models with a trial-stratified baseline hazard and a time-by-treatment interaction; piecewise hazard ratios before and after 2 and 5 years; and the crossing point of cumulative incidence curves for procedural and spontaneous MI.
- Death as a competing risk for MI and cardiac death: report cumulative incidence functions, not Kaplan-Meier complements.

Reconstruction method
- Digitise curves and numbers at risk, then reconstruct individual times and events with the iterative algorithm of Guyot and colleagues, applying it separately by arm and outcome.
- Check each reconstruction against the published event totals and hazard ratios; exclude any curve whose reconstructed hazard ratio departs from the reported one beyond a prespecified tolerance, and report the exclusions.
- Where numbers at risk are absent, treat the curve as a sensitivity-only input, not a primary one.

Pooling
- Where a trial-level summary is needed, use random-effects with REML estimation of tau-squared and the Hartung-Knapp adjustment, with prediction intervals.
- Zero events: for any trial-level count outcomes in the sensitivity analysis, use no continuity correction in the primary model and a treatment-arm-specific correction as sensitivity, or a beta-binomial model; the reconstructed time-to-event models do not require a correction.
- Heterogeneity and transitivity: PCI and CABG are analysed in separate pairwise comparisons against medical therapy. A network over PCI, CABG and medical therapy is retained only if transitivity checks (era, stent generation, statin use, LV function, left main exclusion) support it, because PMID 36265647 already shows that era and population confound the comparison. Consistency is assessed by node-splitting where a loop exists.

Effect modification
- Limited to between-trial meta-regression on pre-specified trial characteristics (ischaemia criterion, proportion with multivessel disease, LVEF inclusion, era, stent type) and to subgroup curves that trials actually publish. The brief records openly that this is ecological, low in power and confounded by era, and does not claim individual-level modification.

Definitions
- Map each trial's MI definition to spontaneous or procedural a priori. PMID 32227755 states that its primary outcome was sensitive to the MI definition, so results are reported under both the trial-reported and a harmonised definition.

Alternative design: a one-stage IPD meta-analysis with the trialists, adding ISCHEMIA, REVIVED-BCIS2 and STICHES participants. This would support true effect modification by ischaemia, anatomy and LV function, as the IPD analyses in PMIDs 30596995 and 40048661 did for subsets. It requires a consortium and is the preferred upgrade if data sharing is agreed.

Registry platform: PROSPERO, registered before data extraction, with the RMST and landmark times fixed in the protocol.

## 8. Result scenarios and why each is publishable

- Benefit emerges after about 5 years for cardiac death or spontaneous MI with no all-cause effect: reconciles PMIDs 32794407 and 34002203 and gives a counselling horizon. Publishable as a resolution of a documented disagreement in a target journal.
- No late benefit and persisting early procedural MI excess: settles the question in favour of symptom-led indication, which is informative for guidelines.
- Divergent PCI and CABG patterns, with CABG showing late benefit and PCI not: supports separate counselling, though the transitivity limit applies.
- Estimates too imprecise to distinguish models: the question is then parked and the IPD route pursued; this is a failure of the aggregate route, stated in advance.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Novelty is the principal risk: many aggregate syntheses exist, and two already report a follow-up-duration effect on cardiovascular death (PMIDs 37949356, 39166630). De-risk by quantifying the added follow-up and events at the start, and by dropping the question if the gain is small.
- Reconstruction of cardiac death and spontaneous MI is unreliable where trials do not publish separate curves. De-risk by restricting the primary analysis to all-cause death and to outcomes with published curves, and by reporting the number of trials contributing to each outcome.
- Outcome definition heterogeneity across eras; mitigated by dual definitions (section 6).
- Reviewers may prefer trialist IPD to reconstruction, as PMIDs 30596995 and 40048661 illustrate. The upgrade path addresses this.
- Scoop risk: moderate. Aggregate syntheses appear at a steady rate in lower-tier journals. A target-journal paper would require the time-varying method or trialist data, which are not yet in the store. NCT03392415 and the 2028 readouts (NCT07349979, NCT05828719) are future updates, not threats within 24 months.
