# Q-025: Withdrawal or continuation of ACE inhibitors and ARBs beyond one year after myocardial infarction with preserved ejection fraction, including older and frail patients and patient-reported burden: a registry-based randomised non-inferiority trial

Narrowing, stated openly. The register line was first framed as a plain continuation-versus-withdrawal trial in low-risk survivors. An independent adversarial check on 24 October 2026 found no randomised trial, no systematic review and no registered ongoing trial of the comparison, but found observational work that already points towards equivalence. It concluded that a trial confirming those findings in a similar low-risk cohort would be incremental. The question is therefore narrowed to a trial that (a) includes patients aged 75 or over, people with frailty and people on multiple drugs, who were outside the observational analysis the adversarial report describes, (b) carries patient-reported burden as a gated co-primary outcome, and (c) is built so that further post-MI drugs can be added as withdrawal arms. The guideline position has not been extracted and no class or level is stated anywhere in this brief.

## 1. Question

In patients who are at least one year after myocardial infarction, have a left ventricular ejection fraction of 50% or more, no heart failure and no other compelling indication for renin-angiotensin system inhibition, including those aged 75 or over and those with frailty or polypharmacy, does discontinuation of an ACE inhibitor or ARB, compared with continuation, preserve a composite of death, myocardial infarction, stroke or heart failure hospitalisation (non-inferiority) and reduce patient-reported treatment burden (superiority, tested only if non-inferiority is shown), over three years of registry-linked follow-up?

## 2. Why it matters now

The decision is made at every long-term review after myocardial infarction, by cardiologists and general practitioners, and concerns whether to keep a drug that may no longer be needed in a low-risk survivor.

Population size. The store holds no incidence figure for this exact group. The nearest sourced figure is from the SWEDEHEART cohort study (PMID 41186482, European Heart Journal - Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, published 2 February 2026), which included 79,968 first-MI patients discharged with a new RAAS inhibitor prescription between 2006 and 2021 in Sweden alone. It is a count of treated patients in one country and not an estimate of the eligible population.

Practice variation. The same study reported discontinuation of 12% to 14% at one year, 27% to 37% at five years and 36% to 51% at 12 years across the four drug classes it examined (statins, beta-blockers, aspirin and RAAS inhibitors; the abstract does not give RAAS inhibitors separately for these figures), and reinitiation after discontinuation of 28% to 46% at one year. Discontinuation is therefore already happening without trial evidence, and reinitiation will contaminate any withdrawal arm.

Timeliness. Open-label strategy trials have recently tested beta-blocker use and interruption after infarction (PMID 38587241, 40888702, 40888716, 39213187, 41910427), and a pooled individual-patient analysis of two interruption trials has just appeared in The Lancet (PMID 42669300, 19 September 2026). The same logic is now being applied to other post-MI drugs, and RAAS inhibitors are the obvious next candidate. The window for a trial of this kind to be first is open but will narrow.

## 3. What is known

- PMID 38587241 (N Engl J Med, 18 April 2024): REDUCE-AMI, registry-based, open-label, 5020 patients with LVEF of at least 50%, beta-blocker against none, primary composite hazard ratio 0.96 (95% CI 0.79 to 1.16). It is the design template for the registry-based approach.
- PMID 40888702 (N Engl J Med, 13 November 2025): 8438 patients analysed, LVEF above 40%, hazard ratio 1.04 (95% CI 0.89 to 1.22).
- PMID 40888716 (N Engl J Med, 13 November 2025): 5574 patients, LVEF of at least 40%, hazard ratio 0.85 (95% CI 0.75 to 0.98).
- PMID 41211954 (N Engl J Med, 5 February 2026): individual-patient meta-analysis of five trials, 17,801 patients, hazard ratio 0.97 (95% CI 0.87 to 1.07). The primary composite occurred in 717 of 8831 (8.1%) and 748 of 8970 (8.3%) patients.
- PMID 39213187 (N Engl J Med, 10 October 2024): ABYSS, 3698 randomised, interruption of long-term beta-blocker was not shown to be non-inferior (hazard ratio 1.16, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.33; P = 0.44 for non-inferiority), and interruption did not seem to improve quality of life.
- PMID 41910427 (N Engl J Med, 2 April 2026): SMART-DECISION, 2540 randomised, discontinuation was non-inferior (hazard ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.57 to 1.13; P = 0.001 for non-inferiority).
- PMID 42669300 (Lancet, 19 September 2026): pooled individual-patient data from ABYSS and SMART-DECISION, 6238 patients, hazard ratio 1.09 (95% CI 0.97 to 1.24; P for non-inferiority 0.016) against a margin of 1.25. The authors note that the primary endpoint is hospitalisation-heavy and trial-dependent.
- PMID 39555814 (N Engl J Med, 13 February 2025): CLEAR, routine spironolactone after MI, 7062 patients, hazard ratio 0.91 (95% CI 0.69 to 1.21) for the first primary outcome.
- PMID 34758252 (N Engl J Med, 11 November 2021): PARADISE-MI, sacubitril-valsartan against ramipril, 5661 patients, hazard ratio 0.90 (95% CI 0.78 to 1.04). It concerns initiation in a reduced-ejection-fraction or congested population, not withdrawal.
- Observational RAAS inhibitor work in the store: PMID 41661197 (Coronary Artery Disease, 1 May 2026), 5017 STEMI patients after DES-PCI, with mortality lower to 12 months (hazard ratio 0.45, 95% CI 0.31 to 0.66) and neutral associations from 12 to 36 months among event-free survivors. PMID 41976977 (Journal of Clinical Medicine, 1 April 2026), a single-centre retrospective cohort of 2530 patients, found discharge RAAS inhibitor use was not associated with all-cause mortality overall but was associated with fewer heart failure rehospitalisations (hazard ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.40 to 0.95). Both are observational and are hypothesis-generating at most.
- A Swedish target trial emulation of ACE inhibitor or ARB use against none after MI with preserved ejection fraction, in people under 75, was reported in the adversarial review as showing no benefit. That publication is not in the store, so it is cited here without an identifier or numbers, and it must be added to the store and re-read before any statement about it is relied on.
- No systematic review or meta-analysis of RAAS inhibitor withdrawal after MI with preserved ejection fraction was found by the adversarial review, and none is in the store.

## 4. What is under way

No registered trial randomises continuation against withdrawal of ACE inhibitors or ARBs after MI. The store holds these nearest entries, none of which answers the question.

- NCT07032389 (PolyACS), polypill strategy after acute coronary syndrome, N 1000 (estimated), primary completion 2030-06-01, win ratio at 6 months. Not a withdrawal comparison.
- NCT03374449 (STOPORNOT), continuation against discontinuation of RAS inhibitors before major surgery, N 2222, primary completion 2023-12-24. Perioperative, not post-MI.
- NCT05271448, continuing or holding ACE inhibitors or ARBs in CKD before coronary angiography, N 600 (estimated), primary completion 2024-06-01, status unknown. Contrast nephropathy outcome.
- NCT07184918 (ACEI-RF), continuing or withholding ramipril around coronary angiography, N 44, primary completion 2025-06-01. Renal function at 72 hours.
- NCT03417388 (WARRIOR), women with non-obstructive coronary disease, N 2476, primary completion 2026-09-14. The strategy includes an ACE inhibitor or ARB but is a different population and comparison.

Beta-blocker trials in the store that are still to report include NCT03778554 (DANBLOCK) and NCT03646357 (BETAMI), but they concern a different drug. Absence of a registered trial does not exclude unregistered work, and ISRCTN and ChiCTR were not searched in the adversarial review.

## 5. Why it is unanswered

Gap type: evidence outdated by a new trial, in the sense that the original ACE inhibitor trials predate modern reperfusion and secondary prevention, and the beta-blocker trials have shown that benefit in this era cannot be assumed. The original ACE inhibitor trials are not in the store, so this reading rests on the argument by analogy and on the beta-blocker trial abstracts.

- G1 Consequence: pass, with a caution. The decision is common and concerns death, MI, stroke and heart failure hospitalisation, but the adversarial review judged consequence per patient modest and the drugs inexpensive, so the case partly rests on burden and polypharmacy in older patients.
- G2 Equipoise: pass, weakened. No randomised withdrawal evidence exists in the store, and the observational signal (PMID 41661197) and the unretrieved Swedish emulation point towards equivalence. Guideline position not extracted, so equipoise is judged on trial and cohort evidence only.
- G3 Not about to be answered: pass. No registered trial of the comparison was found, and no meta-analysis in a target- or general-tier journal covers it. The observational analyses (PMID 41661197, 41976977 and the unretrieved Swedish emulation) are in specialty or other journals and do not trigger the meta-analysis condition.
- G4 Credible design: pass. A registry-based randomised non-inferiority trial would be citable by a guideline committee, and observational data cannot settle withdrawal because of confounding by indication.
- G5 Not incremental: pass with a caution. A trial that merely repeated the low-risk, under-75 population of the observational work would be incremental; the older, frail and patient-reported angle is what keeps it above that bar.

## 6. Design

Primary design: pragmatic, open-label, registry-based randomised non-inferiority trial with blinded or registry-adjudicated outcomes, embedded in a national MI registry with linked prescription, hospitalisation and mortality data, in the manner of REDUCE-AMI (PMID 38587241, protocol PMID 36513329). Registry platform and registration: ISRCTN or ClinicalTrials.gov, to be chosen with the partner registry.

Alternative and first step: target trial emulation of RAAS inhibitor discontinuation at one year in UK Biobank and linked national records, to size event rates in older and frail strata and to check the margin. The emulation does not replace the trial.

Methodological choices for the trial.

- Eligibility: survivors at least one year after MI, LVEF of 50% or more on a recorded echocardiogram, on a stable ACE inhibitor or ARB, no heart failure, no recorded compelling indication such as reduced ejection fraction, chronic kidney disease with albuminuria or diabetes with albuminuria; no upper age limit, and frailty recorded at baseline. Reason: the narrowed question is about the patients the earlier work left out. A stratum with LVEF of 40% to 49% is an option, because the pooled beta-blocker analysis found results consistent irrespective of background LVEF (PMID 42669300).
- Hypertension: patients whose only indication is blood pressure control are eligible only if the treating clinician agrees that the drug can be stopped without replacement, because otherwise reinitiation for blood pressure is built in. Reason: the adversarial review identified hypertension, diabetes and CKD as likely compelling indications that shrink the group and drive crossover.
- Time zero: randomisation at a routine review, with the intervention starting the same day. Reason: this aligns time zero with the treatment decision and avoids immortal time.
- Strategies: discontinue the ACE inhibitor or ARB (no replacement RAAS inhibitor) against continue the current agent and dose. Reinitiation is permitted for clinical need and is recorded as a protocol deviation.
- Primary clinical outcome: time to first occurrence of all-cause death, myocardial infarction, stroke or heart failure hospitalisation, from registry linkage. Reason: the hospitalisation-heavy composite criticised in PMID 42669300 is avoided, and the components are captured well by registries.
- Co-primary patient-reported outcome: change in a generic quality-of-life score and a treatment burden measure at 12 months, collected by electronic questionnaire, tested for superiority only after non-inferiority is shown (fixed-sequence gatekeeping). Reason: ABYSS found no apparent quality-of-life benefit from interruption (PMID 39213187), so this outcome may be null, and a hierarchical test avoids inflating the type I error. The instrument and its minimal important difference have to be set in the pilot, and no value is stated here.
- Safety outcomes: symptomatic hypotension, acute kidney injury and hospitalisation for hypertensive emergency, through registry and hospital linkage where available and questionnaire otherwise. The adversarial review noted that these are not captured well by MI registries, which weakens the pure registry-based advantage and requires supplementary collection.
- Estimand: intention-to-treat for the effect of the assigned strategy and a per-protocol estimand with inverse-probability-of-censoring weights for deviation, both reported. Reason: crossover biases an intention-to-treat non-inferiority comparison towards non-inferiority.
- Analysis: Cox proportional hazards model with the absolute risk difference at three years from Kaplan-Meier estimates, and the margin declared in the protocol before enrolment.

Sample size. Calculation is event-driven for a non-inferiority comparison of hazard ratios with 1:1 allocation, a true hazard ratio of 1.00, one-sided alpha 0.025 and 90% power: required events d = 4 x (1.960 + 1.282)^2 / (ln margin)^2.

- Margin 1.25 (the margin used for the primary endpoint in the pooled analysis PMID 42669300): ln 1.25 = 0.2231, d = 844 events.
- Margin 1.40 (the margin used in SMART-DECISION, PMID 41910427): ln 1.40 = 0.3365, d = 371 events.

Event-rate assumption: a three-year cumulative risk of 8%, taken from the composite of death, myocardial infarction or heart failure in PMID 41211954 (8.1% and 8.3% over a median 3.6 years in recent-MI beta-blocker trial populations). Patients who are event-free one year after MI may have a lower risk, so a sensitivity figure of 5% is also shown. Required participants: margin 1.25 needs 844 / 0.08 = 10,551 at 8% and 844 / 0.05 = 16,882 at 5%; margin 1.40 needs 371 / 0.08 = 4,641 at 8% and 371 / 0.05 = 7,425 at 5%. Add an allowance for loss to follow-up once the registry linkage rate is known. The 5% to 8% three-year range is an assumption and is not taken from a RAAS inhibitor population; the emulation step exists to replace it. The trial is large, which is consistent with the adversarial review's warning that a defensible margin needs several thousand participants.

The owner has no registry-based trial platform for post-MI drugs and no funding for this question.

Collaborative routes drawn from the store: the SWEDEHEART-based group behind REDUCE-AMI (PMID 38587241) and the SWEDEHEART secondary prevention cohort (PMID 41186482), the ABYSS group (PMID 39213187), and the SMART-DECISION group (PMID 41910427). Whether any would partner is not known. Planned research visits in Denmark, Germany and the USA are a route to the first conversations.

Feasibility, stated plainly: medium to low for the trial, which needs a partner registry, a funder and a sample of several thousand; medium for the emulation. Feasibility carries little weight in the ranking by design.

Overlap with the owner's active projects: none on topic. The only link is methodological, with the planned registry-embedded cardiogenic shock platform, and the same registry-embedding work would transfer.

## 8. Result scenarios

- Non-inferiority shown with a patient-reported benefit: supports stopping the drug in low-risk survivors including older patients, a deprescribing result with clear practice consequences, suited to a parent journal because it changes routine review practice.
- Non-inferiority shown without a patient-reported benefit: still informative for guideline committees, as with the beta-blocker work (PMID 41910427), and tells clinicians that stopping is safe but not necessarily better for the patient. The adversarial review judged this the scenario most likely to be sent to a specialty journal.
- Non-inferiority not shown: would be an important, unexpected result showing harm from withdrawal, and would be publishable in a parent journal on the strength of surprise, as with ABYSS (PMID 39213187).
- Inconclusive because of crossover or wide intervals: publishable only as a methods and feasibility paper, which is the main risk.

## 10. Risks, de-risking and scoop risk

- Equipoise erosion: observational work already points towards no benefit from continuing. De-risk with the older, frail and patient-reported angle and with the emulation step.
- Crossover and reinitiation: PMID 41186482 shows reinitiation is common. De-risk with a per-protocol estimand, clear reinitiation rules and registry capture of dispensing.
- Margin and size: a defensible margin implies thousands of participants. De-risk with a platform design that shares controls across drugs.
- Outcome capture: hypotension and burden are not in registries. De-risk with an electronic questionnaire embedded in routine follow-up.
- Journal fit: neutral results may go to a specialty journal. De-risk by building the older and frail stratum and the patient-reported co-primary into the protocol from the start.
- Scoop risk: moderate to low in the next 12 months, because no registered trial exists in the store, but registry groups that ran the beta-blocker trials could extend them to RAAS inhibitors without notice, and ISRCTN and ChiCTR were not searched.

Adversarial check summary (24 October 2026, report at register/briefs/Q-025_adversarial.md): verdict survives with a narrower angle. No randomised trial, review or registered ongoing trial of the comparison was found, so G3 holds. Observational analyses point towards equivalence, which reduces novelty and equipoise. Editor objections were low consequence per patient, the difficulty of a defensible margin, many patients having compelling indications, and parent-journal fit. Design objections were outcome capture beyond registries, crossover bias in a non-inferiority analysis, and an unspecified margin. The recommended narrowing, adopted here, is to include older and frail patients and patient-reported burden, possibly within a multi-drug deprescribing platform. The guideline position still needs separate verification, and the Swedish emulation needs to be added to the store.
