# Adversarial review of Q-010

Question: first-line inotrope and vasopressor strategy in SCAI stage C to D cardiogenic shock, tested in a registry-embedded multi-arm adaptive randomised trial (UK shock platform). Reviewed on 24 October 2026 from the local store, PubMed E-utilities and the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session. The PANDA trial has no NCT number in the retrieved record, and a ClinicalTrials.gov search did not return it, so none is given.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No target-journal or general-journal systematic review, meta-analysis, network meta-analysis or large analysis of first-line agent comparison was found for 2024 to 2026. The existing syntheses are in lower-tier journals but together cover the evidence base, and the comparison most likely to matter (noradrenaline against adrenaline) has no completed adequately powered trial.

- PMID 41737078, Cureus, 1 January 2026 (entrez 25 February 2026). Frequentist network meta-analysis of vasopressors and inotropes in AMI-related cardiogenic shock, 14 studies (N = 5,157; six RCTs, eight observational), searched to December 2025. Outcomes: all-cause mortality, arrhythmia, refractory shock, which are the outcomes in Q-010. It reported milrinone versus dobutamine in-hospital mortality OR 0.90 (95% CI 0.69-1.19) and adrenaline versus noradrenaline refractory shock OR 8.24 (95% CI 1.61-42.18). Overlap is high on outcomes and agents, but it is a low-tier journal, restricted to AMI, and mixes observational data.
- PMID 41377633, Circulation Reports, 10 December 2025. Meta-analysis of noradrenaline against other vasopressors, two RCTs (n = 337), very-low certainty, 28-day mortality about 110 fewer deaths per 1,000 (95% CI 217 fewer to 5 fewer). It confirms the evidence base is thin rather than answered.
- PMID 41978758, Global Cardiology Science & Practice, 28 February 2026. Dobutamine against milrinone, RCTs and observational studies, searched to February 2025. Dobutamine overall mortality OR 1.54 (95% CI 1.07-2.21), non-significant in RCT data (OR 1.30, 95% CI 0.73-2.32).
- PMID 36195825, Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia, 1 December 2022. Network meta-analysis of 17 RCTs in AMI shock; only two RCTs of vasopressors, so no pooled vasopressor analysis.
- PMID 34347952, New England Journal of Medicine, 2021. The milrinone versus dobutamine randomised trial (192 participants): primary composite 49% against 54% (RR 0.90, 95% CI 0.69-1.19), in-hospital death 37% against 43% (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.60-1.21). It was underpowered for mortality, which supports equipoise for the inotrope arms.
- PMID 39415431, European Heart Journal - Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, 8 February 2025. SWEDEHEART instrumental-variable analysis of 16,214 patients: inotropes versus none, adjusted HR 1.72 (95% CI 1.26-2.35) for 30-day mortality. This is a target-family but subspecialty-tier journal and asks inotrope versus no inotrope, not agent against agent.
- PMID 40900181, Clinical Research in Cardiology, 1 October 2025. Dutch registry propensity-matched milrinone versus dobutamine, 30-day mortality 46.5% against 41.9% (p = 0.362).
- PMID 40960311, Annals of the American Thoracic Society, 1 February 2026. Claims-database comparison of milrinone and dobutamine in cardiogenic shock with sepsis, 10,447 patients, in-hospital mortality 41.6% against 42.7%.
- PMID 42636314, Circulation, 24 August 2026 (clinical primer, review). A framework for vasoactive selection that states comparative effectiveness data are limited. Not an answer, but it shows a target journal has just published on the topic and an editor will see the question as acknowledged and open.
- PMIDs 42093634 (Circulation: Heart Failure, 7 May 2026, CCCTN, 3,282 admissions, 37 CICUs) and 41147380 (Journal of the American Heart Association, 28 October 2025, AHA registry, 6,847 admissions, 84 sites). Both document substantial practice variation in agent choice, which supports consequence (G1) and the feasibility of a registry-embedded design, but neither compares outcomes.

Overlap verdict: the observational and low-tier synthesis literature is dense, so a further observational or meta-analytic paper would be incremental. The randomised comparison of first-line strategies is not answered.

## Ongoing trials

Retrieved from the store snapshot and confirmed in part through the ClinicalTrials.gov API.

- PANDA (PMID 41679570, American Heart Journal, 1 July 2026, design paper): prehospital noradrenaline against adrenaline, 1:1, planned 1,155, primary 28-day all-cause mortality, recruitment from 28 February 2024, 450 recruited at publication, estimated 5-year recruitment. Direct overlap with the adrenaline-against-noradrenaline arms. Expected completion around 2029 by the design paper's stated timeline; no registry entry retrieved.
- ACTION-CS, NCT07562932: noradrenaline against dopamine, N = 512, recruiting, primary completion 31 May 2031, primary composite of 28-day death or progressive or refractory shock within 24 hours.
- CAPITAL DOREMI-2, NCT05267886: dobutamine or milrinone against placebo, N = 346, recruiting, primary completion December 2026.
- LevoHeartShock, NCT04020263: levosimendan against placebo on top of conventional inotropes, N = 610, recruiting, primary completion 3 February 2027, co-primary 30-day mortality.
- NORSHOCK, NCT05168462 (design paper PMID 42703766, EuroIntervention, 7 September 2026): lower against standard noradrenaline MAP target, N = 776, primary completion 1 April 2027, primary death or renal replacement therapy at 30 days. This tests dose strategy, not agent choice.
- LEVOECMO, NCT04728932, completed, reported as PMID 41324946 (JAMA, 6 January 2026): levosimendan to wean from ECMO, 205 randomised, 68.3% weaned in each arm (risk difference 0.0%, 95% CI -12.8% to 12.7%). Not a first-line comparison.
- OptimaCC, NCT01367743: adrenaline against noradrenaline, N = 58, completed 2016, too small to settle the question.

None of these is a multi-arm comparison of first-line strategies across SCAI C to D shock, and none is a UK platform trial. Two (PANDA and ACTION-CS) will answer the vasopressor-choice arms, and PANDA is likely to report within about three years.

## Editor objections

1. The question as a whole is not new to the literature: dobutamine against milrinone has been tested in an RCT (PMID 34347952), and noradrenaline against adrenaline and dopamine is in active RCTs. A target-journal editor would ask what a fourth multi-arm trial adds beyond PANDA and DOREMI-2.
2. A protocol or platform paper from a single national system would normally be directed to a specialty journal, and the registry-embedded design paper would be hard to place in a parent journal before results exist.
3. The population (cardiogenic shock of any cause) is heterogeneous, so a pooled first-line result may be uninterpretable. Mortality effects of agents plausibly differ between AMI-CS, HF-CS, post-cardiotomy and post-arrest shock, which was visible in the cited practice-variation analyses.
4. "Usual care in an observational reference arm" adds little, because confounding by indication in those comparisons (for instance PMID 40900181, where milrinone patients were sicker at baseline) is exactly what randomisation is meant to remove.
5. The novelty of the answer depends on the arms: noradrenaline against adrenaline is the strongest signal, and it is already being tested.

## Design objections

- Several arms and several agent classes are conflated. Dobutamine, milrinone and adrenaline-with-noradrenaline are not comparable first-line options in all phenotypes: adrenaline or noradrenaline is a vasopressor decision for hypotension, whereas dobutamine and milrinone are inodilator decisions for low output. A single multi-arm comparison would need a factorial or domain-based structure (vasopressor domain, inotrope domain), with separate randomisation per domain.
- Mortality at 30 days with modest expected effects, and arrhythmia and refractory shock as co-outcomes, would need a large sample. Using the DOREMI primary (RR 0.90) as a guide, the trial would need thousands of patients per domain; this calculation is not in the question and should be shown in the brief from stored inputs.
- Blinding is difficult for agents titrated to haemodynamic response, and open-label first-line prescribing invites crossover and co-intervention, notably early addition of a second agent (50.5% of AHA registry admissions received two or more agents, PMID 41147380).
- Randomisation at first vasoactive prescription needs prehospital or emergency-department enrolment, which PANDA is already doing for one comparison. Consent in an emergency and a registry-embedded model are credible in the UK, but the claim that the planned UK shock platform exists and will carry this arm could not be verified from the store or registry searches.
- Response-adaptive allocation can be credible, but it requires a pre-specified Bayesian decision rule and a stable outcome, and early arms may be dropped when PANDA reports.
- The registry-based reference arm does not need randomisation and should be replaced by a target trial emulation sensitivity analysis only if needed.

Overall, a registry-embedded randomised platform could credibly answer the question if it is restructured as domain-specific comparisons with phenotype stratification, and if it complements rather than duplicates PANDA.

## Verdict

Verdict: survives with a narrower angle.

Reason: the literature does not contain an answered comparison. The only target-family analysis (PMID 39415431) addresses inotrope versus none, the NEJM RCT (PMID 34347952) was underpowered for mortality, and the synthesis papers (PMIDs 41737078, 41377633, 41978758) are in low-tier journals and rely on observational data or two small RCTs. However, the adrenaline against noradrenaline and noradrenaline against dopamine comparisons are being tested by PANDA (planned 1,155) and ACTION-CS (N = 512, primary completion May 2031), and the placebo-controlled inotrope question by DOREMI-2 (N = 346, December 2026) and LevoHeartShock (N = 610, February 2027). The surviving angle is the inotrope-agent comparison within a phenotype (HF-CS and AMI-CS separately), or a platform domain that avoids the vasopressor arms already covered, preferably with a pre-specified prospective meta-analysis or a shared-control agreement with PANDA and DOREMI-2.

Suggested changes:
- G3: change from pass to conditional pass. PANDA is an adequately powered RCT of the noradrenaline-against-adrenaline comparison that could report within 24 to 36 months (planned 1,155, 450 recruited at publication), and DOREMI-2 reports in December 2026. Restrict the question to arms not covered by them, or record the gate as failed for the vasopressor arms.
- G5: keep as a pass only if the observational reference arm is dropped and the design is domain-structured. A multi-arm trial restricted to arms already under test would be incremental.
- Novelty: reduce by one point (suggest 3 if currently 4), because of PMIDs 41737078 and 39415431 and the ongoing trials.
- Equipoise: unchanged for dobutamine against milrinone (PMID 34347952 was underpowered) but lower for noradrenaline against adrenaline, where observational and network signals favour noradrenaline (PMID 41737078, 41377633). Suggest holding the score and noting the split by arm.
