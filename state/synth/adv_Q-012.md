# Adversarial review of Q-012

Reviewer worked from the PICO only. Every PMID and NCT below was retrieved and read in this session from the local store, PubMed esearch/esummary or the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. Numbers are as given in the stored abstracts or registry fields.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review, meta-analysis or large trial emulation of the full question (SGLT2 inhibitor, GLP-1 receptor agonist or renin-angiotensin inhibitor initiation after TAVI, with grace-period handling) was found in Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, European Heart Journal, JACC, NEJM, Lancet, JAMA or BMJ in 2024 to 2026. A PubMed search combining TAVI terms, the drug classes and "target trial" or "emulat*" returned only 42652758. A search of the TAVI and SGLT2 terms restricted to the eight target and general journals returned only 40162639 (and an unrelated Circulation item, 33196311). The question is therefore not scooped at the level of a target-journal observational analysis, but each drug class already has a crowded second-tier literature.

SGLT2 inhibitors:
- 40162639, New England Journal of Medicine, 10 April 2025: the DapaTAVI randomised trial (NCT04696185), 1222 patients in the primary analysis. Primary outcome (death or worsening heart failure at 1 year) 15.0% vs 20.1%, hazard ratio 0.72 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.95). Death alone: hazard ratio 0.87 (95% CI 0.59 to 1.28). Patients all had prior heart failure plus renal insufficiency, diabetes or LV systolic dysfunction. This is the benchmark the proposal intends to use.
- 41746781, Endocrinology, Diabetes & Metabolism, March 2026: systematic review of 3 studies (1 RCT, 2 observational), 3187 patients; composite hazard ratio 0.75 (95% CI 0.65 to 0.86).
- 42919410, Journal of Cardiothoracic Surgery, 28 July 2026: PROSPERO-registered review, 12 reports (1 RCT, 11 observational) after aortic valve intervention.
- 41398445 (Cardiology in Review, 16 December 2025), 42056230 (European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology, 30 April 2026), 42834681 (Cardiovascular Therapeutics, 2026) and 42433614 (International Journal of Cardiology Heart & Vasculature, 1 August 2026) are further meta-analyses or reviews of SGLT2 inhibitors after TAVI. 42426443 (European Journal of Clinical Pharmacology, 10 July 2026) is a critique of patient overlap between these meta-analyses.
- Observational cohorts, all in specialty or lower-tier journals: 41548478 (JACC Advances, February 2026, TriNetX, heart failure subgroup, 2464 treated of 21,828), 41096081, 41598653, 41969667, 42281846, 42355977, 42683897, 41232829, 41901616. These cover SGLT2 inhibitors only and mostly use propensity matching.
- 42655906 (European Journal of Heart Failure, 27 August 2026), 42885795 (JAHA, 16 October 2026) and 41062227 (JACC, 14 October 2025) are DapaTAVI secondary analyses (by ejection fraction, frailty and quality of life). The randomised result is being extended by the original investigators.
- 42652758 (Journal of Clinical Medicine, 17 August 2026) is labelled trial_emulation in the store but is a structured narrative review of SGLT2 inhibitors in valvular disease, not an emulation.
- Time-zero problems in this literature are already being aired: doi:10.1016/j.jscai.2026.105694 (JSCAI, 1 October 2026, "Aligning time zero when evaluating sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors after transcatheter aortic valve replacement") and its reply doi:10.1016/j.jscai.2026.105693, and 42119375 (JACC Advances, June 2026, methodological considerations in TriNetX research on SGLT2 inhibitors after TAVR). The abstracts are not stored for the first pair, so their content is known only from the titles.

GLP-1 receptor agonists:
- 42886864, European Journal of Heart Failure, 16 October 2026: Western Denmark nationwide cohort of 5259 eligible AVR patients (TAVI or SAVR), 292 matched; composite hazard ratio 0.69 (95% CI 0.50 to 0.96); all-cause mortality hazard ratio 0.66 (95% CI 0.46 to 0.95). Strong overlap with the GLP-1 arm, but small, propensity-matched, mixed TAVI and SAVR, and from a specialty-tier journal.
- 41206745, European Heart Journal Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes, 29 April 2026: TriNetX, 25,184 adults with type 2 diabetes after TAVI, 1101 per group after matching.
- 42019817, Canadian Journal of Cardiology, October 2026: TriNetX, 1708 per group, MACE hazard ratio 0.63 (95% CI given in the abstract as truncated in the store).
- 41765256 (American Journal of Cardiology, 1 May 2026, TriNetX, GLP-1 RA, SGLT2 inhibitor or both) and its critique 41806961 (American Journal of Cardiology, 15 May 2026, partial re-analysis).
- 42516860 (JSCAI, June 2026) and 42000295 (Cardiovascular Revascularization Medicine, August 2026) concern tirzepatide after TAVR.

Renin-angiotensin inhibitors:
- 30512100, JAMA, 4 December 2018: STS/ACC TVT Registry linked to Medicare, 15,896 matched patients; 1-year mortality 12.5% vs 14.9% (hazard ratio 0.82, 95% CI 0.76 to 0.90). Exposure was discharge prescription, which carries no grace-period handling and is vulnerable to immortal-time and healthy-user effects.
- 31370954, JACC, 6 August 2019: 10-institution cohort of 2,785 patients.
- 31711153, European Heart Journal, 21 February 2020: PARTNER 2 trial and registries, 3979 patients, baseline ACEI/ARB.
- Meta-analyses: 38939627 (JACC Advances, May 2024, ten studies), 41656974 (Catheterization and Cardiovascular Interventions, April 2026, Bayesian), 39878581 (American Journal of Therapeutics, January 2025), 42103561 (Cardiovascular Revascularization Medicine, May 2026), and several 2019 to 2022 reviews.
- 39291483, JAHA, October 2024: RASTAVI randomised trial (NCT03201185), 186 analysed (94 ramipril, 92 standard care); primary endpoint 10.6% vs 12% (P=0.776), heart failure readmissions 3.2% vs 10.9% (P=0.040). 42209209 (Heart, 28 May 2026): ChiCTR2100042266, 194 patients, LV mass index endpoint.

Overlap summary: SGLT2 inhibitors have an RCT in NEJM and a flood of meta-analyses, so an observational emulation adds little beyond the trial unless it targets a population the trial excluded. RAS inhibitors have a JAMA registry analysis and a small positive-signal RCT programme. GLP-1 receptor agonists are the least settled arm, with only TriNetX and one Danish cohort.

## Editor objections

1. Three drug classes in one paper dilutes the message. An editor at the target journals would ask which decision is being changed, and the answer differs by class (SGLT2: RCT already exists; GLP-1: no RCT yet; RAS: discharge prescribing practice).
2. The SGLT2 arm duplicates DapaTAVI. Using the trial as a benchmark is a methodological contribution, but a Circulation or JAMA Cardiology editor would class a benchmarked emulation of a trial already in NEJM as a methods paper, and a confirmatory one if it agrees. If it disagrees, the likely reading is residual confounding, not trial error.
3. Registry and claims data. TriNetX analyses of this exact question are already published and critiqued (41806961, 42119375, doi:10.1016/j.jscai.2026.105694). The incremental claim must rest on the identification strategy and data quality, not on the question.
4. Population mismatch. DapaTAVI enrolled only patients with a history of heart failure plus renal insufficiency, diabetes or LV dysfunction. A general post-TAVI emulation cannot be benchmarked against it without restricting to the same eligibility, which shrinks the cohort and reduces what is new.
5. Fashionable-drug confounding. GLP-1 receptor agonist and SGLT2 inhibitor use is concentrated in patients with diabetes and obesity, who differ in prognosis and healthcare access; observational hazard ratios near 0.65 to 0.70 (42886864, 42019817) are larger than plausible and invite the objection of healthy-user bias.
6. Novelty is low relative to the volume of second-tier output in 2026. Editors will check for it.

## Design objections

- The sequential emulation with clone-censor-weight grace periods is a credible way to handle initiation after discharge and addresses the time-zero criticism already raised in the literature. Ten-year-old analyses such as 30512100 did not do this.
- The data source is not specified in the PICO. The design needs a source with dispensing records, discharge dates, and linked outcomes (for example Nordic registers, SWEDEHEART-like TAVI registries linked to prescription data, or the US TVT Registry linked to Medicare Part D). TriNetX lacks reliable exposure dates and loss to follow-up, and cannot support the intended 3-year follow-up and kidney outcomes without severe bias. Without a named, linked source the design is not yet credible (gate G4 depends on this).
- Positive and negative control logic is sound in principle but weak in practice. One RCT estimate (DapaTAVI hazard ratio 0.72) is a single positive control for one class. For RAS inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists no randomised result exists to calibrate against, and RASTAVI (n = 186 analysed) is too small. Negative-control outcomes or exposures need to be named.
- The grace period of 90 days is long for patients at high early mortality risk and conflicts with DapaTAVI, which randomised near the procedure. Sensitivity analyses at 30 and 60 days are needed.
- Treatment-effect heterogeneity (diabetes, ejection fraction, eGFR) is where observational data could extend DapaTAVI, and the three-drug structure obscures that angle.
- Competing risk of death must be modelled for heart failure hospitalisation and kidney outcomes.
- Triple outcomes (death or HF hospitalisation, MACE, kidney) increase multiplicity without a stated hierarchy.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The question is not fully answered and no target or general journal has published an analysis with grace-period emulation, but it is partly overtaken for SGLT2 inhibitors, where a NEJM randomised trial (40162639), a second large outcomes trial (NCT05672836, primary completion 2028-12) and an expanding set of meta-analyses and TriNetX cohorts already exist. The strongest remaining angles are (a) GLP-1 receptor agonists after TAVI in patients with diabetes or obesity, where only matched cohorts exist and the earliest RCT (NCT07090343) completes in 2030; (b) renin-angiotensin inhibitors, where the evidence is a 2018 JAMA registry analysis without grace-period handling, small RCTs and an ARB trial not due until 2030; and (c) transportability of DapaTAVI to patients who were not eligible (no heart failure history, older, frail, advanced CKD). I suggest dropping SGLT2 inhibitors as a headline arm and using DapaTAVI only as a calibration control, naming a linked data source with dispensing dates, and prioritising GLP-1 and RAS arms or the transportability question. If the three-class structure is retained, the likely outcome is a methods paper in a specialty journal.

Suggested changes:
- G3: pass for the GLP-1 and RAS arms (earliest hard-endpoint RCT NCT07090343 and NCT07735130 report 2030); for the SGLT2 arm record that NCT05672836 (n = 1040) is due 2028-12 and that 40162639 already answers the core population, so the SGLT2 arm does not pass G3 as an independent question.
- G5: borderline. The question is not a repeat meta-analysis, but a TriNetX-style propensity analysis is incremental. Pass only if the identification strategy (grace-period cloning, linked dispensing data, named controls) is stated and the data source is named.
- Novelty: reduce by one point (from the current score) because of the volume of 2026 literature and published critiques on time zero.
- Equipoise: reduce by one point for the SGLT2 arm (DapaTAVI hazard ratio 0.72, 95% CI 0.55 to 0.95), unchanged for GLP-1 and RAS arms. If the question is narrowed, the equipoise score should be set by the GLP-1 and RAS arms.
