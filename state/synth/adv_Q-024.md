# Adversarial review: Q-024

Incretin-based therapy after myocardial infarction in people with obesity or diabetes: target trial emulation.

Searches run on 24 October 2026: local store (titles and abstracts, GLP-1 class terms with MI/ACS), PubMed E-utilities (three queries, 2024 to 2026), ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 (condition and term queries, plus record reads for six trials). Only records whose title and abstract or registry fields were read are listed.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review, meta-analysis or large analysis on this question was found in a target or general journal in 2024 to 2026. The PubMed query restricted to Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, Eur Heart J, JACC, BMJ, JAMA, Lancet and NEJM returned one hit (PMID 38418010, J Am Coll Cardiol, 2024, a Letter titled "Eligibility for and Preventive Potential of Semaglutide in Overweight and Obese Patients With Myocardial Infarction"; no abstract; it concerns eligibility and preventive potential, not outcomes). The BMJ cohort of tirzepatide (PMID 42556854, BMJ, 5 August 2026; 52,971 adults with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD, tirzepatide versus sitagliptin, US claims) is in a general journal but covers established ASCVD generally, not a post-MI population, and has no heart failure or bleeding focus in the abstract I read.

Observational analyses in the exact population already exist, in lower-tier journals:

- an out-of-store publication, Eur J Prev Cardiol, 2026. Nationwide Czech registry; 126,845 MI survivors, 28,206 with T2D; matched cohort 2,271 (401 GLP-1RA initiators within 12 months). GLP-1RA use was associated with lower MACE (HR 0.7, 95% CI 0.52-0.93), all-cause death (HR 0.61, 0.47-0.80) and cardiovascular death (HR 0.54, 0.36-0.80). Propensity matching, not a trial emulation. Closest overlap: same exposure window and outcomes, T2D only.
- PMID 41297711, Int J Cardiol, 15 February 2026. TriNetX, T2D after AMI, tirzepatide or semaglutide within 14 days, 5,338 per arm; recurrent coronary events HR 0.913 (0.750-1.111), all-cause mortality HR 0.484 (0.413-0.567), HF hospitalisation HR 0.578 (0.531-0.630). Short follow-up (about 11 months), immortal-time and confounding concerns obvious.
- PMID 42219874, Catheter Cardiovasc Interv, 1 August 2026. TriNetX, AMI with PCI, GLP-1RA (n=7,201) versus SGLT2i (n=4,252) within 14 days; GLP-1RA associated with lower 1-year acute heart failure (HR 0.415, 0.343-0.501), recurrent MI (HR 0.799, 0.710-0.899), MACE (HR 0.788, 0.706-0.881) and mortality (HR 0.700, 0.507-0.967). This is the sensitivity comparator in Q-024, already done in a weaker design.
- PMID 41862113, Am J Cardiol, 1 June 2026. TriNetX, tirzepatide within 14 days of AMI or ischaemic stroke in people without diabetes with BMI 27 or above; 833 per matched cohort. Covers the non-diabetic obesity arm.
- PMID 42103200, Am J Cardiol, 1 August 2026. Target trial emulation in TriNetX, ASCVD (including prior MI) and obesity without diabetes, new GLP-1RA initiation. An explicit emulation label in a related population, in a lower-tier journal.
- Other small series: PMID 42210124 (BMC Cardiovasc Disord, 2026, semaglutide in non-diabetic Chinese ACS), PMID 41979990 (Cardiology, 2026, semaglutide versus SGLT2i after ACS in diabetes), PMID 41703411 (J Cardiovasc Med, 2026), PMID 40187657 (Int J Cardiol, 2025, SGLT2i plus GLP-1RA versus SGLT2i in ACS).
- Related Danish and Taiwanese work: PMID 41578841 (Endocrinol Diabetes Metab, 2026, nationwide Danish new-user comparison of GLP-1RA, SGLT2i, DPP-4i in T2D, not MI-specific); PMID 41392024 (Br J Clin Pharmacol, 2026, target trial emulation of early SGLT2i in diabetes with acute MI, 36,103 patients, Taiwan); PMID 41580697 (Cardiovasc Diabetol, 2026, Western Denmark Heart Registry, progression to T2D after MI with overweight or obesity).
- Syntheses: PMID 42387250 (Am J Cardiovasc Drugs, September 2026) pooled seven mainly observational studies, 37,393 patients with T2D from MI-defined populations (9,556 on GLP-1RA); all-cause mortality HR 0.67 (0.49-0.90), MACE HR 0.69 (0.56-0.84), HF events HR 0.78 (0.62-0.98), with substantial heterogeneity and loss of significance under Hartung-Knapp adjustment. PMID 42591121 (Front Endocrinol, 2026) network meta-analysis of RCTs of SGLT2i versus GLP-1RA in T2D with prior MI. PMID 42479368 (Egypt Heart J, 2026) and PMID 42099143 (Curr Med Chem, 2026) meta-analyses of GLP-1RA or SGLT2i in AMI. PMID 40652242 (Cardiovasc Diabetol, 2025) meta-analysis of number needed to treat in MI and ASCVD. None is in a target or general journal.

Overlap: substantial for the question as stated in the diabetes subgroup; limited for the design quality, because every existing analysis uses propensity matching in aggregated networks or a registry without clone-censor-weight handling, and none reports major bleeding or a 3-year horizon with full national linkage.

## Ongoing trials

No randomised trial dedicated to incretin initiation after MI with hard clinical outcomes was found in the registry queries. Trials that include post-MI participants and will report within 24 months of today:

- NCT05556512 (SURMOUNT-MMO), tirzepatide in obesity, N 15,374, primary composite of all-cause death, MI, stroke, coronary revascularisation or HF events, primary completion 2027-10, active not recruiting. Eligibility includes established CVD, so a prior-MI subgroup is likely but is not a post-MI-initiation trial.
- NCT05669755 (REDEFINE 3), CagriSema, N 7,101, primary 3-point MACE, primary completion 2027-09-01. Prior MI is an inclusion criterion; MI within 60 days is an exclusion criterion, so early post-MI initiation is not tested.
- NCT04255433 (SURPASS-CVOT), tirzepatide versus dulaglutide in T2D, N 13,299, completed, primary completion 2025-06-12. NCT03914326 (SOUL), oral semaglutide, N 9,651, completed, primary completion 2024-08-23. NCT03574597 (SELECT), N 17,604, completed, primary completion 2023-06-21. These exclude MI within 60 days in the semaglutide trials, which leaves the early post-discharge window open.
- NCT06383390 (retatrutide cardiovascular and kidney outcomes in obesity), N 10,000, primary completion 2029-02. Not post-MI specific.
- Small mechanistic trials: NCT07295223 (GALACTUS, N 44, inflammatory markers, STEMI, primary completion 2026-08); NCT06557811 (SemaFatCard, N 88, status unknown); NCT07773272 (SLIM-3, N 1,215, CKM-guided therapy after ACS with PCI, MACCE at 2 years, primary completion 2029-03-31; a strategy trial not specific to incretins).

None of these is an adequately powered trial of the same comparison with a post-MI initiation window expected before 2028. The pooled prior-MI subgroups of SELECT, SOUL, SURPASS-CVOT and SURMOUNT-MMO could, however, answer the question for chronic post-MI patients by 2027 and would weaken the case for observational work in that window.

## Editor objections

1. The question is largely answered in direction. Randomised cardiovascular outcome trials already show benefit of GLP-1RA in people with prior MI, so an observational study that confirms benefit adds little; one that does not would be read as confounding.
2. Observational analyses in this exact population are already published (PMIDs [identifier withheld], 41297711, 42219874, 41862113), with concordant signals, so novelty rests only on method quality.
3. Mortality hazard ratios of 0.48 to 0.61 in the published observational work are implausibly large against trial effects, which will make reviewers suspect healthy-user and access bias in any new emulation, especially because incretin access after MI is rationed by cost and by clinician selection.
4. Major bleeding has no plausible mechanism and no signal, so its inclusion looks padded.
5. Registry and primary-care data rarely hold BMI, HbA1c and discharge-time contraindications reliably, so eligibility cannot be emulated as trial-like.
6. The population mixes people with obesity without diabetes and with diabetes, two groups for which the trial evidence and the comparator pharmacology differ.
7. Fit for a parent journal is weak unless the result changes a guideline recommendation or sizes a trial; as a descriptive emulation it fits a subspecialty or metabolic journal.

## Design objections

- Time zero and eligibility: "within 12 months of MI" with time zero at eligibility after discharge invites immortal-time bias unless grace periods and clone-censor-weight handle it; clone-censor-weight with a 12-month window needs a pre-specified grace period and well-modelled time-varying weights, and it inflates variance.
- Drug access confounding: GLP-1RA initiators after MI are selected by income, specialist contact and engagement with care, which linked registry data only partly capture. Negative-control outcomes and exposures are needed.
- Active comparator: SGLT2i is itself a post-MI therapy under study (the sensitivity analysis is already done in PMID 42219874). A better active comparator for diabetes is DPP-4i or sulfonylurea, but these have different baseline selection. For obesity without diabetes there is no natural active comparator.
- Obesity measurement: BMI is missing for many MI patients in national registries, and tirzepatide and semaglutide for weight loss are often privately dispensed and invisible to national prescription records in several countries.
- Power and event rates: with initiation rates reported as about 2% of MI survivors with T2D in the Czech registry (an out-of-store publication), the exposed group may be small in most national datasets before 2024, limiting precision for stroke and bleeding. Data from 2024 onward are needed, giving short follow-up for a 3-year horizon.
- Outcome and exposure mix: all-cause death, recurrent MI, stroke, HF hospitalisation and bleeding in one study dilutes the primary estimand. One primary outcome (for example MACE or HF hospitalisation) is needed.
- Could it credibly answer the question? It could answer a narrower question: effectiveness of early (first 12 months) initiation versus deferral in patients ineligible for or excluded from trials (MI within 60 days, older, CKD, women), and the timing question. It cannot settle efficacy, which randomised data already address.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

Reason: no dedicated randomised trial or target-journal synthesis answers early post-discharge incretin initiation, and no existing analysis uses clone-censor-weight methods with national linkage, but the broad question (does GLP-1RA therapy after MI improve outcomes) has concordant observational answers (PMIDs [identifier withheld], 41297711, 42219874, 41862113), trial evidence in prior-MI subgroups, and further outcome trials reporting in 2027 (NCT05556512, NCT05669755). The surviving angle is timing of initiation within 12 months of discharge (early versus deferred), for populations the trials excluded or underrepresented (MI within 60 days, older people, CKD, women, non-diabetic obesity), with HF hospitalisation or MACE as a single primary outcome and bleeding demoted to safety. Suggested changes: G3 stays a pass but should state that SURMOUNT-MMO and REDEFINE 3 prior-MI subgroups may report by late 2027 and that PMID 42387250 is not in a target or general journal; G5 should be marked as borderline pass, conditional on the narrower timing and excluded-population framing, since a plain exposed-versus-unexposed emulation would be incremental to an out-of-store publication. Novelty should fall by one point and equipoise by one point for the diabetes subgroup, remaining higher for early (under 60 days) initiation and for non-diabetic obesity after MI. Suggested name: timing-of-initiation target trial emulation; also consider a value-of-information or trial-design framing if the emulation shows large early-initiation effects.
