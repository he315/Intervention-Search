# Adversarial review of Q-025

Question reviewed: discontinuation versus continuation of ACE inhibitor or ARB at least one year after myocardial infarction with LVEF of 50% or more, no heart failure and no other compelling indication, as a registry-based randomised non-inferiority trial. Searches were run on 24 October 2026 against the local store, PubMed E-utilities and ClinicalTrials.gov API v2. WebSearch was not used. Every identifier below was retrieved in this session, and abstract content was read for the three records marked as read.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

No systematic review or meta-analysis of RAAS inhibitor continuation or withdrawal after MI with preserved ejection fraction was found in PubMed (searches combining RAAS terms, myocardial infarction, discontinuation or withdrawal, and review publication types, 2020 to 2026). The meta-analyses that did appear concern perioperative continuation, COVID-19, hypertension and CKD, and are not on the same population.

- PMID 40886074, European Heart Journal - Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, first available online 31 August 2025 (abstract read). "Long-term effectiveness of ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers in myocardial infarction with preserved left ventricular ejection fraction." A target trial emulation in Swedish healthcare registries of ACEi/ARB versus no ACEi/ARB in people under 75 years with MI and LVEF of 50% or more, 2010 to 2021, with 10,697 treated and 4,730 untreated. The estimated 5-year risk of the composite of death, MI or heart failure was 7.8% (95% CI 7.1%, 8.5%) with ACEi/ARB and 8.1% (7.0%, 9.3%) without; risk difference -0.3% (-1.6%, 1.0%). The authors conclude that treatment did not confer a benefit. Overlap is substantial on population and direction of effect. It compares initiation at discharge with no use, not withdrawal after one year, it excludes people aged 75 or over, and it is observational, which leaves the question of withdrawal safety formally open. This record was not in the local store at the time of the search and should be added with `add-records`. Its journal is a specialty-tier title, so it does not itself trigger the G3 target-journal meta-analysis condition.
- PMID 41661197, Coronary Artery Disease, 1 May 2026 (abstract read). Korea Acute Myocardial Infarction Registry-National Institutes of Health Registry, 5,017 STEMI patients after DES-PCI, with landmark analyses at 12 months. Mortality was lower with RASi to 12 months (2.1% vs 4.5%, HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.31 to 0.66), and from 12 to 36 months the associations were neutral. This is directly about the post-12-month window of Q-025, but it is observational, STEMI-only, uses mortality as the primary outcome and is in a specialty journal.
- PMID 41186482, European Heart Journal - Cardiovascular Pharmacotherapy, 2 February 2026 (abstract read). SWEDEHEART utilisation and discontinuation of secondary prevention drugs including RAAS inhibitors in 159,155 patients, with ongoing treatment at 91% to 92% at one year and 74% to 79% at 12 years. It describes practice and does not assess outcomes. It is useful as evidence of real-world variation and of reinitiation, which will contaminate a withdrawal arm.
- Local store: a title search of data/records for RAAS terms with discontinuation or continuation and MI found only an unrelated correction and a single-centre type 2 MI cohort (PMID 41820839, BMC Cardiovascular Disorders, 12 March 2026). Nothing in the store answers the question.

## Ongoing trials

None found that randomises continuation against withdrawal of ACEi/ARB after MI. ClinicalTrials.gov searches (condition myocardial infarction or coronary artery disease, intervention ACE inhibitor, ARB or renin-angiotensin, with discontinuation, withdrawal, deprescribing, continuation or cessation terms, randomised allocation, plus five free-text queries) returned no matching study. The nearest registrations are in other settings:

- NCT03374449, Impact of Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibitors Continuation on Outcome After Major Surgery, completed, N 2,222, primary completion 2023-12-24. Perioperative, not post-MI.
- NCT05271448, continuing or discontinuing ACE/ARBs in CKD undergoing coronary angiography, status unknown, N 600, primary completion 2024-06-01. Peri-procedural renal question.
- NCT07184918, continuing versus discontinuing ACE inhibitors and renal function after coronary angiography, completed, N 44.
- NCT03417388, Women's IschemiA TRial to Reduce Events In Non-ObstRuctive CAD, active not recruiting, N 2,476, primary completion 2026-09-14. A different population, and its intervention content was not read here.
- NCT04329195 (COVID-19 setting, terminated, N 44) and NCT04506372 (perioperative, terminated, N 342) are not relevant.

The beta-blocker withdrawal programme (PMID 38587241, N Engl J Med, 18 April 2024, in the store as Tier A; protocol PMID 36513329) shows the template but does not cover RAAS inhibitors. PMID 33203618 (Am Heart J, 1 January 2021) is a MINOCA trial protocol of beta-blocker and ACEi/ARB treatment, a different population and a start-versus-no-start comparison. Absence of a registered trial does not exclude unregistered or investigator-led work; the search could not be run in ISRCTN or ChiCTR.

## Editor objections

1. The equipoise argument is weaker than it looks. PMID 40886074 already finds no difference between ACEi/ARB and no ACEi/ARB at five years in this very population, so an editor will ask what a withdrawal trial adds beyond a confirmatory result that deprescribing is safe.
2. Low consequence per patient. The harms avoided by withdrawal (hypotension, renal events, burden) are modest and the drugs are cheap, so the decision is less consequential than beta-blocker withdrawal, which had a larger evidence base and pending guideline change. G1 is marginal on hard outcomes and rests on polypharmacy burden.
3. Non-inferiority margin. Event rates in this population are low (about 8% over five years in PMID 40886074), so a clinically defensible margin requires a very large sample, and a wide margin will be criticised as permitting harm.
4. Many such patients have hypertension, diabetes or CKD, which are compelling indications. The "no other compelling indication" restriction shrinks the eligible group and the generalisability, and registry data on blood pressure and albuminuria are incomplete.
5. Novelty for a parent journal. A neutral result from a deprescribing trial with a population of mostly low-risk patients is more likely to suit a specialty or pharmacotherapy journal, as the existing Swedish emulation did.

## Design objections

- Registry-based outcomes cover death, MI, stroke and heart failure hospitalisation well, but hypotension, renal events (when not hospitalised) and patient-reported burden are not captured by national MI registry linkage and would need supplementary data collection, which weakens the registry-based advantage.
- Open-label withdrawal introduces contamination: PMID 41186482 shows that discontinuation is common but reinitiation is also common, and clinicians may restart for blood pressure control. Crossover to continuation will bias a non-inferiority analysis towards non-inferiority unless a per-protocol estimand is also reported.
- The non-inferiority margin and sample size were not stated in the PICO and cannot be assessed; with a 3-year event rate of roughly 5% to 8% the trial would need several thousand participants per arm for any margin near an absolute 1 to 2 percentage points.
- Randomising at a routine visit one year after MI selects event-free survivors, which suits the question but means eligibility must be checked from registry data and a clinic note, since LVEF and absence of other indications are not reliably in registries.
- The design is credible in principle: a pragmatic registry-based randomised trial is the right weaker-than-placebo-controlled but guideline-citable route for a withdrawal question, and the SWEDEHEART platform has precedent. It would answer the question better than a further emulation.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The comparison itself is not answered by any randomised trial or synthesis, and no registered ongoing trial was found, so G3 holds on the evidence retrieved. However, a target trial emulation of the same population (PMID 40886074, 2025) reports no benefit from ACEi/ARB, and a Korean landmark analysis (PMID 41661197, 2026) reports neutral associations after 12 months, so the clinical signal points towards equivalence already and the editorial case rests on converting observational reassurance into randomised evidence on withdrawal. The narrower angle is a low-cost withdrawal trial that includes older patients (75 or over, excluded from the Swedish emulation), people with frailty and polypharmacy, and patient-reported burden as a co-primary outcome, possibly bundled with other post-MI drug withdrawal arms in a multi-drug deprescribing platform. Suggested changes: G3 stays as pass but the note should cite PMID 40886074 and PMID 41661197 as non-target-journal observational analyses that do not trigger the meta-analysis condition, and flag that PMID 40886074 is missing from the store; G5 should be recorded as pass with a caution, since a trial that merely confirms the Swedish emulation in a similar low-risk cohort would be incremental; reduce Novelty by about one point (to a 3 if it was 4) because of PMID 40886074; reduce Equipoise by about one point because the observational evidence is already concordant and guideline wording was not retrieved in this review, so the guideline position needs separate verification before the score is finalised. Subspecialty-journal risk should be treated as real (set `subspecialty_only` for review) unless the older, frail and patient-reported angle is adopted.
