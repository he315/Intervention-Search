# Adversarial review: Q-001

Reviewer sources: local store (titles and abstracts), PubMed E-utilities, and the trial snapshot at data/trials/current.jsonl.gz. ClinicalTrials.gov could not be reached through the proxy, so trial details are limited to snapshot fields (no primary outcome text). Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 32794407, Circulation, 2020. Bangalore and colleagues' meta-analysis of routine revascularisation versus initial medical therapy in stable ischaemic heart disease: 14 trials, 14,877 patients, weighted mean follow-up 4.5 years. Death RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.90-1.09); non-procedural MI RR 0.76 (0.67-0.85); procedural MI RR 2.48 (1.86-3.31); trial sequential analysis. It used aggregate risk ratios stratified by stent and statin use, with no time-varying or RMST modelling. It predates ISCHEMIA-EXTEND, REVIVED-BCIS2 and ORBITA-2, so it is partly outdated, but it covers the same decision and the same outcomes in a target journal. Its reception included critical letters and a European Heart Journal commentary (PMID 34110408, 2021) on the biases of that meta-analysis.
- PMID 37949356, Hellenic Journal of Cardiology (entrez 2023-11), 28 RCTs, 20,692 patients. Cardiovascular death RR 0.79 (95% CI 0.69-0.90); MI RR 0.74; benefit increased with follow-up duration (p = 0.001) by meta-regression. Not a target journal, but it addresses the time-varying question crudely and reaches a conclusion that conflicts with the 2020 Circulation paper.
- PMID 39166630, European Journal of Clinical Investigation (2024), 19 RCTs, 8,616 patients, PCI versus OMT. CV death OR 0.77 (95% CI 0.62-0.96); benefit larger beyond 3 years of follow-up (p = 0.04). Same limitations: aggregate data, meta-regression for time.
- PMID 36265647, International Journal of Cardiology (2023), network meta-analysis of 18 RCTs comparing angiography-guided PCI, physiology-guided PCI and CABG with medical therapy in CCS without left main disease or reduced LVEF. CABG alone reduced MI (IRR 0.68, 0.52-0.90), cardiovascular death (IRR 0.76, 0.64-0.89) and all-cause death (IRR 0.87, 0.77-0.99). This already occupies the NMA structure, with aggregate incidence rate ratios.
- PMID 37225285, JACC: Cardiovascular Interventions (2023), 18 trials, 16,908 patients, noncardiac mortality RR 1.09 (0.94-1.26), with meta-regression on follow-up duration. Addresses the ISCHEMIA-EXTEND mortality signal, not the cardiac outcomes.
- PMID 41343048, Clinical Research in Cardiology (2025). Reconstructed IPD from Kaplan-Meier curves of 29 RCTs, 51,275 medically treated chronic CAD patients, single-arm natural history of all-cause death and MI, with risks rising after 5 years. This shows the reconstruction method has been applied to the control-arm substrate; it does not compare revascularisation with medical therapy.
- PMID 41210668, International Journal of Angiology (2025), 10 trials, 14,171 patients; PMID 41130952, Critical Pathways in Cardiology (2026), 28 RCTs, MI OR 0.84 (0.74-0.96). Both low-tier aggregate syntheses reaching mixed conclusions.
- PMID 30596995, European Heart Journal (2019), IPD meta-analysis of FFR-guided PCI versus medical therapy in stable lesions; PMID 40831380, European Heart Journal (2025), IPD meta-analysis of FFR versus angiography to guide PCI. The first covers the FFR-guided PCI versus medical therapy arm of the proposal with true IPD, though it predates ISCHEMIA, ORBITA-2 and REVIVED. The second is a different comparison (guidance method).
- PMID 40048661, European Heart Journal (2025), IPD analysis of REVIVED-BCIS2 and STICHES (1,912 participants), medical-therapy arms and incremental benefit of CABG or PCI in ischaemic LV dysfunction. This is a true IPD analysis covering the LV-function stratum of the proposal, with long follow-up (median 118 months in STICHES).
- PMID 41949523, JACC (2026), pooled analysis of EUROCTO and DECISION-CTO (518 patients); cardiac death or MI similar between groups over mean 3.1 years. Covers a CTO subset only.
- PMID 42789945, Journal of Food and Drug Analysis (2026), single-centre Taiwanese retrospective PSM cohort; no target-journal observational, trial-emulation or biobank analysis of the same question was found in the store or on PubMed in my searches.

I found no target- or general-journal meta-analysis, IPD, network or reconstructed-IPD analysis published in 2024 to 2026 of revascularisation versus medical therapy in chronic coronary syndrome across PCI and CABG with time-varying effects. My PubMed searches combined title terms for revascularisation, PCI, CABG, medical therapy and synthesis designs with target and general journal names for 2022 to 2026, and the store search covered the same journals from 2022. These searches may miss papers with unusual title wording, and the store search relies on title matching. The nearest target-journal syntheses are the 2020 Circulation paper (PMID 32794407) and, for sub-questions, the 2025 European Heart Journal IPD papers above.

Not retrieved in this session: the ISCHEMIA-EXTEND long-term report. The NEJM search did not return it, so I do not cite it or its figures. The Circulation editorial (PMID 36335920, "If the Fates Allow: The Zero-Sum Game of ISCHEMIA-EXTEND") was retrieved but has no abstract, so I draw no inference from its content.

## Editor objections

1. Aggregate-data syntheses of this exact decision are plentiful. At least six comparisons in the store or on PubMed (PMIDs 32794407, 36265647, 37949356, 39166630, 41210668, 41130952) cover the same trials and outcomes, and two of them (37949356, 39166630) already report a follow-up-duration effect on cardiovascular death. An editor will ask what is new beyond reconstructing curves from the same trials.
2. Reconstructed IPD from published Kaplan-Meier curves cannot support effect-modification claims. Ischaemia burden, anatomy and LV function are mostly trial-level characteristics (ISCHEMIA enrolled moderate to severe ischaemia and excluded left main disease and low EF; REVIVED and STICH enrolled LVEF 35% or below; ORBITA trials are symptom trials). Subgroup curves are rarely published, so the modification would be ecological across trials and confounded by era, stent type, medical therapy and crossover.
3. Trial heterogeneity in outcome definitions (spontaneous MI versus peri-procedural MI definitions, cardiac versus cardiovascular death) limits what a pooled "spontaneous MI" outcome can mean; the published curves often report composite or all-MI outcomes.
4. The most credible new data on effect modification are held by the trialists. An IPD analysis of ISCHEMIA with REVIVED-BCIS2, STICHES and FAME 2 would be accepted over a reconstruction, as the 2025 European Heart Journal REVIVED/STICHES IPD paper (PMID 40048661) and the 2019 IPD paper (PMID 30596995) show.
5. Contestable results would split on trial selection (stable versus mixed populations, sham-controlled symptom trials versus event trials), which is already the substance of the published letters on PMID 32794407.

## Design objections

- Time-varying hazard ratios from reconstructed curves are feasible for all-cause death and MI, as PMID 41343048 shows, but not reliably for cardiac death or spontaneous MI, which many trials do not report as separate Kaplan-Meier curves. Reconstruction accuracy depends on numbers at risk, which the older trials often omit.
- A network over PCI, CABG, FFR-guided PCI and medical therapy mixes populations (CABG trials in multivessel disease with LV dysfunction or older eras, PCI trials with bare-metal and early DES). Transitivity is doubtful, and the 2023 network meta-analysis (PMID 36265647) already shows the large CABG versus angiography-guided PCI confounding by era.
- Restricted mean survival time is a reasonable alternative to hazard ratios when proportional hazards fail, and is a genuine methodological contribution; the design could answer a narrower question credibly: whether the benefit of revascularisation on MI and cardiac death emerges after 5 years in the trials with long follow-up, using only trials with extended follow-up (for example those with 5 to 10 years) and comparing PCI and CABG separately.
- Prespecified effect modification by ischaemia, anatomy and LV function would need to be reduced to between-trial meta-regression on a handful of trials, which has low power and an ecological bias; as written the design cannot credibly answer that part.
- A stronger option for the effect-modification question is a target trial emulation in a large registry or biobank, or a trial-collaborative IPD, which the owner could pursue as the alternative design.

## Verdict

Survives with a narrower angle.

The broad question is not scooped by a target-journal synthesis from 2024 to 2026, since the last target-journal aggregate synthesis of this decision I could verify is the 2020 Circulation paper (PMID 32794407), and no ongoing trial will settle the long-term question within 24 months. The question is, however, substantially occupied by lower-tier aggregate meta-analyses, one of which reports longer follow-up effects (PMIDs 37949356, 39166630), by a 2023 network meta-analysis (PMID 36265647), and by IPD analyses of the FFR-guided and LV-dysfunction strata (PMIDs 30596995, 40048661). The reconstructed-IPD method cannot support the stated effect-modification aims, so the proposal should be narrowed to the time-varying (RMST and flexible parametric) effect of revascularisation on MI, cardiac death and all-cause death in the long-follow-up trials, ideally by trial-level strata and with a pre-specified plan to add IPD from ISCHEMIA, REVIVED-BCIS2 and STICHES if the collaboration allows.

Suggested changes: G3 should remain a pass but be annotated that the 2020 Circulation meta-analysis (PMID 32794407) is over 24 months old while several lower-tier meta-analyses since 2023 overlap, so the pooled-evidence novelty test must state how much new follow-up and events the reconstruction adds. G5 should be recorded as at risk, not clean: the aggregate pooled comparison is close to a repeat meta-analysis of the same trials, and the case for G5 rests only on the time-varying method and on long-term follow-up data, so it should be flagged as conditional. Novelty should fall by one point (from the scorer's value, which I did not see). Equipoise should stay unchanged, because the published syntheses reach conflicting conclusions on cardiovascular death (PMIDs 32794407 against 37949356 and 39166630) and guideline positions were not examined in this review.
