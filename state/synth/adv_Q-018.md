# Adversarial review: Q-018 Value of information for early intervention in asymptomatic severe degenerative MR

Reviewer sources: the local store, PubMed E-utilities and the ClinicalTrials.gov API v2, all accessed on 24 October 2026. Every identifier below was retrieved and read in this session. The question was searched for in the store by title and abstract terms, and on PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov by condition and design terms. The search is not an exhaustive systematic search.

## Existing syntheses or analyses

- PMID 40799133, Circulation, 28 October 2025 (epub 13 August 2025): "Long-Term Outcomes of Early Surgery Versus Conventional Treatment for Asymptomatic Severe Mitral Regurgitation: A Propensity Analysis." Prospective single-registry cohort of 1063 asymptomatic patients with severe degenerative MR and preserved LV function, median follow-up 12 years. Cardiac mortality hazard ratio 0.17 (95% CI 0.07-0.40) and all-cause mortality hazard ratio 0.72 (95% CI 0.52-0.99) favoured early surgery. The registry entry NCT01703806 (completed, 1063 enrolled) matches the cohort size. This is the same population and comparator as Q-018, in a target journal, with a conclusion favouring early surgery. It supplies observational inputs for a model but does not make a decision model redundant, and it increases the sense that the observational question is being answered.
- PMID 42832200, JAMA Network Open, 1 October 2026: "Early Mitral Valve Surgery and Survival in Asymptomatic Severe Primary Mitral Regurgitation." Target trial emulation using a three-hospital Korean registry, 802 patients (344 early surgery, 458 watchful waiting), clone-censor-weight analysis. The abstract gives a 5-year restricted mean survival gain of 66 days (95% CI 19-129 days) and an all-cause mortality hazard ratio of 0.69. This removes the "no emulation exists" angle. It is not a target journal.
- PMID 41661461, Netherlands Heart Journal, 1 March 2026: Dutch AMR registry, 99 patients (71 active surveillance, 28 early surgery), median follow-up 5.1 years. Of the surveillance group, 51% went on to facilitated surgery after ESC guideline triggers. Small, but it gives real-world crossover rates that a model needs.
- PMID 21945713, Int J Cardiol, 10 May 2013 (epub 2011): "Selection for early surgery in asymptomatic mitral regurgitation: a Markov model." Marwick, Scuffham and Hunink. Markov model of early surgery versus watchful waiting with QALYs and costs from a US provider perspective. The abstract reports an incremental cost-effectiveness of $54,659 per QALY at 90% repair and a threshold repair rate of 84% for early surgery to be superior. This is a direct precedent for the proposed model, but it is old, deterministic in its headline result, and not a patient-level model with value-of-information analysis. The abstract does not mention expected value of information.
- PMID 39414660, Gen Thorac Cardiovasc Surg, July 2025 (epub 16 October 2024): meta-analysis of 39 observational studies with 9135 asymptomatic MR patients, pooling risk factors for adverse outcomes. It addresses risk stratification, not the intervention comparison.
- No systematic review, meta-analysis, IPD or network meta-analysis of early surgery versus watchful waiting was found in the store or on PubMed in the searches run, and no value-of-information analysis of this decision was found. This absence is a search result, not proof.
- Transcatheter repair for degenerative MR in asymptomatic patients has no randomised or observational evidence found in the searches. The intervention arm "surgical or transcatheter" therefore mixes a surgical evidence base with a device extrapolation.

## Ongoing trials

- NCT03389542, REVERSE-MR: "Early Mitral ValvE Repair Versus Watchful Waiting for Asymptomatic SEvere Degenerative Mitral Regurgitation." Recruiting, estimated enrolment 424, start 10 April 2018, primary completion 10 April 2033. Primary outcome is a composite of all-cause death and adverse cardiovascular events (heart failure, atrial fibrillation, infective endocarditis, cerebrovascular events) over 5 years. This is the only identified randomised comparison of the same strategies. The registered completion date of 2033 lies well beyond 24 months, so G3 is not triggered on trial grounds.
- NCT01708265, Dutch AMR randomised trial: terminated, 12 participants enrolled, primary completion February 2021. It will not answer the question and is evidence that randomised recruitment is difficult, which supports the premise of a modelling approach.
- NCT03975998, Dutch-AMR prospective cohort: recruiting, estimated 500, primary completion October 2026, primary outcome a composite including class I or IIa surgical indication. Its readout is imminent and will produce prospective crossover and event data.
- No trial of transcatheter edge-to-edge repair in asymptomatic degenerative MR was found among the registry results reviewed.

## Editor objections

- The central uncertainty is the effect of early surgery on hard outcomes. A model cannot reduce that uncertainty, only formalise it. Two observational analyses published in the past 13 months (PMID 40799133, PMID 42832200) both favour early surgery, and an editor will ask what a decision model adds over those results and over the ESC and ACC/AHA guideline positions already in the store.
- A model whose inputs are largely observational hazard ratios from highly selected high-volume centres with near-100% repair rates (PMID 40799133 reports 97% repair) will give results that are driven by those inputs. The earlier Markov model (PMID 21945713) already showed that the answer turns on repair rate, operative mortality and baseline risk. Repeating this with updated inputs looks incremental.
- Value-of-information analysis for a trial that is already enrolling (REVERSE-MR, NCT03389542, N 424) invites the objection that the trial exists and the analysis is retrospective to the decision to run it. The analysis could still be framed as a prospective update on whether an additional or larger trial is justified, but the editor will want that case made.
- The intervention arm "surgical or transcatheter" is ambiguous. There is no transcatheter evidence in this population, so the transcatheter part is speculation and would weaken the paper in a target journal.
- Modelling papers rarely reach Circulation, JAMA Cardiology, the European Heart Journal or JACC unless they settle a high-profile policy question or use unusually strong inputs. A health-economics journal or a subspecialty valve or surgery journal is the realistic home as it stands.

## Design objections

- A patient-level Markov microsimulation with probabilistic sensitivity analysis and expected value of sample information can be built and is credible for a value-of-information question, but only if its inputs are defensible. Inputs for the effect of early surgery on mortality and heart failure come from non-randomised data subject to immortal-time and selection bias (PMID 42832200 explicitly addresses immortal time with a grace period, PMID 40799133 uses propensity adjustment). The model would need to propagate that bias uncertainty into the probabilistic analysis, otherwise the expected value of perfect information is understated.
- Durability, reoperation and repair-failure inputs for repair in asymptomatic patients, and the guideline trigger crossover rate in watchful waiting (51% reached surgery in PMID 41661461), are the main structural drivers and are available only from small or single-centre data.
- A guideline committee would cite a decision model only as supporting context. G4 as written requires a design whose answer a committee would cite for the decision; a model alone is unlikely to meet that standard, whereas a combination with an IPD or reconstructed-IPD synthesis of observational cohorts and the REVERSE-MR design would be stronger.
- The model's clinical conclusion cannot be stronger than the evidence it uses, so a headline of "early intervention is cost-effective" would be a statement about assumptions.

## Verdict

survives with a narrower angle

The question is not scooped: no synthesis or value-of-information analysis of this decision was found, and the only dedicated Markov model (PMID 21945713) is from 2011 with a deterministic headline and no expected value of information. The only trial that will answer it (NCT03389542, N 424, primary completion April 2033) is far beyond the 24-month window, and the Dutch randomised trial (NCT01708265) stopped at 12 participants. However, two recent observational analyses (PMID 40799133 in Circulation, PMID 42832200 in JAMA Network Open) reduce novelty of the observational angle, a model's output is bounded by its non-randomised inputs, and the transcatheter component has no evidence base. The narrower angle is a surgical-repair question, framed explicitly as whether the value of information justifies completing or extending REVERSE-MR, with bias-adjusted observational inputs, repair-rate thresholds and the watchful-waiting crossover rate (51% in PMID 41661461) built in, and with the Dutch-AMR cohort readout (NCT03975998, due October 2026) used as a data input. The transcatheter arm should be dropped or treated as a scenario only. The likely journal is below the four target parents unless the analysis is combined with an IPD or reconstructed-IPD synthesis of the observational cohorts.

Suggested changes:

- G3: pass, with the note that REVERSE-MR completes in 2033 and no meta-analysis of this comparison was found in a target or general journal in the past 24 months; the two 2025 to 2026 observational analyses should be named as materially changing the evidence base.
- G5: borderline. It is not a repeat meta-analysis but it risks being incremental against PMID 21945713 unless the patient-level structure and value-of-information components are the stated advance. Record G5 as conditional on the narrower framing.
- G4: record as conditional, since a model alone is unlikely to be cited by a guideline committee.
- Novelty: reduce by one point (to 3 if currently 4), because of PMID 21945713, PMID 40799133 and PMID 42832200.
- Equipoise: leave unchanged or reduce by at most one point. The observational data lean towards early surgery, but there is no randomised answer and the guideline position remains a judgement call. Check the stored guideline wording before finalising.
